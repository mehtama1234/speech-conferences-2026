# Baseline first-principles source

The taxonomy now names a baseline paper instead of treating analyst prose as its
source:

**Gunnar Fant, “Sound, features, and perception,” STL-QPSR 8(2-3), 1967,
pp. 1–14.** [Official PDF](https://www.speech.kth.se/qpsr/1967/1967_8_2-3_001-014.pdf)

Fant gives a speech communication chain with three broad regions: what happens
inside the speaker, what happens in the transmission medium, and what happens in
the listener. Within those regions he separates intended meaning, sentence form,
production activity, acoustic production, the signal in the medium, auditory
analysis, phonetic identification, sentence structure, and the received message.
That chain is the baseline map. It is not a list of current machine-learning
topics.

## What the paper contributes to the atlas

- Speech research can intervene at different links in one communication chain.
  A method that changes the signal is not automatically solving the same problem
  as a method that changes a listener's interpretation.
- At the production end, source behavior and vocal-tract filtering explain why a
  measured waveform contains both energy generation and shape imposed by the
  tract.
- A message and its physical signal are related but not identical. The same
  intended distinction can have different acoustic realizations, and one acoustic
  segment can carry information about more than one linguistic unit.
- A useful representation should retain the information needed for its task while
  avoiding detail that adds cost without helping the decision. This creates a
  recurring tension between fidelity, compactness, robustness, and interpretability.
- Perception is not a sequence of isolated lookups. Context, memory, comparison,
  correction, and prediction constrain what a listener can infer from a changing
  signal.

## How derivation uses the source

The paper supplies the starting chain. The papers in the corpus determine where
that chain needs finer boundaries. A final subtheme must therefore show:

`Fant stage or distinction -> ordinary speech pressure -> failed simple shortcut ->
recurring paper move -> evaluation target -> boundary from neighboring moves`.

The source supports the starting account; it does not prove that any current
conference category is natural, complete, or scientifically correct.
