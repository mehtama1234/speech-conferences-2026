Simultaneous Masked and Unmasked Decoding with
Speculative Decoding Masking for Fast ASR without Accuracy Loss
Koji Okabe1, Hitoshi Yamamoto1
1Data Science Laboratories, NEC Corporation, Japan
k-okabe@nec.com, h-yamamo@nec.com
Abstract
In this paper, we introduce two methods, Simultaneous
Masked and Unmasked Decoding (SMUD) and speculative de-
coding masking, into Partially autoregressive (PAR) decoding.
These methods achieve the same recognition accuracy as Au-
toregressive (AR) decoding while maintaining higher compu-
tational efficiency than AR in Automatic Speech Recognition
(ASR). SMUD and speculative decoding masking can accu-
rately identify hypotheses where decoder score computation can
be omitted. By omitting these computations, they achieve faster
processing while obtaining the same search results as AR de-
coding.
In TED-LIUM2 evaluations, SMUD with speculative de-
coding masking achieved a WER of 7.3% and an RTF of 0.41,
as compared to AR’s WER of 7.3% and RTF of 0.59, showcas-
ing the method’s ability to maintain the same high accuracy as
AR while enhancing computational efficiency.
Index Terms: Automatic speech recognition (ASR), partially
autoregressive (PAR) decoding, speculative decoding, beam
search, CTC-decoder joint decoding
1. Introduction
In recent years, high-accuracy ASR using Transformer-based
encoder-decoder models which employ Autoregressive (AR)
decoding, has become feasible [1, 2, 3, 4, 5].
While AR
decoding is highly accurate, it suffers from the issue of ex-
tended processing times caused by iterative decoder processing.
As a consequence, Non-autoregressive (NAR) decoding meth-
ods [6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12] have been proposed to address this
issue. Although NAR methods do offer faster processing, they
have not yet achieved accuracy comparable to that of AR meth-
ods [13]. In many ASR applications, high recognition accuracy
is required, leading to the continued use of highly accurate AR
in many ASR engines, including Whisper [3]. Given this back-
ground, there is a demand for faster search methods than AR
that do not compromise its accuracy.
To bridge this gap, Partially Autoregressive (PAR) decod-
ing [14] has been proposed to integrate the benefits of AR’s
accuracy and NAR’s speed. This method generates preliminary
recognition results using CTC greedy search and employs AR
decoding only in regions with low CTC posterior probabilities.
While PAR decoding is faster than AR decoding and more ac-
curate than NAR decoding, it still suffers from poor accuracy as
compared to AR decoding.
In this paper, we have identified the primary causes for the
poor accuracy of PAR when compared to AR: early search ter-
mination, inability to use CTC prefix scores, and low mask
prediction accuracy. To resolve these issues and achieve fast
decoding while maintaining the accuracy of AR, we introduce
two methods into PAR: Simultaneous Masked and Unmasked
Decoding (SMUD) and speculative decoding masking.
SMUD has two key characteristics. The first is that while
searching for hypotheses within the masked region, it simul-
taneously calculates decoder scores for both the hypotheses
within the masked region and those considered to have exited
it to outside the masked region. By comparing these scores,
SMUD can accurately determine whether the search within the
mask can be terminated. The second characteristic is that, re-
gardless of whether a hypothesis is inside or outside the mask, it
always calculates CTC prefix scores, enabling highly accurate
CTC-decoder joint decoding. These characteristics help main-
tain the high recognition accuracy of AR. Furthermore, spec-
ulative decoding masking enhances mask prediction accuracy
by utilizing decoder outputs in addition to CTC greedy search
results for predicting the mask. Specifically, the entire prelimi-
nary recognition result from the CTC greedy search is fed into
the decoder at once, and positions in the input tokens are fur-
ther masked where the output tokens differ from them. This
approach continues to enable fast mask generation, as it pro-
cesses the entire recognition result in one batch. By combining
these methods, we achieve fast inference speeds without accu-
racy degradation as compared to AR decoding.
2. Preliminary
2.1. AR with CTC-decoder joint decoding
Autoregressive (AR) decoding predicts tokens one by one, us-
ing the previously predicted token sequence as decoder input to
predict the next token iteratively, as shown in Figure 1 (a). This
method involves using beam search to predict the next token for
the top B hypotheses, and it is widely recognized as one of the
most accurate search methods in ASR, including Whisper [3].
Further, [15, 16] have proposed a method that combines the
CTC prefix score with the neural network decoder score in AR
decoding. The CTC prefix score is the log value of the CTC pre-
fix probability, which is defined as the cumulative probability of
all token sequences that have the current hypothesis as their pre-
fix. By using both scores together, higher recognition accuracy
can be achieved, further enhancing the advantages of AR. For
example, OWSM [4, 5] utilizes this joint-decoding technique.
However, to obtain the recognition result of L tokens using
AR, it is necessary to iteratively input the intermediate recog-
nition results into the decoder L times, resulting in slower pro-
cessing. This slowdown occurs not only due to increased com-
putational complexity but also because each time an input is fed,
CPU cache misses occur, requiring the neural network’s weight
parameters to be read from memory.

2.2. Partially autoregressive decoding
Partially autoregressive (PAR) decoding [14], as illustrated in
Figure 1 (b), initially obtains a preliminary recognition result
(ˆygCTC) using fast CTC greedy search (gCTC) in an NAR man-
ner. Afterward, tokens with CTC probabilities lower than Pthres
are masked with mask tokens (#), resulting in the masked to-
ken sequence ˆymask, where only these masked tokens are re-
predicted using high accuracy AR decoding. By partially em-
ploying AR decoding, re-predicted segments maintain the high
recognition accuracy of AR decoding, without the large degra-
dation seen in NAR methods [13]. Moreover, computational
cost is reduced as compared to full AR decoding because the
computationally expensive iterative decoder processing is only
applied to the masked regions and skipped for the unmasked
regions.
However, despite these advantages, PAR has three issues
that lead to degraded accuracy compared to AR:
1. Early search termination: The termination criteria of beam
search in masked regions are based on the appearance of to-
kens following the mask (post-mask tokens). Beam search
may terminate early if the same tokens as the post-mask to-
kens appear within the mask regions. This issue will be dis-
cussed in detail later, on the basis of an example.
2. Inability to use CTC prefix scores: PAR cannot utilize CTC
prefix scores, which were used in AR to enhance recognition
accuracy, because CTC prefix scores cannot be computed
solely within the mask. They need to be calculated sequen-
tially from the beginning of the input, including regions out-
side the mask. In PAR, which limits its search to within the
mask, it is not possible to compute CTC prefix scores.
3. Low mask prediction accuracy:
Since CTC does not
achieve as high recognition accuracy as the decoder, the mask
prediction accuracy using CTC probabilities is insufficient.
If the incorrectly predicted tokens by gCTC are output with
a higher probability than Pthres and are not masked, these in-
correct tokens remain in ˆymask, leading to recognition errors.
To address the issue of early search termination, consider
an example shown in Figure 1 (c) where the correct transcrip-
tion, using character-based tokens, for a given utterance is s e
a
c u c u m b e r. Suppose that the tokens c u c are
masked due to their low CTC probabilities, and as a result, the
masked token sequence ˆymask becomes s e a
# # # u m b
e r. If we define a post-mask token as a token that immediately
follows the masked region, in this example, the post-mask token
would be u. Thus, the appearance of u would trigger the hy-
pothesis search termination criteria during beam search. When
performing beam search within the masked region, c might be
selected as the first token in the mask region, followed by u as
the next token. Since the post-mask token u appears, the search
terminates early, and the recognition result for the mask region,
which would correctly be c u c, instead becomes c. As a re-
sult, the overall recognition output is s e a
c u m b e r.
Thus, early search termination can lead to deletion errors.

4. Speculative decoding masking
We propose speculative decoding masking (SDmask), which
provides a more accurate mask prediction than using only
gCTC. Since gCTC has lower recognition accuracy, it often out-
puts incorrect tokens with high probability, resulting in incor-
rect unmasked tokens. Therefore, we also utilize a more accu-
rate decoder for mask prediction as well. However, to maintain
speed, we calculate the decoder scores using a NAR approach.
Below is a detailed explanation of the process. As shown
in Figure 3, speculative decoding masking involves inputting
the entire token sequence ˆygCTC obtained from gCTC into the
decoder at once, predicting the subsequent tokens across the
entire sequence, and obtaining ˆydecoder. Similar to speculative
decoding, a technique known for speeding up large language
models (LLM) inference [17], this method can predict the entire
sequence of L tokens in a single input pass, making it more
cache-efficient and keeping the masking process fast.

5. Experiments
5.1. Experimental setup
We used four E-Branchformer models [2, 18] that were used
in the experiments conducted in [14].
The E-Branchformer
models were pre-trained on the following datasets and are pub-
licly available on the HuggingFace hub: AISHELL-11, JSUT2,
LibriSpeech-100h (LS-100)3, TED-LIUM24. All models were
trained and evaluated using the ESPnet toolkit [19].
Common settings for AR decoding, PAR decoding, and
SMUD include a beam size of 5, and no external language
model, with inference conducted on a CPU (Xeon Gold 6230R).
The CTC weight was 0.3 for AR and SMUD, 0.0 for PAR. In
common with both PAR and SMUD, Pthres was set to 0.95, and
the maximum search length M was set to 5. Additionally, fur-
ther experiments were conducted by combining SMUD with
speculative decoding masking.
For this study, we used several datasets for evaluation,
similarly to what is described in [14]. Specifically, we used
LibriSpeech [20] and TED-LIUM2 [21] as English datasets,
JSUT [22] as a Japanese dataset, and AISHELL-1 [23] as a
Mandarin dataset.
We followed the evaluation metrics described in [14] as
well. We evaluated accuracy using the word error rate (WER)
or character error rate (CER) depending on the dataset. Specif-
ically, we used CER for the AISHELL-1 and JSUT evaluation
sets, and WER for English evaluation sets. The real-time factor
(RTF) was used to measure inference speed. For the RTF, we
took the mean of all evaluation sets.
5.2. Results
Experimental results for CER and WER are presented in Ta-
ble 1, and RTF values are shown in Table 2.
It can be observed that PAR achieves smaller RTFs than
does AR, but at the cost of increased recognition error due to the

On the other hand, SMUD employing CTC-decoder joint

masks further reduces accuracy degradation, achieving recog-

for instance, shows an RTF of 0.41, which is significantly faster
than AR’s 0.59.
From the above results, it can be concluded that combin-
ing SMUD with speculative decoding masking achieves slightly
lower speed than PAR but with improved recognition accuracy
over PAR. More importantly, it delivers recognition accuracy
comparable to AR while being faster than AR.
6. Conclusion
In this study, we have introduced Simultaneous Masked and
Unmasked Decoding (SMUD) into fast PAR decoding to main-
tain the high accuracy of AR decoding. SMUD enables precise
search termination and CTC-decoder joint decoding to address
the accuracy degradation in PAR while preserving its speed ad-
vantage over AR. Additionally, we have proposed the specula-
tive decoding masking to enhance mask prediction and further
address the accuracy degradation of PAR.
Our experimental results confirm the effectiveness of
SMUD and speculative decoding masking. Unlike PAR decod-
ing, which exhibits noticeable degradation in recognition ac-
curacy, SMUD maintains high recognition accuracy with sig-
nificantly better computational efficiency than AR. Specifically,
in TED-LIUM2 evaluations, SMUD with speculative decoding
masking achieved a WER of 7.3% and an RTF of 0.41, as com-
pared to AR’s WER of 7.3% and RTF of 0.59. This demon-
strates that SMUD, combined with speculative decoding mask-
ing, offers a faster method than traditional AR decoding with
nearly no degradation in accuracy.
