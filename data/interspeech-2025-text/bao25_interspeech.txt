Frequency-Domain Enhanced Extreme Bandwidth Extension Network with
ICCRN for Superior Speech Quality
Hongtao Bao, Xueliang Zhang∗
College of Computer Science, Inner Mongolia University, China

Abstract
Bandwidth extension is a critical task in speech processing
that enhances narrowband audio quality by reconstructing es-
sential high-frequency components, which are vital for natural
and intelligible speech. Recent advancements like EBEN (Ex-
treme Bandwidth Extension Network) achieve notable results
with lightweight generators and real-time operations. However,
down-sampling limitations degrade frequency-space informa-
tion, causing speech distortion as up-sampling struggles to re-
cover lost details. To address this, we propose replacing the
generator with an ICCRN in the frequency domain. Leverag-
ing cepstral-domain features and inplace convolutions, our ap-
proach mitigates information loss during down-sampling and
preserves global spectral structure. Evaluations on the French
LibriSpeech dataset show significant improvements, including
clearer spectrograms, fewer generator parameters, and lower
computational costs, advancing the state of the art in bandwidth
extension.
Index Terms: bandwidth extension, frequency-space informa-
tion, ICCRN, cepstral-domain features, inplace convolutions
1. Introduction
In recent years, deep learning techniques have been incorpo-
rated into bandwidth extension methods, significantly enhanc-
ing the audio quality and naturalness of bandwidth extension
systems. Time-domain bandwidth extension methods[1, 2, 3,
4, 5] utilize neural networks to map narrowband speech wave-
forms and directly generate or predict wideband speech wave-
forms. Frequency-domain bandwidth extension techniques[6,
7, 8, 9, 10] typically employ spectral features from the lower
bands (such as complex spectra, magnitude spectra, etc.) as
inputs for neural models, with the learning target generally be-
ing the clean speech spectrum in the high-frequency bands or
masks used to estimate the high-frequency parts (for example,
ideal ratio masks, complex ideal ratio masks, etc.).
The goal of bandwidth extension technology is to synthe-
size or estimate missing high-frequency components by using
low-frequency information, thereby improving the quality and
perceptual effect of speech or audio signals. This approach
is particularly suitable for telephone communications or other
scenarios where high-frequency loss occurs due to bandwidth
limitations. Through deep learning models, patterns related
to high-frequency content can be learned from low-frequency
data, and reasonable high-frequency reconstruction can be per-
formed based on this, making the restored audio sound more
complete and natural.

EBEN[3] leverages a multiband decomposition of the raw
captured speech to decrease the data time-domain dimensions,
and gives better control over the full-band signal. This multi-
band representation is fed to a U-Net-like model, which adopts
a combination of feature and adversarial losses to recover an
corrupted audio signal. This method achieves excellent perfor-
mance on the French LibriSpeech dataset[11] with a lightweight
generator and real-time compatible operation.
Despite its excellent performance, EBEN still has certain
limitations. Firstly, compared to frequency-domain process-
ing, time-domain methods may be limited by the constraints
of time series analysis, making it challenging to directly cap-
ture changes in the spectrum. In contrast, frequency-domain
approaches provide a clear representation of a signal’s spec-
tral structure, enabling more precise control over the bandwidth
extension process. By manipulating frequency components,
frequency-domain methods can achieve more flexible and ac-
curate bandwidth extension. Moreover, the downsampling op-
eration in EBEN might disrupt the spatial information of fre-
quencies, and the upsampling process cannot fully restore this
information, which could lead to a deeper level of speech dis-
tortion.
This study further improves EBEN to enhance bandwidth
extension performance. Firstly, the method is modified to per-
form bandwidth extension in the frequency domain, allowing
for more precise control over the extension process. The multi-
band decomposition of the raw captured speech is retained,
but with a difference: the decomposed bands are transformed
into the frequency domain via Short-Time Fourier Transform
(STFT) for frequency-domain operations. Secondly, consid-
ering that the original generator’s downsampling might cause
a deeper level of speech distortion, the generator is replaced
with ICCRN[12] (Inplace Cepstral Convolutional Recurrent
Network), which effectively utilizes cepstral domain features
and inplace convolution to address the issues of downsam-
pling while preserving global information. Experimental results
demonstrate that the improved EBEN model not only has fewer
parameters and lower computational requirements compared to
the original EBEN but also exhibits superior performance.

processing. This technique was initially proposed in[13]. The

compose a signal into multiple non-overlapping subband chan-
nels with equal bandwidth, and synthesis filters{Gi}i∈[1,M ] for
subsequent signal reconstruction. These filters are derived from

In subband coding, PQMF analysis filter outputs are down-
sampled by a factor of M. This accelerates inference and allows
different frequency bands to be modeled as conditionally in-
dependent. PQMF analysis thus reduces channel redundancy
and computational complexity. In our experiments, we used
EBEN’s efficient PQMF implementation with native PyTorch
functions, relying on strided convolutions for high performance
and compatibility.
2.2. ICCRN Model
Figure 3 depicts ICCRN’s architecture. As an inplace model,
it eliminates frequency downsampling or upsampling, reduc-
ing information loss and avoiding the introduction of irrele-
vant data. The frequency dimension stays fixed at f=161, with
all convolutional layers having c=20 output channels. ICCRN
uses cepstrum analysis to examine time-frequency (T-F) domain
features. A real-valued FFT is applied to each channel’s T-
F feature map per frame. In the resulting cepstral space, the
spectral envelope energy, containing most semantic informa-
tion and timbre cues, concentrates in the low cepstral region,
while harmonics appear as sparse peaks distributed periodically
across higher cepstral bands[14]. By analyzing harmonic struc-
tures, ICCRN predicts and synthesizes high-frequency compo-
nents beyond the original bandwidth, achieving high-quality
extension that maintains natural audio characteristics. This
harmonic-based method improves audio quality and preserves
timbral fidelity, ensuring accurate signal representation.
We utilize a bidirectional LSTM operating channel-wise
(F-chBLSTM) to transform the input channel dimension into
a higher-dimensional representation. Once this transformation
is complete, the resulting features are processed by n sequential
CFBs. Next, a two-layer channel-wise LSTM (T-chLSTMx2)
creates a mask for the encoder’s output, with its hidden layer
size adjusted to 2c. During decoding, the masked feature map,
enriched by skip connections, is processed through n stacked
CFBs and further refined by a time dimension channel-wise
LSTM. The channel dimension is then expanded to 2×m chan-
nels using a 1×1 convolutional layer, where m corresponds to
the number of bands produced by the PQMF banks decompo-
sition. This channel arrangement models the concatenation of
complex spectra across different frequency bands, obtaining the
predicted high-frequency components, with the factor of 2 rep-
resenting the real and imaginary parts of the estimated STFT
spectrum. Ultimately, by combining the low-frequency signal
from the input and the predicted high-frequency components in
the time-frequency domain, we synthesize an extended band-
width audio signal. This process effectively enhances the fre-
quency range of the original narrowband signal by generating
accurate high-frequency components that complement the ex-
isting lower frequencies, thereby significantly improving audio
quality.
2.3. Details of the method
First, the 16kHz sampled speech signal is decomposed into
M equal-bandwidth, non-overlapping sub-bands in the time
domain using PQMF banks. Among these, the lowest-
frequency first sub-band undergoes a Short-Time Fourier Trans-
form (STFT), converting it into a dual-channel complex spec-
trum, which is then fed into the generator ICCRN. During the
encoder-decoder process, ICCRN progressively generates mid-
to-high frequency information, ultimately outputting an eight-
channel complex spectrum. Each pair of these eight chan-
nels represents the real and imaginary parts of the complex
spectra for one of the four sub-bands. By processing only
the low-frequency information contained in the first sub-band,
this method significantly reduces information redundancy and
greatly decreases computational complexity. After network pro-
cessing, the complex spectra of the four sub-bands are subse-
quently transformed back to the time domain via inverse Short-
Time Fourier Transform (iSTFT), thereby recovering the four
time-domain sub-bands. Finally, PQMF banks is used once
again to reconstruct the original audio signal from these four
sub-bands, restoring its full spectral range. The discriminator
design draws inspiration from the architecture of the discrimi-
nators in EBEN. It employs multiple discriminators to directly
process the sub-band signals decomposed by PQMF banks,
without the need to recombine or upsample these sub-band sig-
nals. Unlike EBEN, we input not only the generated high-
frequency bands but also the original low-frequency band. This
approach allows the discriminator to obtain more contextual in-
formation, aiding it in more accurately evaluating whether the
generated high-frequency bands are reasonable and natural. To
accommodate this multi-band input, we have adjusted the pa-
rameters of each layer in the discriminator accordingly. Addi-
tionally, to ensure consistency across different frequency bands,
we use a full scale MelGAN discriminator identical to the one
in EBEN. This further validates the quality and overall consis-
tency of the generated audio.

3. Experimental Setup
3.1. Datasets
We evaluated our model on the French LibriSpeech dataset[11],
using the same processing and degradation settings as EBEN.
Specifically, the degradation uses a 2nd order low-pass filter
(600 Hz cutoff, unitary Q-factor) applied viafiltfilt to ensure
zero phase shift, and adds Gaussian white noise 23 dB below
the signal power.
3.2. Configuration
for STFT, we adopt 320/80 for window-length/hop-length, and
the analysis uses a Hamming window. We use 320-point Fast
Fourier transform (FFT) to extract 161-dimensional complex
spectra for 16 kHz sampling rate. The model is optimized by
Adam. The learning rate is 0.0003. The batch size is 16. The
parameter M for the PQMF banks is set to 4. The genera-
tor’s network architecture aligns with that of ICCRN, utiliz-
ing 20 channels and employing a discriminator similar to the
one used in EBEN. To verify the effectiveness of the proposed
method, we selected EBEN as a baseline model and conducted
comparisons using the same evaluation protocol as EBEN. Ad-
ditionally, we compared our approach with other methods, in-
cluding Audio U-net[16], Hifi-GAN v3[17], Seanet[18], Strm-
Seanet[5].

5. Conclusions
This paper presents an improved bandwidth extension network
to address the potential loss of frequency-space information and
increased speech distortion during the downsampling process
in the original EBEN model. To this end, we replaced the
original generator with a new one called ICCRN (Inplace Cep-
stral Convolutional Recurrent Network) and adjusted the pa-
rameters of each layer in the discriminator. The ICCRN effec-
tively mitigates downsampling issues by utilizing cepstral do-
main features and inplace convolution techniques, preserving
global information integrity. Additionally, our method retains
the PQMF method for efficient conversion of subband speech
signals into the frequency domain. Experimental results show
that our method outperforms the original EBEN model in objec-
tive metrics and either surpasses or performs comparably in sub-
jective evaluations. Notably, our method generates finer mid-to-
high frequency details, resulting in superior audio quality.
