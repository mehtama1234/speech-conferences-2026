A-SMiLE: Affective Sparse Mixture-of-Experts Adapter with Multi-Task
Learning for Spoken Dialogue Models

Abstract
Large language models have made remarkable progress in gen-
erating coherent and contextually relevant dialogue. How-
ever, they still struggle to capture fine-grained paralinguistic
cues, limiting emotional coherence and contextual appropri-
ateness. To address this challenge, we propose an Affective
Sparse Mixture-of-experts adapter with multi-taskLearning (A-
SMiLE) to enhance the spoken dialogue model’s capability for
affective perception across multiple dimensions. Our approach
integrates Valence, Arousal, and Dominance (V AD) modeling
with response generation, thereby ensuring expressive and con-
textually aware interactions. Evaluations on DailyTalk and
a hard-case benchmark demonstrate significant improvements
over baselines in both emotion prediction and response genera-
tion. It shows the potential of cognitive-inspired affective mod-
eling to enhance intelligent speech interactions on the spoken
dialogue models.
Index Terms: Spoken Dialogue, Large Language Model, Rep-

of-Experts
1. Introduction
Large language models (LLMs) have revolutionized natural
language processing, demonstrating remarkable capabilities in
complex reasoning and dialogue generation [1]. However, spo-
ken dialogue inherently conveys richer multimodal signals, inte-
grating linguistic content with paralinguistic cues that critically
shape communicative meaning [2].
Early LLM-based spoken dialogue systems [3] primar-
ily process speech through textual representations, overlook-
ing critical paralinguistic cues such as emotion, speaking style,
and sentiment, leading to monotonous, unempathetic responses
[4]. Despite recent efforts [2, 4, 5, 6] to integrate paralin-
guistics into LLM-based dialogue systems, challenges remain
due to the complexity of human emotions and inherent con-
straints in data and models. Psychological research suggests
that humans can experience approximately 34,000 distinct emo-
tional states [7], yet existing approaches predominantly rely on
predefined categorical labels or emotion representations from
pretrained speech encoders. These methods are inherently re-
stricted by the limited diversity of available emotional speech
corpora, often leading to models that fail to capture fine-grained
affective nuances, including subtle emotional shifts, sarcasm,
and complex emotional states such as depression. As a re-
sult, these limitations can cause misalignment between model
responses and conversational context, reducing the overall co-
herence and emotional appropriateness of generated dialogue.
Furthermore, speech adapters, lightweight trainable networks
that map speech representations into LLM-compatible embed-

erating responses that adapt to the speaker’s emotional state,

LLMs [9]. However, most existing adapters for spoken dialogue
systems are primarily optimized for text-centric tasks, leading
to task-specific overfitting [10, 11, 12] by prioritizing lexical
alignment while disregarding essential paralinguistic cues. This
bias limits the model’s ability to adapt to nuanced paralinguistic
variations.
To bridge these gaps, we propose a novel framework that in-
tegrates multidimensional emotion perception into representa-
tion learning for LLM-based spoken dialogue systems, aligning
with human cognitive and affective processing. Our approach
is grounded in Russell’s circumplex model of affect [13], which
characterizes emotions along Valence, Arousal, and Dominance
(V AD), and the theory of affective neuroscience [14], which
suggests that the brain exhibits functional specialization in pro-
cessing different dimensions of emotional stimuli. By lever-
aging these principles, our model dynamically structures latent
emotion representations to enable human-like affective percep-
tion, ensuring both emotional coherence and adaptability in re-
sponse generation.
To achieve this, we employ a Cognitive-Inspired Affective
Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (S-MoE) [15] adapter architecture
combined with a multi-task learning approach, jointly model-
ing emotion representation learning and dialogue generation.
Our proposed S-MoE architecture enables fine-grained affective
control in spoken dialogue by dynamically activating special-
ized experts for different emotional dimensions (e.g., valence,
arousal), ensuring responses that are both emotionally and con-
textually coherent. Specifically, our contributions are summa-
rized as follows:
• Drawing on cognitive theories of affect processing, we lever-
age S-MoE to dynamically structure representation learning,
enabling human-like affective perception and enhancing the
model’s ability to capture fine-grained emotional nuances.

2. Proposed Method
2.1. Overall Architecture
As depicted in Figure 2, our framework integrates a speech
encoder with an S-MoE adapter to enable human-like affec-
tive representation learning. The speech encoder processes in-
put speech, and the resulting representations are refined by the
MoE-based adapter, which dynamically selects specialized ex-
perts based on affective cues. This enhances the model’s abil-
ity to understand nuanced emotions and generate adaptive re-
sponses. The transformed features are subsequently aligned
with the input space of an LLM. Within a multi-task learning
paradigm—comprising V AD prediction and response genera-
tion—the LLM jointly optimizes emotion modeling and dia-
logue response generation, ensuring that responses are both se-
mantically appropriate and emotionally attuned.
2.2. Cognitive-Inspired Affective S-MoE Adapter
Distinct brain regions process different affective dimensions:
Valence is primarily regulated by the amygdala and prefrontal
cortex [19], Arousal is modulated by the ventromedial pre-
frontal cortex (vmPFC) and limbic structures [20], and Dom-
inance is associated with the anterior insula [21]. The brain dy-
namically engages specialized neural circuits to regulate emo-
tional experiences, supporting the idea that affective processing
is distributed across functionally distinct neural regions.
Inspired by this cognitive principle, we propose an Af-
fective S-MoE Adapter to enable dynamic and efficient emo-
tion representation learning within a multi-task learning frame-
work. The architecture leverages task-aware expert activation to
learn specialized representations for valence, arousal, and dom-
inance, while integrating these dimensions to generate emotion-
ally coherent responses.

2.3. Two-Stage Multi-Task Learning Framework
2.3.1. Stage 1: Alignment
In the first stage, training focuses on aligning the speech em-
bedding with the LLM’s input space. To achieve this, the LLM
remains frozen while only the speech adapter ϕ is optimized.
During this phase, Cross-Entropy (CE) loss is applied for this
speech adapter optimization.
2.3.2. Stage 2: EAML
Once the LLM successfully processes speech embeddings, the
second stage fine-tunes both the LoRA [22] adapter θ and the
speech adapter ϕ, enabling emotionally adaptive learning for
spoken dialogue.

3. Experiments
3.1. Dataset
This study adopts a two-stage training approach with distinct
datasets. Stage 1 trains the model on a 10,000-hour corpus com-
prising GigaSpeech [23], CommonV oice [24], and LibriSpeech
[25] to align speech embeddings with the LLM’s input space.
Stage 2 fine-tunes the model on DailyTalk, a multi-turn dia-
logue dataset. DailyTalk consists of 2,541 scripted dialogues
between two speakers (one male, one female), totaling 20 hours
of speech. In this dataset, we extract V AD labels using a pre-
trained dimensional speech emotion recognition model [26].
For evaluation, we use two different test sets to assess the
performance of both our model and baseline systems in spoken
dialogue tasks. One is the DailyTalk test set, including 300 Dai-
lyTalk dialogues. Another is a hard-case emotional dataset com-
prising two challenging categories: (1) Low-Valence, Low-
Dominance Utterances , which often correspond to emotion-
ally complex states such as depression, selected from IEMO-
CAP [27], and (2) Sarcasm, where meaning is highly context-
dependent, making conventional models struggle due to their
reliance on lexical cues, sourced from MUStARD [28]. The
dataset consists of a total of 0.8 hours of speech.
3.2. Evaluation Metrics
To assess the model’s ability to comprehend both semantic con-
tent and paralinguistic emotional cues in speech while generat-
ing high-quality, empathetic responses, we employ a modified
system prompt designed to emphasize empathy in dialogue gen-
eration. Evaluation is conducted using the GPT-4o-2024-11-
20 model, which independently scores responses from different
systems based on empathy on a 0-10 scale.
For response text generation, we use ROUGE-L [29] and
METEOR [30] to measure content similarity with ground-truth
responses. To evaluate V AD prediction, we adopt the Con-
cordance Correlation Coefficient (CCC) score [31] as a perfor-
mance metric.
3.3. Implementation Details
Our framework integrates Qwen2.5 (0.5B/7B) as the language
model with a HuBERT Large speech encoder. Context process-
ing retains a 4-turn history window. Optimization is performed
using the Adam optimizer with a batch size of 64 and learning
rates of 1e-3 for Stage 1 and 1e-4 for Stage 2. We apply LoRA
parameterization (r=8) exclusively to the LLM components to
enable efficient adaptation [32].
3.4. Baseline Approaches
We evaluate our approach against two baseline configurations
using Qwen2.5 0.5B and 7B LLM as the backbone, namely
Text-LLM (Text-Only) and Text-LLM (Cascaded).
Text-LLM (Text-Only) involves fine-tuning a text-based
LLM on DailyTalk without incorporating paralinguistic infor-
mation. It serves as a reference to assess the model’s perfor-
mance when relying solely on lexical content without access to
prosodic or emotional cues. Text-LLM (Cascaded) incorporates
V AD labels as additional textual input for response generation
to enable the model to predict response styles and text better.
3.5. Evaluation on hard-case emotional dataset
Table 1 presents the results for V AD predictionand response
generation. Our proposed framework significantly outperforms
existing paralinguistics-enhanced LLMs across all emotion di-
mensions, achieving CCC V = 0 .48, CCC A = 0 .39, and
CCCD = 0.43, demonstrating enhanced sensitivity to subtle
emotional nuances.
For response generation, our model attains superior per-
formance, surpassing Qwen-Audio by ∆CCCV = 0 .06,
∆CCCA = 0.05, and ∆CCCD = 0.15. These improvements
highlight the effectiveness of our framework in enhancing af-
fective alignment and response expressiveness.

3.8. Ablation Study
To evaluate the contributions of EAML and Cognitive-Inspired
Affective S-MoE, we conduct an ablation study across mul-
tiple speech adapter architectures, including the Conv-Based
Adapter, Q-Former, and Transformer encoders with 4-layer and
6-layer configurations. The objective is to analyze the indepen-
dent and joint effects of S-MoE and EAML on emotion percep-
tion and response generation.
As shown in Table 4, removing either S-MoE or EAML re-
sults in a notable performance drop, underscoring their individ-
ual contributions. Without S-MoE,the model exhibits limited
affective sensitivity, leading to weaker response coherence and
reduced emotional expressiveness. Similarly, removing EAML
diminishes the model’s ability to adaptively leverage emotional
cues, making its responses less empathetic. In contrast, incor-
porating both S-MoE and EAML achieves the highest perfor-
mance across emotion classification accuracy, response coher-
ence, and stylistic alignment, demonstrating their complemen-
tary roles. The results confirm that expert routing and multi-
task learning jointly enhance spoken dialogue modeling by ef-
fectively capturing and utilizing paralinguistic information.
These findings highlight the significance of integrating
cognitively guided expert activation with emotionally adaptive
training to improve expressiveness and contextual alignment in
spoken dialogue systems.
4. Conclusion
Inspired by the compartmentalized affective processing of the
human brain, we introduce an emotion-enhanced spoken dia-
logue model, A-SMiLE, which applies an affective sparse MoE
adapter. The proposed approach integrates emotionally adaptive
multi-task learning and a cognitive-guided S-MoE for LLMs.
Our approach facilitates fine-grained emotion perception and
adaptive response generation by dynamically selecting spe-
cialized experts and optimizing emotion-aware representations.
This advancement emphasizes the potential of leveraging cogni-
tive principles to improve the emotional intelligence of spoken
dialogue systems, paving the way for more nuanced and contex-
tually aware speech interactions. Evaluations on DailyTalk and
a hard-case emotional dataset demonstrate that our model sur-
passes baselines in capturing subtle affective nuances and pro-
ducing contextually appropriate, empathetic responses. These
observations underscore the critical role of cognitive-guided af-
fective modeling in spoken dialogue systems and its transforma-
tive potential to redefine human-machine interactions, bridging
the gap between artificial intelligence and emotionally intelli-
gent communication.
