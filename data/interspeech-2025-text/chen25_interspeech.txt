Abstract
Speaker recognition is essential for secure authentication and
personalized voice assistants in smart home settings, but it faces
challenges due to intrinsic speaker variability, such as aging and
emotional fluctuations. Existing methods often rely on pretrain-
ing and require extensive data. To address these challenges,
we propose a framework for time-varying and emotion-robust
open-set identification (OSI) for smart home environments, uti-
lizing few-shot foundation enrollment-time tuning and style-
rich zero-shot text-to-speech (TTS) systems. We explore best
practices for synthetic data selection and suitable open-set
outlier-focused loss functions. Our proposed method improves
handling emotional and aging variations in target speakers, en-
hancing robustness to intrinsic variability while maintaining re-
silience to unknown outliers. Experiments demonstrate strong
generalization across multiple time-varying and emotionally
rich benchmarks.
Index Terms: speaker recognition, speaker identification, few-
shot learning, speech synthesis, robust recognition
1. Introduction
Speaker recognition is a crucial task that identifies a speaker
from their voice, playing a vital role in secure authentication
systems and personalized voice assistants in smart home. How-
ever, achieving robust performance in real-world scenarios re-
mains challenging due to the inherent variability in speaker
voice prints. While environmental noise and mismatched
recording devices are known to degrade system performance,
intrinsic speaker variations—such as aging and emotional fluc-
tuations—can be just as disruptive. As speakers undergo physi-
ological changes or emotional shifts, their voice characteristics
(e.g., pitch, energy, prosody) change over time, undermining
recognition accuracy.In some cases, repeated enrollment under
different conditions has been used to mitigate these issues [1],
but such approaches are often complicated and do not fully ad-
dress the underlying variability.
Most existing studies on speaker recognition focus on en-
hancing robustness against device and environmental variabil-
ity, often relying on large-scale pretraining with diverse speak-
ers [2]. However, comparatively less attention has been given
to managing intrinsic speaker variability, especially over time
for a specific group of targets , which is particularly useful in
smart home settings [3] (or open-set speaker identification OSI).
V oice changes related to aging or emotional states remain a
significant hurdle for short-term, continuous, or time-varying
recognition tasks in these settings, where the goal is to robustly
recognize all speakers despite variations. Existing methods for

age-invariant [4] or emotion-invariant representations often re-
quire extensive enrollment data and intensive training, making
them impractical for real-world applications.
To address these challenges, we propose a novel framework
for time-varying and emotion-robust speaker recognition during
the enrollment stage using few-shot fine-tuning for target speak-
ers. Rather than relying on exhaustive domain-specific pretrain-
ing, our approach uses speaker-specific zero-shot text-to-speech
(TTS) systems to generate synthetic speech that captures the tar-
get speaker’s varying emotional states and intrinsic vocal char-
acteristics. This is particularly beneficial for groups of target
speakers in enrollment scenarios. We then perform few-shot
open-set fine-tuning with text-independent enrollment data, re-
ducing the number of required utterances to as few as three,
and explore the best practices for utilizing this augmented data.
Through this process, we significantly enhance the system’s ro-
bustness to intrinsic speaker variability.
Our major contributions are as follows:
• We demonstrate that recent zero-shot timbre-controllable
speech synthesis techniques, combined with a synthetic data
sampling control policy and training methodology, can effec-
tively address intrinsic speaker variations and achieve robust
speaker recognition in smart home OSI settings.
• We propose an open-set enrollment fine-tuning strategy that
integrates synthetic and real data to improve speaker recog-
nition OSI performance. This approach requires only lim-
ited enrollment samples while remaining focused on the tar-
get speaker and robust to outliers.
• We thoroughly evaluate our proposed method across several
challenging tasks, including time-varying and emotionally
rich evaluation benchmarks, showcasing its generalization
capabilities in real-world speaker recognition scenarios.
2. Related Work
Recent research has largely focused on learning intrinsic-
invariant speaker representations. For instance, [5, 6] intro-
duced an emotion-robust framework that utilizes pretrained
emotion-specific extractors and pretraining techniques for
emotion-robust embeddings. For aging-robust speaker recog-
nition, methods such as those in [1, 4, 7, 8] apply cross-
aging learning techniques to improve speaker recognition per-
formance over time. Most existing methods primarily focus on
pretraining with cross-domain data for robustness or using tem-
plate updating as a remedy for shifts in speaker characteristics.
Several studies have also explored the use of speech syn-
thesis to strengthen speaker recognition. Previous research
has examined data augmentation for text-dependent verification
[9, 10] and accent recognition pretraining [11]. Earlier TTS sys-

3.3. Tuning with outliers focusing loss functions
In fine-tuning, to focus on target variations while maintaining
proper attention to outliers, we introduce an open-set training
approach that models “otherness” (Figure 1). The core of this
approach is the use of reciprocal points to model the “otherness”
of speakers. Let Rk represent the reciprocal points for speaker
k. The goal is to maximize the distance from Rk.
For each speaker’s enhanced enrollment dataDenroll+ (in-
cluding synthetic unknown speakers), training is conducted us-
ing the speaker reciprocal point loss. The learning loss com-
bines reciprocal points loss and prototype loss, for speakerk:

4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental setting
4.1.1. Datasets and benchmarks (Table 1)
• ESD: It contains 10 English and 10 Mandarin speakers, with
350 utterances for each emotion per speaker across 5 distinct
emotional settings [28]. The data is relatively clean for each
speaker. Enrollment samples are all selected from the neu-
tral state and testing ones from 4 emotional states (happiness,
anger, sadness and surprise).
• IEMOCAP: It includes 10 speakers with more pronounced
emotional variation based on dramatic scripts or free expres-
sions [29]. We choose the script session which includes 5
target emotions. Each speaker has 500 utterance on aver-
age, covering all types of variations. The dataset selected
from plays is relatively noisy and emotional, capturing more
speaker intrinsic shifts. As in the ESD setup, neutral speech
are only for enrollment, and emotional ones for testing.
• V oxCeleb2: Sourced from Youtube, this dataset includes a
broad range of real-world samples of various speakers. The
training part is following the V oxwatch [3] settings for focus
on target speaker group OSI. The watch list is setting to 5 for
smart home (and also for all other datasets).
• SpeakerAging: A self-collected dataset1, focusing on time-
varying effects, with data sourced from both YouTube and
Bilibili. We select a subset of 15 speakers exhibiting sig-
nificant short-term aging variation. Each speaker contributes
over 1500 samples, with a balanced distribution across all
weekly time spans. Only enrolled at 0 month, the testing set
is evaluated at four time spans: 0, 4, 8, and 12 months.
All data, evaluation benchmarks and code are open-sourced1.
4.1.2. Core Learning Settings
The training set includes 3 enrollment samples for 5 target
speakers (5-watchlist [3]) and K-splits to cover all target speak-
ers in the dataset, with other speakers treated as outliers. Syn-
thetic samples are selected from 1000 sentences (similar content
type) in each dataset (selecting N nearest for each, Sec 3.2), syn-
thesized by GPTSoVITS-v2 or FishSpeech-v1.4. Additionally,
the Denroll+ set includes synthetic samples for 12 unknown
speakers, selected from 200 speakers in LibriTTS [30] based
on the selection policy (Section 3.2), and are treated as nor-
mal speakers. Few hyperparameters are needed: σ is set high,
using the farthest synthetic unknown speakers compared to all
targets. Rk and radius are learned, with MLP post-adapter (the
most effective tuning architecture in our experiments). Stan-
dard training settings are applied, and all other details are fully
demonstrated1.

4.1.3. Metrics
The metric of this method include thereshold independent met-
ric open-set classification rate (OSCR) [21, 22], equal error
rate (EER) and minDCF [3, 31]. OSCR specifically addresses
open-set thresholding—distinguishing unknowns from known
speakers and ensuring correct classification of known speak-
ers—whereas EER/minDCF provides a broader performance
measure without explicitly focusing on unknown classes.
4.2. Results
The experimental results using the proposed method are pre-
sented in Table 2 with the ERes2NetV2 foundation model [31].
In these experiments, the training target was optimized using the
emotional speaker dataset [28, 29]. Without any data augmenta-
tion, direct enrollment and compare embedding ( 1 ) show un-
stable performance across all datasets, largely due to significant
intrinsic speaker variations. Initially, we applied target speaker
data augmentation directly during enrollment ( 2 3 ). How-
ever, regardless of whether the controlling policy was used dur-
ing training, the performance of direct enrollment degraded. For
the fine-tuning–based method, we compared different sampling
controlling policies with SpeakerRPL loss (starting from 4 ).
Several observations emerged:
1. Enrollment only: Fine-tuning solely with 3 target enroll-
ment data degrades performance ( 4 ).
2. Target speaker data augmentation: While augmentating
target speaker data benefits when tuning compared to using
only the enrollment, it also tends to overfit ( 5 ).
3. Synthetic unknown speakers: Incorporating unknown syn-
thetic speakers during training is effective (6 ) and can there-
fore outperform the direct baseline 1 .
4. Targets augmentation and unknown speakers: Training
with both target data augmentation and unknown synthetic
speakers (even without controlling policy) further enhances
performance ( 7 ).
5. Controlling unknown speakers: Based on 8 , controlling
the unknown synthetic speakers leads to large additional im-
provements ( 8 ).
6. Optimal Full-Controlling Policy: The combination of con-
trolling policy to both targets and unknown speakers yields
the overall best performance ( 14 ).
The sampling and controlling strategy is critical across all
dataset evaluations. Additionally, we compare training losses
for few-shot learning ( 9 - 14 ), where the proposed Speaker-
RPL open-set recognition loss consistently performs well, par-
ticularly with a focus on outliers, in EER, minDCF metrics, and
OSCR. These findings align with the results in Table 4 using the
WavLM-TDNN models [32]. Furthermore, when applying the
proposed method to the SpeakerAging dataset (Table 3), we en-
rolled speakers only at the first time point and evaluated across
subsequent time points. The proposed method consistently out-
performs all baselines.

5. Conclusions
We propose a robust intrinsic speaker recognition system for
open-set identification in smart home scenarios, leveraging
speech synthesis and enrollment-time few-shot tuning. Our
findings demonstrate that an optimal synthetic data selec-
tion policy and suitable open-set outlier-focused loss func-
tions are critical for performance. We show that the pro-
posed SpeakerRPL-Controlling Policy outperforms alternative
approaches. Overall, the method improves training by better
handling emotional and aging variations in target speakers, en-
hancing robustness to intrinsic speaker variability while main-
taining resilience to unknown outliers. Experimental results un-
derscore the significant value of state-of-the-art, style-rich TTS
systems for speaker recognition tasks.
