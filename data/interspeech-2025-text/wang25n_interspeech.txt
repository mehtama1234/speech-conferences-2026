Abstract
Anomalous Sound Detection (ASD) requires modeling a
compact and discriminative normal sound distribution.
Re-
cently, angular margin loss with multiple sub-centers has been
shown to be effective for ASD by extending sub-centers to cap-
ture intra-class diversity and maximizing their orthogonality to
enhance model discriminability. However, existing methods do
not consider that the orthogonality of intra-class sub-centers
needs to be optimized based on the inherent data structure to
avoid over-extension of the representation space due to over-
orthogonality. To address this issue, we propose a Dual Or-
thogonality Sub-Center Loss (DOSCL) that enforces strict or-
thogonality of inter-class sub-centers to improve anomaly dis-
crimination while applying relaxed constraints on intra-class
sub-centers to capture the data structure.
Experiments on
the DCASE2023 Challenge Task2 dataset show that DOSCL
achieves 1.74% AUC and 0.62% pAUC improvements over a
strong baseline, validating its effectiveness.
Index Terms: anomalous sound detection, angular margin loss,
sub-center, orthogonality
1. Introduction
Anomalous Sound Detection (ASD) is crucial for applications
such as industrial monitoring [1], smart home technology [2],
and health inspection [3]. However, the acquisition of anoma-
lous sound is challenging, requiring the development of mod-
els that rely solely on normal sound data [4].
The primary
goal of ASD is to create a compact and discriminative distri-
bution of normal sounds to effectively detect anomalies that
deviate from this distribution [5].
To improve the modeling
of normal sound distributions for ASD, outlier exposure (OE)-
based [6] methods have received considerable attention [7, 8, 9].
These methods introduce data unrelated to the target of detec-
tion as proxy anomalies, allowing the construction of classifi-
cation tasks based on sample attributes [10]. The compactness
and discriminative power of normal sound distributions can be
further improved for OE-based methods by using angular mar-
gin losses, such as ArcFace [11], AdaCos [12]. They minimize
within-class compactness while maximizing between-class sep-
aration, closely aligning with the primary objective of the ASD
task [13, 14].
To more accurately capture the inherent diversity within
samples, angular margin loss with multiple sub-centers, such as
SCArcface [15], SCAdaCos [16], have been proposed. They ex-
pand the representation space by providing multiple sub-centers
for each class, thus improving the representation capability of
the model [17]. These class centers are often obtained by ran-
dom initialization and set to be non-trainable [16, 18], since
random vectors are nearly orthogonal to each other in high-
dimensional spaces [19], which ensures discriminability be-
tween different classes.
As the number of sub-centers for each class increases from
one to multiple, the relationships between these sub-centers can
be categorized into two types: inter-class pairs and intra-class
pairs.
To optimize anomaly discrimination, inter-class pairs
should be highly orthogonal [20], while intra-class pairs need
to be orthogonalized by considering the underlying structure
of the data to avoid representation space over-expansion [21].
However, existing methods do not consider this point and focus
solely on maximizing inter-class discriminability by enforcing
orthogonality among all sub-centers.
To address this issue, we propose a Dual Orthogonality
Sub-center Loss (DOSCL), which categorizes sub-centers into
inter-class and intra-class, imposing orthogonality constraints
in a dual mechanism.
DOSCL enforces strict orthogonality
constraints on the inter-class cosine similarity, while applying
relaxed constraints on the intra-class cosine similarity.
This
dual approach enables the model to enhance its ability to de-
tect anomalies while effectively adapting to the underlying
data structure. The AUC and pAUC are improved by 1.74%
and 0.62%, respectively, compared to a strong baseline on the
DCASE2023 Challenge Task2 dataset.

Due to the diversity of samples within a class, a single class
center is insufficient for efficient representation.
To address
this, angular margin loss with multiple sub-centers has been in-
troduced, e.g., SCArcface [15], SCAdaCos [16]. This kind of
methods expands the class-center matrix W from d × C × 1 to
d × C × S, which means that the number of centers per class is
extended from 1 to S. In addition, SCArcface represents sam-
ples by the nearest sub-center, while SCAdaCos uses multiple
sub-centers to model samples jointly, capturing more complex
distributions. Therefore, the joint representation strategy is able
to achieve better performance [16].
2.2. Orthogonality of class centers
As analyzed in [24], class centers should be well dispersed
in the feature space to maximize inter-class differences.
To
achieve this, existing approaches achieve orthogonalization be-
tween class centers from different perspectives. For example,
in [16], the class center matrix is initialized as an unlearnable
random matrix. While this improves the orthogonality between
sub-centers, it limits the ability of the model to match the un-
derlying structure of the data. In [23], an orthogonal regularity
term is imposed on the class center matrix to promote orthogo-
nality between class centers. Other possible improvements in-
clude initializing the class center matrices as orthogonal and
setting them as unlearnable. However, all of these approaches
only consider the orthogonality requirement of inter-class sub-
centers and cannot deal with the orthogonality requirement of
sub-centers in the multiple sub-center angular margin loss, as
we discuss in the next section.
3. Proposed method

Inter-class sub-centers.
To ensure that normal and proxy
anomaly samples are sufficiently separated in feature space, the
inter-class sub-centers should be as orthogonal as possible. This
separation enhances the sensitivity of the model to anomalies.

the probability density function of the angle θ between sub-
centers in high-dimensional space can be expressed as:

However, since the dimensionality of these sub-centers is inher-

dimensionality results in inadequate orthogonality of the ran-

d vectors pairwise orthogonal in a d-dimensional feature, when
the number of sub-centers pairs is larger than the feature dimen-
sionality, the inter-class sub-centers should be preferentially or-
thogonalized to ensure the sensitivity of the model to anomalies.
Intra-class sub-centers. The size of the feature representa-
tion space for intra-class sub-centers is primarily influenced by
the number of sub-centers. When there is only one class center,
high-probability samples are concentrated around it, resulting in
a limited representation space. As the number of sub-centers in-
creases, samples are distributed not only around the centers but
also in the intermediate regions, thereby expanding the feature
space. Given a fixed number of sub-centers, the angle between
these sub-centers further determines the size of the feature space
[26]. However, samples in the same class should be represented
in a compact space due to their inherent similarity [27], even
though intra-class diversity exists. Excessive orthogonality be-
tween intra-class sub-centers can lead to an overly large and dis-
persed representation space. This makes the distribution of nor-
mal samples less compact and increases the risk of anomalous
samples finding a suitable location within this space, reducing
the effectiveness of anomaly detection.

4. Experimental results
4.1. Experimental setup
Experiments were conducted on the DCASE2023 Challenge
Task2 dataset (“First-shot unsupervised anomalous sound de-
tection for machine condition monitoring”) [28], which is an
ASD task dataset consisting of real-world data. This dataset,
sourced from MIMII DG [29] and ToyADMOS2 [30], contains
fourteen device types: Fan, Gearbox, Bearing, Slide rail, Toy
car, Toy train, Valve, Vacuum, ToyTank, ToyNscale, ToyDrone,
bandsaw, grinder, and shaker. Each type has only one indepen-
dent device data. The data from each device in dataset consist
of 1000 normal samples in the training set, of which 990 are
from the source domain and 10 are from the target domain, and
the test set contains 200 samples, of which 100 are from the
source domain and 100 are from the target domain. The sam-
ples in dataset are 10 seconds in length, with a sampling rate of
16 kHz.
Evaluation metrics including area under the receiver operat-
ing characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) and partial AUC (pAUC)
with p = 0.1. The metric pAUC is the AUC computed under a
low false-positive rate range, namely [0, p], which is used be-
cause a high true positive-rate is desirable under this conditions
in practical applications to avoid frequent false alarms [31]. All
experiments were conducted in the development and evaluation
set of the DCASE2023 Challenge Task 2 dataset, and the re-
sults are the mean and standard deviation of five independent
experiments.
The baseline was adopted from [32], an efficient ASD
method that uses a finely designed model structure and is trained
by SCAdaCos with randomly initialized and non-learnable class
center matrix. All experiments maintain consistent network ar-
chitectures and training setup with the baseline, only modifying
the angular margin loss components to ensure controlled com-
parison. The training data is categorized into 186 classes based
on the attributes of the samples. For multiple sub-center an-
gular margin losses, each class has 16 sub-centers with feature
dimensions of 512.

4.2. Performance comparison
Considering that our approach is based on angular margin loss
and is related to the orthogonality of the class centers, we per-
formed three sets of experiments: First, we evaluated the basic
angular margin losses (ArcFace, AdaCos) and their trainable
multiple subclass extensions (SCArcFace, SCAdaCos). Both
SCArcFace and SCAdaCos utilize a joint representation strat-
egy. Second, we investigated different strategies for the orthog-
onalization of sub-centers: 1) fixed centers with random initial-
ization (∗), 2) fixed centers with orthogonal initialization (†) and
3) learnable sub-centers with orthogonality loss (OL). Third, we
integrated our proposed DOSCL (β = 100, α = 6 × 103) with
existing multiple sub-center angular margin losses. The experi-
mental results are shown in Table 1.

4.3. Performance impact of hyperparameter
The hyperparameters α and β are designed to regulate the mean
and variance of sub-centers cosine similarities, respectively. A
sensitivity analyzes were conducted by alternately fixing each
parameter, for the SCAdaCos and SCArcFace methods with
DOSCL, respectively, as shown in Fig. 3. The harmonic mean
of combined AUC and pAUC H was chosen as the performance
metric, as it provides a comprehensive measure of performance.
The results show that the performance of the model depends
more on the choice of α, and if α is not the optimal choice, the
performance can drop significantly.

5. Conclusion
This paper analyzed the orthogonality requirements of inter-
class and intra-class sub-centers within the multiple sub-center
angular margin loss framework and proposed a novel DOSCL
loss function to optimize their orthogonality. DOSCL ensures
that inter-class sub-centers are as orthogonal as possible to en-
hance anomaly detection, while applying a relaxed constraint
on intra-class sub-centers. This strategy allows the sub-centers
to better match the underlying data structure, thereby avoid-
ing overly constrained sub-centers that could impair the com-
pactness of the distribution.
Evaluated on the DCASE2023
Challenge Task2 dataset, DOSCL enhanced model performance
compared to a strong baseline, achieving a 1.74% increase in
AUC and a 0.62% increase in pAUC.
