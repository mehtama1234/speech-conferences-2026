Efficient Streaming TTS Acoustic Model with Depthwise RVQ Decoding
Strategies in a Mamba Framework
Joun Yeop Lee, Sangjun Park, Byoung Jin Choi, Ji-Hyun Lee,
Min-Kyung Kim, Hoon-Young Cho
Samsung Research, Samsung Electronics, Republic of Korea
{jounyeop.lee, sj0.park, bj87.choi, jihyuny.lee, mk 0.kim, h.y.cho}@samsung.com
Abstract
Recent advances in neural codec-based text-to-speech (TTS)
systems have achieved remarkable synthesized speech quality.
However, their reliance on large model size and heavy computa-
tional requirements limits CPU-based on-device deployment. In
this work, we present a Mamba-based streaming acoustic model
with two novel depthwise decoding strategies for residual vector
quantization (RVQ)-based codec: a Masked Language Model
(MLM) approach and an Implicit Neural Representation (INR)
approach. The MLM strategy iteratively refines tokens along
the code depth axis to enhance speech quality, whereas the INR
approach predicts all quantization levels in parallel to reduce
computational costs. We further incorporate a speaker embed-
ding conditioning mechanism for a zero-shot scenario, enabling
robust performance on unseen speakers. Experimental results
demonstrate comparable or even superior improvements in both
objective and subjective metrics compared to other larger TTS
baseline models.

1. Introduction
Recent advancements in text-to-speech (TTS) technology have
seen a paradigm shift from traditional continuous intermediate
features, such as mel-spectrogram [1, 2, 3], to discrete token-
based intermediate features [4, 5, 6, 7]. This shift has re-
sulted in significant performance improvements, driven largely
by large-scale models and language model-inspired methodolo-
gies. These token-based approaches have enabled more fine-
grained acoustic modeling and better integration with language
models, contributing to remarkable gains in speech synthesis
quality.
However, while these advancements excel in high-resource,
server-based environments, they do not inherently address the
long-standing challenges of computational cost and real-time
performance, which are critical for on-device applications. The
computational demands of large models remain a bottleneck
when deploying TTS systems on resource-constrained hard-
ware, where latency and efficiency are important. Moreover,
a substantial number of current devices have not yet fully ex-
ploited the capabilities of parallel computation facilitated by
GPU/NPU. Previous works using continuous intermediate fea-
ture have adapted a lightweight, streaming acoustic model de-
coder architecture which has low latency[8]. Similarly, we
propose a Mamba-based [9] streaming decoder in the acoustic
model to bridge this gap for the discrete token-based TTS.
Numerous neural codec models utilize residual vector
quantization (RVQ) for speech synthesis [10, 11, 12, 13]. In
these models, multiple quantization tokens must be predicted
at each time step. Previous works, such as MusicGen [14] and
Small-E [15], have employed delayed decoding strategies that
buffer multiple frames before synthesis, leading to increased la-
tency. In contrast, our acoustic model directly predicts RVQ
tokens corresponding to each time step, enabling low-latency
inference. Our approach introduces two novel depthwise de-
coding strategies tailored for RVQ:
• Masked Language Model (MLM) decoding strategy that it-
eratively refines tokens along the code depth axis to capture
inter-level dependencies, which enhances synthesis quality.
• Implicit neural representation (INR) strategy that predicts
multiple quantization levels simultaneously to reduce com-
putational overhead and inference latency.
These strategies are designed to maintain the benefits of token-
based TTS systems while overcoming the computational and
latency limitations inherent to on-device deployment.
The MLM approach proposed in this work differs from
prior non-autoregressive (NAR) models, such as [16], which
consider both the time and depth axes of tokens for masking
scheduling. Although effective for NAR generation, such ap-
proaches are not well suited for streaming applications. In con-
trast, our method only considers the depth axis of tokens, ensur-
ing compatibility with streaming inference while still modeling
dependencies between tokens.
Unlike prior work where INR has been applied in TTS pri-
marily in alignment modeling, such as in SegINR [17], our ap-
proach leverages INR for depthwise prediction. This allows for
efficient modeling across quantization levels without requiring
additional blank tokens, resulting in a simple and fast depthwise
prediction mechanism.
By integrating decoding strategies into a Mamba-based
streaming acoustic model, we present a practical solution that
balances synthesis quality with the efficiency required for real-
time, on-device TTS applications. The following sections pro-
vide detailed insights into the core techniques and experimental
results. Experimental results on the zero-shot scenario demon-
strate that our proposed model achieves comparable or superior
performance in terms of speech quality and speaker similarity,
despite having lower computational requirements and a smaller
model size compared to baseline models. Audio samples are
available on our demo page1.

2.1. Mamba
Mamba [9] is a structured state-space model that is specif-
ically designed for efficient sequential data modeling. Un-
like Transformer-based architectures, which rely on global self-
attention, Mamba processes inputs incrementally. The key ad-
vantages of Mamba include:
• Streaming-Friendly Inference: It generates outputs incre-
mentally without requiring access to the entire input se-
quence.
• Scalability: Its computational efficiency enables efficient
modeling of long sequences.
These properties establish Mamba as an effective alternative to
attention-based models in streaming TTS scenarios where real-
time performance and resource efficiency are paramount.
2.2. Masked Language Model (MLM)
The MLM [18] has proven its effectiveness in capturing con-
textual dependencies in natural language processing by training
models to predict randomly masked tokens. Inspired by its suc-
cess in natural language processing, MLM has been adapted to
TTS systems [16].
In [16], masking is applied mainly along the time axis dur-
ing training. Specifically, parts of the token representation se-
quence are masked randomly, and the model is trained to predict
the missing tokens using the surrounding context. This masking
strategy enables the model to learn temporal relationships be-
tween consecutive tokens, which are crucial for accurate speech
synthesis.
During inference, the entire sequence of RVQ representa-
tions is initially masked, and tokens are predicted iteratively.
MLM’s capability to capture temporal dependencies across
masked tokens makes it suitable for generating acoustic rep-
resentations in high fidelity.
2.3. Implicit Neural Representation (INR)
INRs [19] are a framework for encoding continuous signals,
such as audio or images, as continuous functions of their input
coordinates. INRs typically use multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs)
to learn these mappings, representing complex structures com-
pactly and efficiently without relying on discrete grids.
A key advantage of INRs lies in their ability to encode high-
dimensional data as smooth, parameterized functions. Instead
of explicitly storing data points or features, INRs model signals
as mappings from coordinates (e.g., spatial or temporal indices)
to values (e.g., pixel intensities or tokens). This approach al-
lows INRs to generalize well to unseen input resolutions and
enables continuous interpolation between points. Especially in
this paper, we modify SegINR [17] for depthwise decoding.
This foundational technology provides a basis for efficient,
compact representations in various domains, including TTS sys-
tems where multiple depth levels of tokens need to be predicted
effectively.

3.1. Mamba-based Streaming Acoustic Model
For the streaming acoustic model, as in the typical streaming
TTS systems, the entire text sequencex0:N is given, and acous-
tic tokens must be generated one at a time to ensure low la-
tency. To achieve this, we design the encoder and streaming de-
coder utilizing the Mamba-based framework with specific roles
and configurations. The overall architecture of the proposed
Mamba-based acoustic model is represented in Figure 1.
Building upon the Mamba, we further extend it with
speaker conditioning (e.g. SpkMamba) to enable zero-shot
speaker adaptation. Specifically, the speaker embedding is in-
jected into the input projection layers of the Mamba block.
For speaker representation, we utilize an ECAPA-TDNN-based
speaker encoder [20], modified to take acoustic tokens as an in-
put instead of mel spectrograms. This speaker encoder is trained
jointly with the acoustic model, ensuring that the learned em-
beddings are well aligned with the TTS objective.
The encoder processes the entire input text phoneme se-
quence using a bidirectional Mamba architecture [21] replaced
with SpkMamba, enabling the model to leverage the full-text
sequence context for accurate text-to-acoustic token mapping.
Duration is estimated through the monotonic alignment search
during training and a duration predictor at the inference phase.
Subsequently, Gaussian upsampling aligns the encoder output
sequence with the acoustic token sequence using duration.
The decoder operates autoregressively frame-by-frame. At
each timestep t, it concatenates the previous acoustic embed-
ding At−1, obtained by summing the RVQ token embed-
dings a0:D−1,t−1 across all D depth levels, with the current
upsampled encoder representation Et. This joint representation
is then used to predict the current acoustic tokena0:D−1,t using
a depthwise decoding module.
3.2. Depthwise Decoding Strategies for RVQ
To use RVQ codec in TTS, we should predict multiple depth
levels of tokens at each acoustic frame. Thus, accurate and ef-
ficient decoding of RVQ tokens is critical for high-quality TTS
synthesis. Our framework addresses this challenge by intro-
ducing two depthwise decoding strategies that operate along
the code depth axis. Moreover, these strategies can be applied
to various types of TTS systems utilizing RVQ-based neural
codecs, including Speech LLM scenarios where the input se-
quence is also processed in a streaming manner.

3.2.1. Masked Language Model (MLM) Decoding
The MLM decoding strategy repurposes the concept of mask-
ing from natural language processing. Unlike previous works
that mask tokens over the time axis, we focus solely on the
depth dimension of RVQ. During training, we employ depth-
wise masking, where selected portions of the RVQ represen-
tations are masked, and the model is trained to predict these
missing values. This strategy encourages the model to learn
inter-level dependencies.
At inference time, for a given frame, all depth tokens are
initially masked. Then the system iteratively predicts missing
tokens following a fixed schedule as in Figure 2(a), applying
top-k sampling (with k = 5 ) to maintain diversity. While this
iterative procedure increases computation relative to a single-
pass approach, it results in high-quality token generation.
3.2.2. Implicit Neural Representation (INR) Decoding
In contrast to the iterative MLM strategy, the INR-based ap-
proach leverages depth indices to predict all RVQ quantization
levels in parallel as in Figure 2(b). We modify the SegINR
model [17], to take input the sequence of depth indices (from
0 to D− 1) along with a repeated hidden representation of the
last SpkMamba block Hn. By treating the depth indices as con-
tinuous coordinates, the INR model generates the complete set
of depthwise tokens in a single forward pass, eliminating the
need for iterative refinement and explicit blank tokens.
Since INR performs a single forward pass over the depth
levels at each frame, it runs faster than MLM-based decoding.
However, it may capture slightly fewer cross-depth dependen-
cies, potentially leading to marginally lower synthesis quality
compared to MLM. Thus, the INR approach is particularly ap-
pealing for resource-constrained applications where minimal la-
tency is a primary goal.
3.3. Integration and Trade-offs
By integrating the proposed acoustic model with our two depth-
wise decoding strategies, we offer a flexible solution tailored to
diverse deployment scenarios. The MLM-based approach ex-
cels in synthesizing high-quality, natural-sounding speech by
capturing inter-RVQ level correlations. However, the INR-
based method prioritizes speed and computational efficiency
by generating tokens in parallel. Consequently, system design-
ers can choose the appropriate decoding strategy based on spe-
cific quality or latency requirements, thus enabling effective on-
device TTS across a range of hardware specifications.
4. Experiments
In this section, we detail the experimental evaluations of our
proposed streaming TTS system, demonstrating the effective-
ness and efficiency of our model in a zero-shot scenario.
4.1. Experimental Setup
We conducted experiments on the LibriTTS dataset [22], which
consists of multi-speaker recordings. Our training data included
the train-clean-100, train-clean-360, and train-other-500 sub-
sets. For evaluation, we selected 500 utterances from the test-
clean subset, ensuring a strict zero-shot condition by using un-
seen speakers. The objective evaluations were performed on
all 500 utterances, while subjective evaluations were conducted
using a sampled subset consisting of 30 utterances to ensure re-
liable human assessments.
4.2. Evaluation Metrics
Objective tests were performed using the following metrics:
• Character Error Rate (CER, %): Measures pronunciation
accuracy by comparing ASR transcriptions (obtained via a
Whisper large model [23]) with ground truth text.
• Speaker Embedding Cosine Similarity (SECS) : Quanti-
fies speaker similarity by computing the cosine similarity be-
tween embeddings extracted with Resemblyzer2.
• UTMOS: Predicts perceptual quality via a neural MOS pre-
diction network [24].
• Real-Time Factor (RTF) : Assesses computational effi-
ciency by measuring average synthesis time divided by the
audio duration across the test set.
• latency (sec): Measures the average time taken for the model
to generate the first acoustic token after receiving the input
text.
For RTF and latency, we focused solely on the token generation
process, excluding codec-related processing, as our method is
related to the acoustic model. Both metrics were measured on a
single-threaded CPU (AMD EPYC 7413).
Subjective evaluations were conducted through human lis-
tening tests on a scale from 1 to 5. The following metrics were
used:
• Mean Opinion Score (MOS) : Rates overall speech quality
including naturalness.
• Similarity MOS (SMOS) : Evaluates perceptual speaker
similarity compared to a reference sample.
For both subjective tests, 150 testers participated via Amazon
MTurk3 platform.
4.3. Implementation Details
For the neural codec, we utilized the official pretrained HiFi-
Codec [10] model, which employed a grouped-RVQ approach.
The codec consisted of four codebooks, divided into two
groups, each containing two codebooks. The model was trained
on 24 kHz speech samples, down-sampled by a factor of 320.
For text input, we employed the International Phonetic
Alphabet-based phonemizer4 to ensure consistent and accurate
phonetic representations. Our acoustic model consisted of four
bi-SpkMamba blocks (hidden dimension 384) in the encoder

4.4. Comparison Models
The proposed models were compared against state-of-the-art
baselines, including VITS [25], V ALLE-X [26], the model pro-
posed by Lee et al. (2024) [6], and Small-E [15]. To adapt
the baseline VITS for zero-shot, we incorporated the ECAPA-
TDNN as the reference encoder. Except for VITS, all three
models are token-based TTS systems. Specifically, we used
mamba-based Small-E to compare mamba-based structures.
Three configurations of the proposed streaming Mamba
acoustic model (SMAM) were evaluated: SMAM with
MLM decoding (SMAM+MLM), SMAM with INR decoding
(SMAM+INR), and SMAM without advanced decoding strate-
gies (SMAM+noMLM). The SMAM+noMLM, utilized for the
ablation study, employs a single-step decoding process without
any masking during both the training and inference phases.
All results of our experiments are presented in Table 1.
4.5. Objective Results Analysis
Our experiments demonstrate that, despite having significantly
fewer parameters (25–26M) compared to baselines (ranging
from 46M to 371M), the proposed methods achieved notable
improvements in terms of intelligibility. SMAM+MLM at-
tained a CER of 2.73%, outperforming all the baselines, while
SMAM+INR achieved a CER of 3.51%, which is slightly lower
than those of SMAM+MLM.
In terms of SECS, although the proposed models did not
surpass other baselines, they achieved scores close to the ground
truth (GT) benchmark, indicating that speaker similarity is well
preserved despite the small model size. The UTMOS results
confirmed high perceptual quality, with SMAM+MLM scoring
4.13 compared to lower scores from the larger baseline models.
From a computational perspective, SMAM models demon-
strated superior efficiency. SMAM+MLM achieves an RTF
of 0.701, while SMAM+INR lowers it further to 0.568, sig-
nificantly outperforming non-streaming baselines, which range
from 2.404 to 7.665. By supporting streaming on a CPU,
our models maintain latencies below 0.07 seconds. In con-
trast, baseline models generate entire utterances at once, re-
sulting in latencies of at least 14 seconds, which increase fur-
ther for longer texts. This substantial improvement emphasizes
SMAM’s effectiveness for streaming TTS on CPU devices.
Also, objective results highlight a clear trade-off as men-
tioned in Section 3.3: while the SMAM+MLM offered the high-
est synthesis quality, SMAM+INR provided advantages in com-
putational speed and model compactness.
4.6. Subjective Results Analysis
Similar to the UTMOS results, the MOS indicated that the pro-
posed methods deliver better perceptual speech quality, with
SMAM+MLM showing the best score of 4.02. The SMOS re-
sults differ from those of SECS, as SMAM+MLM exhibited the
highest perceptual similarity, scoring 3.36, compared to Lee et
al., scoring 3.27. This discrepancy arose since SECS measures
embedding space distances, which may not fully align with per-
ceptual similarity judgments. Thus, while SECS reflects overall
similarity in the embedding space, SMOS captures subjective
perceptions of speaker similarity more accurately. Also, the per-
formance between SMAM+MLM and SMAM+INR supported
the trade-off as in the objective results.
4.7. Ablation Study
To show the impact of our depthwise iterative refinement strat-
egy, we compared SMAM+MLM with SMAM+noMLM. The
removal of MLM resulted in a severe degradation of all metrics
in both objective and subjective tests, except RTF and latency
results. These ablation results showed the importance of captur-
ing inter-level dependencies for high-quality synthesis.
5. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduced a zero-shot streaming TTS sys-
tem based on the Mamba framework (SMAM), incorporating
two novel depthwise decoding strategies for RVQ: Masked
Language Model (MLM) and Implicit Neural Representation
(INR). Our experiments demonstrated that SMAM achieves
high computational efficiency while maintaining superior per-
formance compared to larger baseline models, with a clear
trade-off between quality (MLM) and speed (INR). These find-
ings enable flexible deployment across diverse hardware con-
straints, making SMAM a strong candidate for real-time stream-
ing TTS on CPU. Future work will explore fully streaming mod-
els across the entire TTS pipeline including codec and apply
depthwise decoding strategies to decoder-only speech LLMs.
