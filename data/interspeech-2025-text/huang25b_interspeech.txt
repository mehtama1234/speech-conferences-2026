Abstract
As deep neural networks permeate various aspects of so-
ciety, research has demonstrated their vulnerability to back-
door attacks, particularly with untrustworthy third-party plat-
forms, enabling attackers to manipulate model outputs using
customized triggers.
However, most audio-based backdoor
methods are either sample-agnostic or audible. In this paper, we
propose Compression-based Backdoor Attack (CBA), a novel
strategy designed specifically for audio compression scenar-
ios, which also utilizes fusion as well as iterative optimization
strategies to generate sample-specific triggers and ensure both
the effectiveness and stealthiness of the triggers. Extensive ex-
periments validate the attack efficiency and robustness.
Index Terms: Backdoor attack, deep learning, audio compres-
sion, speech classification.
1. Introduction
Over the past several years, Deep Neural Networks (DNNs)
have evolved continuously and gained widespread recognition
for their superior performance, permeating various aspects of
daily life, including privacy and security-sensitive areas. En-
suring the security of DNNs is paramount, yet, despite their su-
perior performance, they are vulnerable across various stages,
from data collection to model deployment. And these vulnera-
bilities can be exploited by malicious attacks. One such vulner-
ability is the risk of backdoor attacks [1].
The efficacy of DNNs is highly dependent on extensive
training datasets and considerable computational resources, im-
posing a significant burden on individual researchers and small
teams. To avoid the labor-intensive process of creating datasets,
researchers often resort to third-party datasets or data sourced
on the Web, which can potentially compromise both data accu-
racy and security. Furthermore, training large-scale DNNs re-
quires substantial hardware resources, prompting researchers to
consider cost-saving solutions, such as outsourcing. Two com-
mon approaches are Machine Learning as a Service (MLaaS)
and transfer learning, both of which would introduce security
concerns, including the threat of backdoor attacks.
Backdoor attacks aim to embed hidden threats within a
model that are triggered under specific conditions to manipu-
late the model’s output. In the audio domain, Koffas et al. [2]
utilized ultrasonic as triggers. Liu et al. [3] selected correspond-
ing triggers from a predefined pool of natural or ambient audible
sounds. Cai et al [4]. proposed adding a small portion of audio
as a trigger using the auditory masking effect. However, some
of these methods are audible and even inaudible ones severely
disrupt the audio spectrogram. In addition, other methods [5],
[6], [7] proposed stealthy backdoor attacks, but require higher
poisoning ratio for effective attack, making them more suscep-
tible to detection. Considering that additive noise is more easily
noticeable and detectable by humans, meanwhile, achieving a
balance between attack effectiveness and stealthiness presents
a significant challenge, this paper introduces a novel method
named CBA that generates backdoor audio samples by com-
pressing the original audio, which results in imperceptible and
sample-specific triggers that closely resemble the original au-
dio. Our contributions are as follows:
• We introduce a backdoor attack via audio compression to in-
conspicuously poison the training data and can be passively
activated.
• We designed an iterative optimization algorithm to generate
sample-adaptive triggers while maintaining the stealthy of the
triggers. With just a 5% injection rate of poisoning samples,
we achieve an attack success rate exceeding 99%.
• We conduct extensive experiments to compare our method
with existing approaches, and evaluate its performance in
terms of effectiveness, stealth and robustness.

2.2. Threat Model
2.2.1. Attackers’ Knowledge and Capabilities
Our attack is designed for MLaaS scenario, in which the victim
uploads a validated dataset and model to a third-party platform
for training, and then hosts the trained model on platform and
provides API interface for users. In this scenario, attackers can
generate poisoned samples by accessing training data but cannot
obtain any information about the model structure.
2.2.2. Attackers’ Goal
The attacker’s objectives can be summarized in the following
three goals:
• Effectiveness: The primary goal is to embed a backdoor into
the model while maintaining its original performance and
achieving a high attack success rate.
• Stealthiness:
Poisoned samples must remain sufficiently
stealthy, with minimal differences from clean samples.
• Robustness: We define robustness in two aspects: (1) the em-
bedded backdoor should resist classic defense methods; (2)
poisoned samples should preserve trigger features and remain
effective after data preprocessing.
3. PROPOSED METHOD
In this study, we highlight the distinctive properties of au-
dio compression that make it particularly suitable for back-
door attacks. Audio compression is widely used to reduce data
size while maintaining perceptual quality. The process is in-
herently content-dependent, leading to variable perturbations
across samples. Furthermore, compression can also be seen
as a data enhancement method that improves the performance
of the model and counteracts overfitting by introducing possi-
ble perturbations. Given these properties, we propose an audio
compression-based trigger mechanism for backdoor attack as
illustrated in Fig. 1.

3.1. Trigger Generation with Sample Interpolation
The methodology employed in our research comprises two prin-
cipal components: audio compression and interpolation masks
generation. Initially, the audio data is compressed using two
distinct techniques: bit compression and wavelet compression.
• Bit Compression: We scale the audio to predetermined range
of bit depths followed by integer quantization.
• Wavelet Compression: Wavelet decomposition is utilized to
segregate the audio signal’s frequency content by multilayer
wavelet decomposition, after which the resulting coefficients
are compressed.
The compressed samples are used as the initial backdoor
samples. However, they differ significantly from the original
audio, as shown in Fig. 2, making them easily detectable by
the victim. Therefore, to enhance the stealthiness of the triggers
within the compressed audio samples, i.e., to increase the sim-
ilarity between the backdoor samples and the original samples,
we propose a method to embed the features of the compressed
audio into the features of the original audio. This method ef-
fectively reduces the unnatural perturbations introduced by the
compression process. We achieve this by designing a mask vi
that controls the interpolation of the compressed features. Our
objective is to maintain the effectiveness of the compressed fea-
tures as triggers while minimizing the difference between the
feature of compressed audio and original audio.

4. EXPERIMENTS
4.1. Experimental Setup
Dataset and Models. This paper adopts Google Speech Com-
mands dataset [9]. From this dataset, 21,115 audio samples
with 10 labels were selected. We evaluated the performance
using three deep learning models, ResNet34 [8], LSTM [10],
and KWT [11].
Attack Setup. We use Resnet18 as the surrogate model and
train for only 10 epochs with 40% training data. Each mask vi
was optimized 200 iterations, with a learning rate η of 0.0002,
and mask value bounds lb, ub of 0.9 and 0.2. We set λ to 2.5
as default and the poisoning ratio was set to 5% following [12].
The attack results were averaged over three independent exper-
iments.
Training Setup. All experiments were conducted using the
PyTorch framework on Nvidia RTX A6000 GPUs. We set the
batch size of the victim model to 256. Both models use Adam
optimizer with a learning rate of 0.002, and a cross-entropy loss
function.
Baseline and Evaluation Metrics.We compare CBA with
several baseline methods, including BadNets [13], Blended
[14], Ultrasonic [15], PBSM [4] and PhaseBack [7].
Two
metrics are used for evaluation: (1) Accuracy of Clean Data
(ACC): measures the percentage of correctly classified audio
samples without triggers. (2) Attack Success Rate (ASR): in-
dicates the percentage of poisoned samples classified as target
labels by the victim model.

4.3.4. STRIP [18]
Fig. 4 shows the detection results of applying STRIP to test
samples for the model poisoned by CBA. It demonstrates that
the clean and backdoor samples have similar and non-separable
entropy distributions. Since the triggers of CBA are sample-
specific, superimposing different content onto the poisoned
samples cannot destroys corresponding triggers.
4.3.5. Neural Cleanse [19]
The anomaly index of the reverse-engineered trigger identified
by Neural Cleanse for the actual target class in the model poi-
soned by CBA-Bit and CBA-Wavelet is 1.86 and 1.74 respec-
tively, which falls below the threshold of 2. This indicates that
CBA successfully evades Neural Cleanse detection. The rea-
son is that Neural Cleanse is specifically designed to detect tiny
and fixed triggers, making it incapable of reverse-engineering
an adaptive, global, and sample-specific backdoor trigger.

5. CONCLUSION
This letter proposes an effective inaudible backdoor attack
paradigm for speech classification. The proposed attack is vali-
dated on three deep learning models, demonstrating high attack
success rates without affecting the prediction accuracy of nor-
mal inputs across all tested models. Our method surpasses ex-
isting backdoor attacks in both stealth and effectiveness, while
also exhibiting robust against several widely used and powerful
backdoor defense countermeasures.
