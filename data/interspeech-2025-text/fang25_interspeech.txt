MMLoRA: Multitask Memory Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning for
Multimodal SER

Abstract
The differences in emotional expression present significant
challenges for the development of effective emotion recogni-
tion systems. Although large language models (LLMs) have
demonstrated strong performance, their generalization in emo-
tion recognition tasks is often limited by variations in indi-
vidual emotional expression, which remain inadequately ad-
dressed even by parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques such
as LoRA. To address these challenges, we proposes a mul-
titask memory parameter-efficient fine-tuning method (MM-
LoRA) that enhances multimodal SER by incorporating gender
as an auxiliary task. The method leverages shared LoRA experts
to facilitate information exchange between tasks and employs a
mixture of LoRA experts to process task-specific information.
Additionally, a memory mechanism propagates task-specific in-
formation across layers. Experimental results demonstrate that
the proposed MMLoRA significantly improves emotion recog-
nition performance compared to vanilla LoRA.
Index Terms: large language model, LoRA, multimodal, mul-
titask, speech emotion recognition.
1. Introduction
Speech emotion recognition (SER) is essential for improving
the effectiveness of human-computer interaction and personal-
ized services, and it plays a pivotal role in advancing emotional
intelligence systems [1]. A key challenge in SER lies in the vari-
ability of emotional expression across individuals, as the same
emotion can be conveyed differently by different people [2, 3].
To address this challenge and enhance cross-individual general-
ization, researchers have employed multitask learning strategies
that account for individual differences, including gender [4, 5],
identity [6], and so on, thereby improving the accuracy of emo-
tion recognition.
In recent years, LLMs have achieved significant break-
throughs across various domains [7]. Notable examples include
models like LLaMA2 [8] and Qwen [9] in natural language pro-
cessing (NLP), as well as Multimodal Large Language Mod-
els (MLLMs) like Video-LLaMA[10], LLaVA [11] and GPT-4
[12], which extend these advancements to tasks involving multi-
modal information. These large-scale pre-trained models lever-
age extensive pre-training to capture intricate patterns in data,
demonstrating outstanding performance when fine-tuned on a
wide range of downstream tasks. However, as the scale of these
models continues to grow, the computational cost associated
with comprehensive fine-tuning has become a significant chal-
lenge. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) [13] has emerged as an
effective approach, freezing pre-trained model weights and in-

has shown promise in enhancing the adaptability of LLMs to

Despite the success of LoRA in alleviating GPU mem-
ory limitations during fine-tuning, its application to multi-task
learning scenarios exposes critical limitations [14]. Fundamen-
tal challenges stem from the restricted trainable parameter bud-
get and persistent catastrophic forgetting [15], jointly impairing
the model’s cross-task generalization capabilities. Current ef-
forts in multi-task fine-tuning predominantly focus on resolv-
ing inter-task conflicts through architectural innovations. Chen
et al.
[16] developed a sparse mixture of LoRA experts to
enhance the baseline architecture, effectively alleviating data
conflicts during multimodal large language model (MLLM) in-
struction tuning across heterogeneous domains. Similarly, Song
et al. [17] devised language-specific LoRA modules for Whis-
per models to reduce linguistic interference while preserving
existing knowledge during new language integration.
While
these approaches demonstrate progress in task conflict mitiga-
tion, they predominantly adopt a parameter-isolation paradigm
that enforces single-task specialization during inference. This
architectural constraint not only diminishes the synergistic ben-
efits of multi-task learning but also fundamentally restricts
downstream tasks from harnessing cross-task knowledge trans-
fer, which is a critical advantage inherent to multi-task frame-
works.
Multitask learning strategies that incorporate personalized
knowledge have proven effective in Speech Emotion Recogni-
tion (SER) for addressing individual differences, thereby im-
proving emotion recognition accuracy. To extend these bene-
fits while overcoming the task-isolation limitations of existing
parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods, this paper proposes a
novel multitask parameter-efficient fine-tuning method for mul-
timodal SER. In the proposed MMLoRA, we adopt gender
recognition and emotion recognition as the multitask setting.
Specifically, a universal LoRA expert is introduced to facilitate
cross-task information sharing, while task-specific information
is captured through a mixture of task-specific LoRA experts.
Additionally, a memory mechanism is incorporated, allowing
the model to retain and utilize task-specific information across
layers, thus enabling simultaneous multitask inference. In our
experiments, VideoLLAMA serves as the base multimodal pre-
trained language model, though this approach is not limited to
this model and can be applied to other open-source large models
as well. The main contributions of this paper are summarized
as follows:

2. Method
In this paper, we focus on enhancing the performance of ef-
ficient parameter fine-tuning methods in MLLMs for multi-
modal SER through multitask learning. The multitask setting
is considered as a gender-assisted emotion recognition task in
this paper. We validate the effectiveness of the proposed MM-
LoRA method within an open-source multimoda LLM, Video-
LLaMA. Section 3.1 provides a detailed explanation of the
proposed MMLoRA architecture, while Section 3.2 outlines
the fine-tuning process for the gender-assisted multimodal SER
task within Video-LLaMA model.
2.1. Preliminaries
LoRA was introduced as an effective technique for fine-tuning
pre-trained LLMs with reduced computational cost. It works
by freezing the original weights and introducing trainable low-
rank decomposition matrices into specific layers. This approach
significantly reduces the number of trainable parameters, mak-
ing it more computationally efficient without sacrificing per-
formance. Specifically, for a pre-trained weight matrix W0 ∈
Rd×k, LoRA introduces two trainable matrices A ∈Rd×r and
B ∈Rr×k, where r is the adaptation rank and being much
smaller than both d and k. Instead of directly fine-tuning W0,
the model learns the update ∆W as follows:
W = W0 + ∆W = W0 + A × B
(1)
2.2. Multitask memory LoRA
In prior methods for downstream task fine-tuning, a task-
specific LoRA matrix is appended to each layer of a large lan-
guage model. Although this method is effective for individ-
ual downstream tasks, employing a shared LoRA matrix across
all tasks can limit task interaction and exacerbate conflicts be-
tween tasks during multitask inference. This limitation reduces
the ability of model to leverage cross-task information and ad-
dress task-specific conflicts, thereby impeding overall multi-
task performance. To simultaneously handle multiple tasks and
enhance the performance of subtasks, we propose a multitask
memory parameter-efficient fine-tuning method for multimodal
SER, which is designed to improve multitask fine-tuning capa-
bilities. The proposed MMLoRA is illustrated in Figure 1.
The MMLoRA framework introduces several pivotal com-
ponents: a universal LoRA expert, routing networks, a task-
specific mixture of LoRA experts, and task memory units, with
the trainable elements being the routing networks and the LoRA
experts. It should be noted that different tasks have their own
task-specific memories, and multiple task objectives achieve
cross-layer information transmission through their respective
memory units. Consider the main task as an example: in this
case, the memory LoRA of the current layer receives the hid-
den state from the general output of the previous layer, which is
then processed through the task-specific mixture of LoRA ex-
perts. Finally, the main task memory unit in the current layer
is updated by integrating the main task memory from the previ-
ous layer, general task-related information, and the task-specific
details of the current layer.
Specifically, the input token xi is first processed by the rout-
ing network R(·), which is a linear layer that computes the
probability distribution PN of assigning the input token xi to

3. Experiments
3.1. Dataset
The dataset used for our experiments is the IEMOCAP [18],
a widely recognized dataset for speech emotion recognition.
We utilized a subset of IEMOCAP, comprising 5,531 utterances
categorized into four emotion classes: angry, happy (with ex-
cited relabeled as happy), sad, and neutral. The experiments
were conducted using a leave-one-session-out cross-validation
approach.
3.2. Multimodal large language model
Video-LLaMA is an open-source multi-modal framework that
enables LLM to simultaneously process both the visual and au-
ditory content and engage in conversation with humans. In this
paper, we use a modified version of Video-LLaMA, with the
visual branch removed and utilize the audio-language branch of
Video-LLaMA, which includes a pre-trained ImageBind [19] as
the audio encoder. The process begins by converting the audio
clips into spectrograms using Mel-spectrogram bins. The audio
encoder then maps each spectrogram into a dense vector. This
branch also features a positional embedding layer to inject tem-
poral information, an audio Q-former that fuses features from
different audio segments, and a linear layer that maps the audio
representations into the embedding space of LLMs. The version
of Video-LLaMA used in our experiments is Video-LLaMA-2-
13B-Pretrained.
3.3. Experimental Setup
During the training phase, the LLaMA2 component of VideoL-
LaMA is kept frozen, and only the parameters of the MMLoRA-
related modules and the audio adapter are optimized using
4950

AdamW [20] with a learning rate of 3e-4. The model is trained
for 10 epochs. Regarding the hyperparameter settings of MM-
LoRA, the task-specific mixture of LoRA experts includes 8 ex-
perts, with the top-K routing value set to 2. Experiments were
conducted on a system equipped with NVIDIA A800 80GB
GPUs. For evaluation, we used the widely adopted weighted
accuracy (WA) and unweighted accuracy (UA) metrics for the
IEMOCAP dataset.
3.4. Experiment Results and Analysis
To verify the effects of the proposed MMLora method, we con-
duct ablation experiments. First, we evaluated the multimodal
emotion understanding capabilities of Video-LLaMA, and con-
ducted experiments using both zero-shot and few-shot inference
methods. In these experiments, we adopted more stringent in-
struction settings to ensure that the model could produce the ex-
pected results. For instance, when evaluating on the IEMOCAP
dataset, we specified instructions such as: ”Given the context
the context, audio tokens, and utterance, detect the emotion of
the current speaker. The emotion must be one of the following:
[’neutral’, ’happy’, ’anger’, ’sad’]. The output must be format-
ted exactly as follows: [one emotion].”. For the few-shot exper-
iment, we provided three examples to guide the model. These
examples helped the model understand the output format and
improve its emotional classification by showing how different
emotions are expressed in various contexts. The results of the
zero-shot and few-shot experiments are shown in Table 1. The
few-shot performance on the IEMOCAP dataset improved by
over 10% compared to the zero-shot setting, demonstrating the
effectiveness of example guidance in improving emotion clas-
sification accuracy.
The proposed MMLoRA is a parameter-efficient method
designed for multitask learning, specifically aimed at enhancing
the performance of emotion recognition subtasks. To evaluate
its effectiveness, we compared the downstream emotion recog-
nition performance of vanilla LoRA under the same MLLM
framework. To ensure a fair comparison, the rank parameters
and training hyperparameters of LoRA were kept identical to
those of MMLoRA, with both methods applied to the FFN lay-
ers. As shown in Table 1, the proposed MMLoRA achieves
81.11% WA and 82.13% UA on IEMOCAP, which indicate that
MMLoRA produces a better results based on multitask memory
design.
To validate the effectiveness of the MMLoRA architecture
under the same multi-task conditions, we conducted an abla-
tion study using Multi-task LoRA. Unlike the multi-output of
MMLoRA, this variant employs a single output supervised by
the losses of both tasks simultaneously. This experiment aims to
evaluate the advantages of structures such as memory units over
the vanilla LoRA architecture in leveraging multi-task learning

4. Conclusion
In this paper, we introduced a novel multitask memory
parameter-efficient fine-tuning method designed to enhance
multimodal speech emotion recognition by incorporating gen-
der as an auxiliary task. MMLoRA leverages both shared and
task-specific LoRA experts, as well as a memory mechanism,
to enable efficient multitask learning and improve generaliza-
tion across diverse emotional expressions. The experimental
results on the IEMOCAP dataset demonstrated that MMLoRA
outperforms existing methods, providing a robust and scalable
solution for parameter-efficient fine-tuning in emotion recog-
nition task. These findings underscore the value of the pro-
posed framework and offer valuable insights that may extend to
broader applications of multitask learning within large model
fine-tuning contexts.
