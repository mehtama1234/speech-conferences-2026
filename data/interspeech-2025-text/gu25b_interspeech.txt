Abstract
Large speech foundation models like Whisper face significant
deployment challenges due to their massive storage require-
ments. While post-training quantization (PTQ) offers a prac-
tical compression solution, existing methods suffer severe per-
formance degradation below 8 bits, particularly for transformer-
based architectures with prevalent weight outliers. We propose
an ultra-low bit PTQ framework combining three key innova-
tions: 1) K-means clustering for distribution-aware nonlinear
quantization, 2) Mixed-precision allocation based on column-
wise outlier density, and 3) Selective retention of critical out-
liers in sparse FP32 format. Evaluated on Whisper-Large-V3
(1.5B parameters), our method achieves 2.12-bit quantization
with only 0.17% absolute WER increase on LibriSpeech test-
clean. The approach also maintains whisper’s robust capabil-
ities, showing less than 1% WER degradation across multiple
dataset.
Index Terms: speech recognition, model compression, quanti-

1. Introduction
Large-scale speech models like Whisper[1] and USM[2], with
billions of parameters, have shown remarkable capabilities
across a wide range of speech tasks, consistently delivering im-
pressive performance on diverse domain datasets. However,
their substantial storage requirements pose a significant chal-
lenge to their deployment in practical scenarios. This challenge
has propelled significant research efforts in model compression
techniques to reduce storage footprint while preserving perfor-
mance.
Various compression paradigms have emerged to address
this challenge, each with distinct advantages. Pruning meth-
ods, which remove redundant parameters while maintaining
model architecture, have demonstrated effectiveness through
structured [3] and unstructured approaches [4, 5], while knowl-
edge distillation transfers knowledge from large teacher mod-
els to compact student networks [6]. However, these methods
typically require computationally expensive retraining or archi-
tectural modifications that limit their practicality for massive
models.
Quantization has emerged as particularly promising for
speech models due to its hardware-friendly nature and
architecture-agnostic implementation. This technique reduces
model size by decreasing the precision of numerical repre-
sentations in model parameters. While extensively studied
in computer vision [7, 8] and natural language processing
[9, 10, 11, 12, 13], automatic speech recognition remains rel-

atively unexplored. Most existing work [14, 15, 16, 17] focuses
on Quantization Aware Training (QAT) [8], requiring retrain-
ing on original datasets. Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) of-
fers greater practicality by eliminating retraining needs, yet cur-
rent PTQ methods [18, 19, 20] exhibit significant performance
degradation below 8 bits - still prohibitive for billion-parameter
models. The development of lossless PTQ techniques below 4
bits remains an open frontier in speech technology.
This context underscores the critical need for efficient quan-
tization techniques that maintain performance while dramati-
cally reducing model size. Such advancements would enable
broader deployment across resource-constrained applications
and devices. Motivated by this challenge, we propose a k-
means-based mixed-precision method for ultra-low-bit PTQ of
large-scale speech models. Our contributions include:
1. K-means Quantization Framework: We adapt the k-means
clustering algorithm for speech model quantization, en-
hancing efficiency through intelligent weight clustering and
distribution-aware quantization.
2. Mixed-Precision Allocation Strategy: A novel bit alloca-
tion mechanism that identifies critical features (e.g., outlier-
containing weights) and dynamically assigns higher preci-
sion to preserve model integrity.
3. Ultra-Low Bitrate Validation: Experimental results on
Whisper-Large demonstrate our method achieves 2-bit quan-
tization with < 1% absolute WER increase across multiple
datasets, establishing new state-of-the-art PTQ compression
for speech models while maintaining robustness. The evalu-
ation on other models like Wav2Vec2 demonstrates the broad
applicability of the proposed method.
This work bridges the critical gap between theoretical com-
pression potential and practical deployment requirements for
billion-parameter speech models, enabling new possibilities for
edge deployment and multi-model applications.
2. Related Works
2.1. Post-Training Quantization
Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) has emerged as a crit-
ical paradigm for deploying neural networks in resource-
constrained environments by reducing storage and computa-
tional overhead [21]. The core challenge lies in mapping con-
tinuous floating-point values to discrete integer representations
while preserving model fidelity.
Quantization strategies are broadly categorized by their
mapping granularity: linear quantization employs uniform in-
tervals to partition the value range, enabling hardware-efficient
implementations through fixed-point arithmetic. Seminal works
like Jacob et al. [22] established foundational linear quantiza-

2.2. Whisper Architecture and Quantization Challenges
The Whisper architecture [1], a transformer-based model pre-
trained on 680k hours of multilingual audio, achieves state-of-
the-art speech recognition through massive scale (up to 1.55B
parameters). However, its heavy reliance on attention mecha-
nisms introduces distinctive weight distribution characteristics,
including magnitude outliers concentrated in specific attention
heads [24]. These outliers create significant quantization bot-
tlenecks—conventional PTQ methods suffer severe accuracy
drops below 4 bits due to insufficient resolution for critical pa-
rameters [25], motivating specialized solutions for ultra-low-bit
speech model compression.
3. Methods
To address the challenges posed by limited range represented
by low-bit and outliers in Whisper during quantization, we
adopted several methods, including K-Means based quantiza-
tion, mixed-precision allocation, and selective outlier retention.
These approaches help ensure that the quantized weights more
closely align with the original full-precision weights, thereby
reducing the performance loss caused by quantization. This sec-
tion will provide a detailed overview of these methods.

3.3. Mixed Precision Allocation

model’s weights varies significantly. Some groups are more
clustered and can be adequately represented with only a few
centroids, while others are more dispersed and require a larger
number of centroids. To ensure that the quantized weights more
accurately reflect the original weights, different numbers of
centroids—i.e., different levels of quantization precision—are
needed to be allocated to each group.

3.4. Selective Outlier Retention
To further reduce the disruption caused by outliers when de-
termining centroids during weight quantization, and to prevent
performance degradation after outliers are quantized, we selec-
tively retain certain outliers. These outliers are excluded from
the quantization process and do not participate in the K-means
clustering of their respective groups. Instead, they are preserved
in a sparse, full-precision matrix format, which is then com-
bined with the dense quantized weights.

4. Experiment
4.1. Experimental Setup
Models The experiments are conducted on Whisper-Large-V3,
one of the best open-source speech models available, with 1.5
billion parameters. Model’s weight is downloaded from Hug-
gingface.
Data To demonstrate the capability and robustness of the quan-
tized model, we selected a variety of datasets as test sets. Fol-
lowing the dataset configuration used in the Whisper paper, we
chose several datasets for evaluating English speech recogni-
tion: LibriSpeech test-clean and test-other, TED-LIUM 3, Com-
monV oice 5.1, V oxPopuli.en, and FLEURS.en. For multilin-
gual speech recognition, we selected the French, German, Span-
ish, and Italian datasets from Multilingual LibriSpeech. These
datasets collectively provide a comprehensive evaluation frame-
work across both English and multiple languages.
Quantization After determining the quantization centroids us-
ing K-Means, we applied the GPTQ strategy to update the
remaining parameters. The calibration set used for this pro-
cess consisted of 2,048 randomly sampled entries from the
LibriSpeech train-clean-500 dataset. The quantization of the
Whisper-Large-V3 model was performed using an RTX 4090
GPU, and the entire process took approximately three hours.
4.2. Main Results
Our framework establishes new benchmarks for speech model
compression across multiple bitwidths, as detailed in Table 1.
At 4-bit uniform quantization, the method achieves near-
lossless performance with 2.04% WER on LibriSpeech test-
clean (vs 2.02% FP32), matching GPTQ’s accuracy while using
8× fewer bits. The advantages amplify at lower precisions: 3-
bit quantization maintains 2.05% WER (+0.03% absolute) com-
pared to GPTQ’s degraded 2.18%, demonstrating superior dis-
tribution alignment through K-means clustering.
The critical divergence occurs at 2-bit operation, where our
method sustains functional recognition (14.04% WER) versus
GPTQ’s catastrophic failure (51.13%). This 3.6× relative im-
provement stems from adaptive handling of weight outliers -
particularly evident in challenging datasets like Common V oice
(40.71% vs 210.94% WER). Progressive precision reduction re-
veals graceful degradation: 4-bit → 3-bit → 2-bit transitions
incur 0.02% → 0.03% → 12.02% absolute WER increases on
LibriSpeech clean, compared to GPTQ’s unstable 0.01% →
0.15% → 49.09% collapse.
Crucially, our method preserves Whisper’s cross-dataset ro-
bustness at all bitwidths. The 2.12-bit configuration achieves
≤ 1% absolute WER degradation on clean read speech (Lib-
riSpeech: 2.19% vs 2.02%), spontaneous talks (TED-LIUM:
3.67% vs 3.88%), and noisy recordings (V oxPopuli: 7.93%
vs 7.32%). Even on accented Common V oice data – where
GPTQ fails completely at 2 bits – our approach maintains us-
able 11.92% WER versus 8.56% FP32 baseline. This consis-
tency across five distinct English speech domains (read, sponta-
neous, accented, noisy, educational) confirms that our quantiza-
tion strategy retains Whisper’s core capability to handle diverse
acoustic conditions.
Enabling mixed-precision (2.1-bit) and outlier retention
(2.12-bit) further enhances robustness, recovering near-original
performance while maintaining 98.3% model compression. The
hierarchical precision allocation – 4-bit for outlier-dense atten-
tion heads, 2-bit for clustered feedforward weights – proves es-
sential for preserving both accuracy and Whisper’s signature
cross-domain reliability.

4.3. Ablation Study
To isolate the contributions of each proposed component in
our framework, we conduct a comprehensive ablation study
on Whisper-Large-V3. Specifically, we analyze the impact of
(1) K-Means based quantization, (2) mixed-precision alloca-
tion, and (3) selective outlier retention on quantization perfor-
mance. We evaluate these ablations across multiple bitwidths
and benchmark them on the LibriSpeech test-clean dataset.
The performance trends observed are consistent across other
datasets as well.
4.3.1. Impact of K-Means Based Quantization
The effectiveness of K-Means clustering for weight quantiza-
tion is demonstrated by comparing our approach to a standard
uniform GPTQ quantization baseline. Table 1 shows that re-
moving K-Means clustering significantly degrades performance
at lower bitwidths. For instance, at 2-bit quantization, uniform
GPTQ quantization leads to a WER of 51.13%, whereas our
K-Means-based approach improves it to 14.04%.
4.3.2. Effect of Mixed-Precision Allocation
We analyze the role of mixed-precision allocation by compar-
ing standard fixed-bit quantization to our adaptive bit allocation
strategy. As shown in Table 1, enabling mixed-precision allo-
cation (e.g., 2.1-bit quantization) reduces WER from 14.04%
to 6.04%, by selectively assigning higher bitwidths to outlier-
heavy layers. This demonstrates the effectiveness of precision-
aware weight allocation, particularly in low-bit quantization
regimes, where uniform assignment struggles to capture outlier
distributions.
4.3.3. Benefit of Selective Outlier Retention
To quantify the impact of selective outlier retention, we com-
pare models with and without the sparse FP32 outlier preser-
vation mechanism. Results in Table 1 indicate that incor-
porating outlier retention further refines model accuracy. At
2.12-bit quantization, enabling outlier retention reduces WER
from 6.04% to 2.19%, recovering over 60% of the original
full-precision model’s accuracy. This highlights the neces-
sity of outlier-aware quantization techniques, particularly for
transformer-based speech models where weight outliers signif-
icantly influence performance.
4.4. Generalization to SSL Speech Models
The effectiveness of our method extends to self-supervised
learning architectures as demonstrated by Wav2Vec2 [26] eval-
uations in Table 2. At 4-bit uniform quantization, the model
achieves near-original performance with 6.37% WER versus
6.11% FP32 baseline. Progressive bit reduction maintains func-
tional recognition down to 2-bit quantization (22.53% WER),
though with expected accuracy degradation typical of ultra-low
precision.

5. Conclusion
We present a novel post-training quantization framework that
enables ultra-low-bit compression of large speech models
through K-means clustering, mixed-precision allocation, and
selective outlier retention. Our method achieves lossless 2.12-
bit quantization of Whisper-Large-V3, reducing model size by
98.3% while maintaining less than 1% WER degradation across
diverse datasets. The approach significantly outperforms linear
quantization baselines, particularly below 4 bits, and demon-
strates generalizability to SSL architectures like Wav2Vec2.0.
Future work will focus on dynamic bit allocation based on ut-
terance complexity, hardware-aware optimization for edge de-
vices, and integration with pruning techniques for compounded
compression effects. This advancement opens new possibili-
ties for deploying billion-parameter speech models in resource-
constrained environments without compromising recognition
accuracy.
