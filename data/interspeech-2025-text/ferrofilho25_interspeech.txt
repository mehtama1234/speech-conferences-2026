Evaluating Deep Speaker Embedding Robustness to Domain, Sampling Rate,
and Codec Variations
Alexandre Ferro Filho1, Diogo Fernandes Costa Silva1, Pedro Elias Engelberg Silva Borges1,
Arlindo Rodrigues Galv˜ao Filho1
1AKCIT, Federal University of Goi´as, Brazil
diiogofernands@gmail.com
Abstract
Speaker verification systems based on deep speaker embed-
dings perform well under matched training and evaluation
conditions, but performance degrades under domain shifts.
This work evaluates the sensitivity of four speaker embed-
ding models—ECAPA-TDNN, TitaNet, ECAPA2, and ReD-
imNet—to variations in acoustic domains, sampling rates, and
audio codecs. Experiments on datasets with far-field speech,
noise, and music interference demonstrate that all models de-
grade under mismatched conditions. ReDimNet demonstrates
the smallest performance degradation compared to the other
models but is still affected in certain cases. Downsampling and
low-bitrate compression further degrade performance, revealing
the reliance of models on high-frequency information and their
sensitivity to compression artifacts.
Index Terms: speaker verification, speech recognition, compu-
tational paralinguistics
1. Introduction
Automatic Speaker Verification (ASV) systems are deployed
in biometric authentication, access control, and voice assis-
tants, requiring reliable performance across diverse environ-
ments.
While deep speaker embedding models, such as x-
vector [1], ECAPA-TDNN [2], TitaNet [3], and ReDimNet [4],
achieve high accuracy under matched training and evaluation
conditions, their performance degrades under domain shifts.
Variations in channel characteristics, codecs, sampling
rates, background noise, and microphones introduce shifts that
significantly reduce ASV accuracy. Models trained on clean,
wideband datasets like VoxCeleb [5] often fail in narrowband,
noisy, or far-field conditions [6]. Approaches such as domain
adversarial training [7], and data augmentation [8, 1] improve
robustness but remain limited when facing unseen domains or
extreme distortions [9, 10].
Recent datasets expose these weaknesses. CN-Celeb [11]
captures multi-genre, real-world speech with noise; DIPCo [9]
simulates far-field meeting scenarios with reverberation and
overlapping speakers; JuKeBOX [12] introduces low-quality
speech mixed with music. Although these datasets highlight
performance gaps, the extent to which state-of-the-art models
degrade across domain shifts, sampling rates, and codec varia-
tions remains underexplored.
This work investigates the sensitivity of deep speaker em-
beddings to domain shifts. We evaluate ECAPA-TDNN, Ti-
taNet, ECAPA2, and ReDimNet across far-field (DiPCo), multi-
genre (CN-Celeb), noisy and reverberant (VOiCES), and low-
quality music-mixed speech (JuKeBOX). Our analysis reveals
performance degradation across domains and highlights the
need for more robust embedding learning strategies.

2. Related Works
Speaker verification systems have advanced through improve-
ments in deep speaker embedding architectures, domain adap-
tation techniques, and evaluation across diverse acoustic condi-
tions. The x-vector model [1] introduced TDNN-based embed-
dings, while ECAPA-TDNN [2] incorporated channel attention,

chitectures, such as ECAPA2 [13] and TitaNet [3], further en-
hanced embedding extraction with squeeze-excitation and at-
tentive pooling. ReDimNet [4] proposed a more efficient design
optimized for both clean and noisy data. These models achieve
competitive performance in matched conditions, but their ro-
bustness degrades under domain shifts, indicating that architec-
tural refinements alone are insufficient.
To address this, recent works have explored leveraging
pretrained speech models and domain adaptation strategies.
Whisper-SV [14] adapts the Whisper ASR model for speaker
verification using multi-layer aggregation and attention, im-
proving performance in low-resource settings. Transfer learn-
ing and knowledge distillation from ASR-trained Conformers
[15] reduce EER in VoxCeleb evaluations. Multi-Level Domain
Adapters [6] introduce domain-specific modules that encode
domain information into embeddings, achieving up to 21.1%
EER reduction on the 3D-Speaker dataset. While these meth-
ods improve robustness, they often rely on domain labels, large-
scale pretraining, or target-domain adaptation, limiting their ap-
plicability across unseen domains.
Despite these advances, domain mismatch remains a funda-
mental challenge. Models trained on clean, wideband datasets
like VoxCeleb [5] degrade in narrowband, noisy, or far-field
conditions. Early work explored domain adversarial training [7]
and data augmentation [8], but their effectiveness diminishes
under severe acoustic shifts [7, 8]. Evaluation datasets such
as CN-Celeb [11], DiPCo [9], VOiCES [16], and JuKeBOX
[12] expose these limitations, highlighting the performance gap
across diverse domains. However, prior work has not systemat-
ically isolated the combined impact of sampling rates, codecs,
and channel characteristics on embedding robustness. Our work
fills this gap by quantifying the performance degradation under
these factors, offering a more comprehensive assessment of em-
bedding resilience.
3. Experimental Setup
3.1. Datasets
We evaluate model performance using four datasets selected
to expose the sensitivity of speaker embeddings to diverse do-
main shifts. CN-Celeb [11] contains multi-genre speech with
varied recording conditions and noise levels, reflecting channel
and content variability typical of media broadcasts. VOiCES
[16] comprises far-field speech with noise and reverberation, as-
sessing robustness to distance and acoustic interference. DiPCo
[9] features multi-party conversations with speaker overlap and
room reverberation, representing meeting scenarios with di-
arization challenges. JuKeBOX [12] consists of speech mixed
with music and compression artifacts, testing resilience to non-
speech interference and low-quality signals. These datasets col-
lectively cover critical domain variations, enabling a detailed
analysis of embedding degradation across conditions encoun-
tered in real-world applications.
3.2. Metrics
We evaluate speaker verification performance using Equal Error
Rate (EER) and minimum Detection Cost Function (minDCF),
two principal metrics in the field. EER represents the threshold
at which the False Acceptance Rate (FAR) equals the False Re-
jection Rate (FRR), providing a threshold-independent assess-
ment. minDCF considers application-specific costs, weighting
missed detections and false acceptances based on prior proba-
bilities.

3.3. Models
We evaluate four speaker embedding models commonly
adopted in speaker verification:
ECAPA-TDNN, TitaNet,
ECAPA2, and ReDimNet. These models incorporate different
architectural choices aimed at improving robustness and dis-
criminability in embedding extraction. Table 1 summarizes the
domains and augmentation techniques applied during training.
ECAPA-TDNN [2] builds upon the x-vector architecture,
introducing multi-scale feature aggregation, squeeze-excitation
blocks, and channel-wise attention to enhance speaker represen-
tation. ECAPA2 [13] refines this design with additional modifi-
cations to the network structure, maintaining the same training
data and augmentation strategies, which include noise, music,
babble, reverb, speed perturbation, and compression applied to
wideband, interview-style speech.
TitaNet [3] also derives from the TDNN framework but in-
troduces residual connections and attention mechanisms, focus-
ing on scalability and efficiency. Unlike the ECAPA models,
TitaNet was trained on a broader range of domains, including
telephone and read speech, with data augmentation involving
reverb, speed perturbation, and time/frequency masking.
ReDimNet [4] diverges by prioritizing robustness to do-
main shifts. It employs regularization techniques and discrimi-
native training objectives to mitigate the impact of channel vari-
ability and noise. Its training data aligns more closely with
ECAPA models, consisting of wideband, interview-style speech
augmented with noise, music, babble, reverb, and speed pertur-
bation.
4. Methodology
We investigate the domain sensitivity of deep speaker em-
beddings by evaluating the performance of ECAPA-TDNN,
ECAPA2, TitaNet, and ReDimNet under variations in acous-
tic domains, codecs, and sampling rates. The evaluation fol-
lows the speaker verification pipeline shown in Figure 1, where
audio samples are processed by a model to extract speaker em-
beddings, and cosine similarity is computed between pairs to
determine whether they belong to the same speaker. A balanced
set of test trials was created for each evaluation dataset, ensuring
an equal number of same-speaker and different-speaker pairs.
We select evaluation datasets that represent domains dis-
tinct from those used in model training to expose performance
degradation under domain shifts.

5. Results and Discussions
5.1. Domain Sensitivity Across Datasets
Our evaluation shows that all tested models (ECAPA-TDNN,
TitaNet, ECAPA2, and ReDimNet) experience performance
degradation when evaluated on datasets representing domain
shifts. As shown in Table 2, models trained on clean, wide-
band data achieve lower EER on CN-Celeb compared to DiPCo,
VOiCES, and JuKeBOX. This degradation is more pronounced
in datasets characterized by far-field conditions, background
noise, or music interference, indicating that current speaker em-
beddings overfit to training data, particularly clean, wideband
speech.
Performance on VOiCES highlights the sensitivity of em-
beddings to noise and reverberation, as the dataset includes
noise augmentation and multi-microphone setups to emulate
real-world acoustic variability.
Models with poor results on
VOiCES are likely to struggle in practical applications involv-
ing distant-talking scenarios and non-ideal environments.
A
similar pattern is observed in DiPCo, which introduces conver-
sational overlap and room effects, and JuKeBOX, which repre-
sents an extreme domain shift with speech mixed into music.
Among the models, ReDimNet achieves the lowest EER
across most domain-shifted datasets, aligning with its focus on
improving robustness to domain variability. ECAPA2 outper-
forms ECAPA-TDNN in noisy and far-field conditions, such
as VOiCES and DIPCo, suggesting greater stability to acous-
tic variations. ECAPA-TDNN exhibits substantial degradation
under these domain shifts, while TitaNet consistently yields the
highest EER, indicating that its training regime and architec-
ture are less effective in mismatched conditions. These results
reinforce that architectural refinements alone are insufficient to
ensure generalization, and embeddings remain vulnerable to re-
alistic domain shifts.

5.2. Sampling Rate Variation
Performance degradation is observed across all models when
reducing the sampling rate from 16kHz to 8kHz, as shown
in Table 3.
ReDimNet and ECAPA2 exhibit greater robust-
ness, maintaining lower EER under both conditions. Although
ECAPA-TDNN shows high absolute EER at 8kHz, its rela-
tive degradation is lower (57%) compared to TitaNet (102%),
indicating that TitaNet is more sensitive to bandwidth reduc-
tion. These findings are consistent with prior work indicating
that speaker embeddings depend on high-frequency informa-
tion. The results emphasize that even state-of-the-art models are
vulnerable to sampling rate limitations, which is critical in ap-
plications involving narrowband audio, such as telephone-based
verification systems.

5.3. Codec Variation
Experiments with codecs reveal that all models degrade when
audio is encoded using lossy or companded compression for-
mats, as shown in Table 4.
ReDimNet achieves the lowest
EER across all evaluated codecs (WAV, MP3, FLAC, and u-
law), suggesting greater robustness to compression artifacts.
ECAPA2 is less affected than ECAPA-TDNN and TitaNet but is
consistently outperformed by ReDimNet. ECAPA-TDNN ex-
hibits the highest degradation, particularly in MP3 and u-law,
indicating greater sensitivity to low-bitrate artifacts. TitaNet
shows intermediate performance but also degrades under MP3
compression.
Although FLAC is a lossless codec, the source audio files
were previously encoded using AAC before being transcoded to
the tested formats. This pre-processing step likely explains why
FLAC performance does not match expectations for truly loss-
less audio. These results reinforce that compression artifacts
reduce the discriminative power of embeddings, especially in
models more reliant on clean training data, and highlight that
evaluation with seemingly lossless formats can still reflect en-
coding dependencies from upstream processing.
5.4. Score Distribution Analysis
To further analyze the behavior of the best-performing model,
ReDimNet, we plot the cosine similarity score distributions
for same-speaker and different-speaker pairs across evaluation
datasets. Figure 2 shows that in challenging scenarios such as
JuKeBOX and CN-Celeb, the distributions present greater over-
lap, aligning with the higher EERs observed. In contrast, DiPCo
displays more separable distributions, corresponding to lower
EERs.
However, VOiCES reveals an additional source of degra-
dation: although the distributions may visually suggest sepa-
rability, the application of multiple noise augmentation tech-
niques and distractors introduces intra-speaker variability, caus-
ing samples from the same speaker to sound perceptibly differ-
ent. This leads to increased false rejections, as the model fre-
quently misclassifies same-speaker pairs as different speakers.
Visual inspection further indicates that music interference
in JuKeBOX often results in false acceptances, while reverber-
ation and background noise in DiPCo primarily increase false
rejections. This analysis complements the quantitative evalua-
tion, reinforcing that domain conditions affect both the separa-
bility of embeddings and the consistency of same-speaker rep-
resentations, particularly in noise-augmented datasets such as
VOiCES.
6. Conclusion
This work investigated the sensitivity of deep speaker embed-
dings to domain shifts, sampling rate variations, and codec com-
pression in speaker verification systems. We evaluated ECAPA-
TDNN, ECAPA2, TitaNet, and ReDimNet across diverse eval-
uation conditions and observed performance degradation across
all models when exposed to mismatched domains.
ReDim-
Net demonstrated the lowest degradation percentage, indicating
greater robustness, but it also exhibited limitations under severe
shifts such as speech mixed with music.
Our results show that variations in sampling rates and
codecs further degrade performance, reinforcing that speaker
embeddings depend on high-frequency content and are vulner-
able to compression artifacts.
Even the most robust model,
ReDimNet, experienced consistent but less severe degradation
compared to others, suggesting that current embedding models
remain susceptible to real-world audio variations despite archi-
tectural advancements.
These findings indicate that existing training paradigms re-
lying on clean, wideband, and augmented data are insufficient
for generalization to realistic conditions. Developing domain-
invariant embedding models and adaptive training techniques
that explicitly account for channel, noise, and sampling vari-
ability remains an open challenge. Addressing this sensitivity
is critical to ensure reliable speaker verification performance in
practical deployment scenarios involving heterogeneous audio
environments.
