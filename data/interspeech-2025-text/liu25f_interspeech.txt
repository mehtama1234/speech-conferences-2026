Abstract 
Multi-Task Learning (MTL) is widely used in automatic 
stuttering detection to identify stuttering symptoms; however, 
task conflicts can hinder performance. This paper addresses the 
task conflict issue in MTL-based stuttering detection and 
proposes a rule-based MTL strategy and a Multi-Mixture-of-
Experts (MMoE) MTL framework to alleviate these conflicts. 
We analyze the inherent conflicts in stuttering detection tasks 
and develop a rule-based MTL strategy to mitigate them. 
Additionally, we introduce an MoE-based adaptive multi-task 
strategy to optimize task allocation. Experimental results show 
that our approach outperforms current state-of-the-art methods. 
In the 2024 SLT Stuttering Speech Challenge, the rule-based 
MTL strategy achieved a 19.9% increase in average F1 score 
over the baseline, securing first place. The MMoE-MTL 
strategy further enhanced task collaboration, improving the 
average F1 score by 7.55%, demonstrating its effectiveness. 
Index Terms: sturrering, MTL, MMoE 
1. Introduction 
Stuttering is a common speech disorder that affects 
approximately 70 million people globally, representing about 1% 
of the total population [1], [2]. For people who stutter (PWS), 
stuttering not only impacts their social functioning but may also 
have negative effects on their mental health. With the 
advancement of communication technologies, these impacts 
may be further exacerbated. Therefore, the realization of 
automated stuttering detection holds significant practical value. 
Stuttering symptoms typically manifest as the prolongation, 
pausing, and repetition of syllables, words, or phrases [3]. 
These symptoms exhibit both commonalities and differences: 
from a pathological perspective, they may stem from similar 
neural mechanisms and often co-occur clinically, showing 
strong correlations. However, from an acoustic signal 
perspective, they exhibit significant variations in temporal 
characteristics. Existing research on automated stuttering 
detection and classification can be broadly categorized into two 
approaches: one that focuses on the differences between 
symptoms, treating stuttering detection as a multi-class single-
task problem, and another that focuses on the correlations 
among symptoms, treating stuttering detection as an MTL 
problem. 
Despite the extensive research on stuttering detection, 
accurately detecting multiple stuttering tasks remains a 
challenge due to the conflicting relationship between their 
correlations and differences. On one hand, most studies focus 
solely on the differences between stuttering tasks, overlooking 
the correlations among them. However, these correlation 
insights are crucial for stuttering detection, especially in data-
scarce scenarios. On the other hand, while some studies have 
considered the correlations between tasks to improve detection 
performance, they often neglect the potential conflicts among 
tasks. In MTL for stuttering detection, there are both shared and 
conflicting components between tasks. Shared components 
include temporal dependencies, pronunciation abnormalities, 
and phoneme-level features, while conflicting components may 
involve time scales and signal characteristics. When sub-tasks 
may be unrelated or even contradictory, the overall 
effectiveness of MTL can be compromised. 
To address this, we propose a novel stuttering event 
detection method aimed at balancing the shared and conflicting 
components within stuttering MTL. The model first 
investigates the relationships between tasks in stuttering MTL 
and then designs a rule-based MTL strategy based on these 
relationships. Additionally, we introduce an MMoE-based 
automated MTL strategy. Experimental results demonstrate 
that our approach outperforms existing state-of-the-art 
techniques in stuttering detection tasks. 
2. Related Work 
Most studies have approached stuttering detection as either a 
multi-class classification problem or a binary classification task 
for various speech disfluencies. Early works, such as Esmaili et 
al. [4], used Support Vector Machines (SVM) with Perceptual 
Linear Prediction (PLP) features to detect speech prolongations. 
Over time, research has shifted towards more complex models 
[5], [6], [7], [8], [9], [10], [11]. Long Short-Term Memory 
(LSTM) networks, in particular, have gained attention for their 
ability to capture long-term dependencies and contextual 
features in speech. Gupta et al. [6] combined weighted Mel-

the success of advanced neural network architectures in 
stuttering detection. In recent years, models leveraging pre-

3. Method 
3.1. Proposed model 
In this study, we use a 12-layer Conformer encoder [19] as the 
baseline model. The baseline model adopts an MTL  framework 
with five labels, where the head consists of a single-layer 
BiLSTM network. The specific architecture is illustrated in 
Figure 1(a). To further optimize model performance, we 
propose a rule-based MTL approach, in which tasks /p, /wr, and 
/i are treated as objectives for MTL, while tasks r and b are 
trained as independent tasks. The corresponding structure is 
shown in Figure 1(b). Additionally, we design an MTL model 
based on a Mixture of Multiple Experts (MMoE) framework, 
which includes five expert networks: a linear layer, a single-
layer BiLSTM, a two-layer BiLSTM, a single-layer 
Transformer, and a two-layer Transformer. These expert 
networks are integrated through a multi-task learning 
mechanism. 
3.2. Multi-Task Learning

key challenge is overfitting and insufficient generalization 
ability [20]. Overfitting is particularly severe in resource-

address this issue, multi-task learning (MTL) is an effective 
regularization method. By introducing an additional auxiliary 
task that is distinct yet related to the primary task, MTL

employ an MTL framework with five tasks to solve the

the model includes a shared network, a Transformer denoted as 
f, and a head network hk for each task, where k∈K. After the 
data is processed through the shared network, it is passed to

4. Experimental Setup 
4.1. Dataset 
In this paper, the proposed method was evaluated using the AS-
70 dataset[21]. The dataset comprises recordings of adults who 
stutter (AWS) and native Mandarin speakers. A total of 70 
native Mandarin-speaking AWS participated in the recording 
sessions, including 24 females. Each participant underwent an 
hour-long recording session, consisting of two parts: dialogues 
and reading speech commands. The annotation guidelines 
specify five types of stuttering, including: 
/wr: Word/phrase repetition, used to mark the entire 
repetition of a character or phrase. 
/b: Block, indicating a pause due to breath or stuttering. 
/p: Prolongation, referring to elongated phonemes. 
/r: Sound repetition, indicating repeated phonemes that do 
not constitute a complete character. 
/i: Interjection, representing filler characters caused by 
stuttering, such as 'uh', 'um', or 'er'. 
4.2. Experimental Setup 
The model is built using the Wenet[22] library for feature 
extraction and audio processing. We used the Adam optimizer 
with a learning rate of 0.0001, combined with weighted entropy, 
focal loss, and an early stopping strategy with 10 patience 
epochs for model training. In this study, we followed the dataset 
split method used in the SLT2024 competition to divide the 
training, validation, and test sets, as shown in Table 1.

5. Results Analysis 
This section provides a detailed analysis of the experimental 
results for the proposed stuttering event detection model, 
focusing on the following aspects: first, an ablation study to 
establish a stable and reliable baseline; second, an analysis of 
task conflicts in MTL and a comparison of performance 
differences among various MTL strategies; and finally, a 
comprehensive comparison between the proposed method and 
existing approaches. 
5.1. Baseline 
To establish a reliable benchmark, this study optimized the 
baseline model in three key areas: 1) incorporating a pre-trained 
ASR model, 2) testing different shared bottom layer 
configurations, and 3) experimenting with various head-layer 
structures. Table 2 presents the impact of different shared 
network and head-layer configurations on stuttering task 
performance. The shared configurations involve varying the 
number of Conformer layers (3, 6, 12, and 15) and using a pre-
trained ASR model, while the head-layer configurations 
explore different BiLSTM layer counts (1, 2, and 3). The results 
show: 1) the ASR pre-trained model significantly outperforms 
the non-pre-trained version, 2) the 12-layer Conformer model 
performs best than other layers, and 3) a single-layer BiLSTM 
surpasses multi-layer BiLSTM configurations. Based on these 
findings, the study adopts the Conformer (12-layer) - BiLSTM 
(1-layer) - MTL architecture as the baseline for further 
experiments.

5.2. Multi-Task Learning Strategies 
5.2.1. Task Conflict Similarity Analysis 
 
Figure 2: Heatmap of the inter-task gradient cosine similarity 
using the Conformer-BILSTM-MTL framework. 
In the MTL framework, low correlation between tasks can lead 
to interference among shared parameters, thereby affecting 
model performance. Particularly in the stuttering detection task, 
there are significant differences in the time scales of different 
tasks. To verify whether this inconsistency triggers multi-task 
800

conflicts, this study quantifies the degree of task conflict by 
calculating the gradient descent cosine similarity. The 
experimental results are shown in Figure 2. Analysis indicates 
that there are indeed significant gradient conflicts between tasks: 
specifically, the /b and /r tasks exhibit strong conflicts with all 
other tasks, while the /p, /i, and /wr tasks show relatively 
smaller conflicts. This finding provides important evidence for 
the subsequent design of targeted multi-task optimization 
strategies. 
5.2.2. Multi-task strategy comparison 
To mitigate the negative impact of task conflicts on model 
performance in MTL, this study proposes two innovative 
solutions: a rule-based MTL strategy and an MTL method 
based on Multi-Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE). Table 3 compares 
the performance of four training strategies: (1) single-task 
independent training (No-MTL); (2) original multi-task 
learning (Origin-MTL); (3) rule-based MTL strategy (Rule-
MTL); and (4) MMoE-based MTL method (MMoE-MTL). The 
rule-based MTL strategy employs a grouped training scheme, 
where the /p, /wr, and /i tasks form a three-task MTL, while the 
/b and /r tasks are trained as independent models.

The experimental results indicate that: First, comparing No-
MTL and Origin-MTL, the original MTL improves the 
performance of the /p, /i, and /wr tasks, but significantly reduces 
the performance of the /b and /r tasks, confirming the presence 
of task conflicts. Second, comparing Origin-MTL with Rule-
MTL shows that grouping /p, /wr, and /i into a three-task MTL 
yields better performance than the five-task MTL, validating 
that these three tasks are more highly correlated. Finally, a 
comparison of the performance between Rule-MTL and 
MMoE-MTL reveals that the MMoE method achieves 
significant improvements in the /b, /r, and /wr tasks, with 
particularly notable performance gains observed in the /b task. 
These results suggest that MMoE not only effectively mitigates 
conflicts arising from other tasks that hinder the /b task, but also 
provides the shared information necessary for the /b task. 
Furthermore, the gradient descent similarity analysis further 
corroborates this conclusion. As shown in Figure 3, compared 
to Origin-MTL in Figure 2, MMoE-MTL exhibits lower 
gradient conflicts, providing strong evidence for the 
effectiveness of the MMoE method. 
5.3. State-of-the-art results 
To comprehensively evaluate the performance advantages of 
the proposed method, this study conducted a systematic 
comparison with existing state-of-the-art methods. The baseline 
[18] refers to the results of a three-layer Conformer applied to 
multi-task learning. T018[18] uses external normal data to 
simulate stuttering data and incorporates the Zipformer 
architecture. T031[18] proposes a fine-grained contrastive 
learning (FGCL) framework and introduces a novel mining 
algorithm to identify simple and easily confused frames in 
audio data. As shown in Table 4, the results demonstrate that 
the proposed method significantly outperforms existing 
approaches in F1 scores for core tasks such as /b/, /p/, /r/, and 
/i/. Furthermore, the F1final score shows an 11.27% 
improvement over the best-performing model, T018[18]. These 
findings strongly validate the effectiveness and superiority of 
the proposed stuttering event detection method.

6. Conclusions 
This study systematically explores the task conflict issue in 
automatic stuttering detection within the multi-task learning 
(MTL) framework and proposes rule-based and Multi-Mixture-
of-Experts (MMoE)-based MTL frameworks to effectively 
alleviate conflicts between tasks. By using the Conformer 
network to extract shared acoustic features and combining the 
rule-based MTL strategy with the MMoE-based adaptive multi-
task strategy, we successfully enhanced the performance of 
stuttering detection. Experimental results show that the 
proposed method achieved significant outcomes in the 2024 
SLT Stuttering Speech Challenge, with the rule-based MTL 
strategy improving the average F1 score on the AS-70 dataset 
by 19.9% compared to the baseline model, and the MMoE-
based strategy further increasing the F1 score by 7.55%. These 
results not only validate the effectiveness of the proposed 
method but also provide new insights for the future application 
of multi-task learning in speech processing. 
Future work will focus on analyzing the deeper 
relationships between tasks in stuttering detection to improve 
interpretability from a theoretical perspective. Additionally, we 
plan to further optimize model performance by integrating 
acoustic signals with semantic information, and explore the 
model's application and scalability on other stuttering datasets.
