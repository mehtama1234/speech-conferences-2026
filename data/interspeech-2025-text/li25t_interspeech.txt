SA-RAS: Speaker-Aware Style Retrieval Augmented Generation for
Expressive Zero-Shot Text-to-Speech Synthesis

Abstract
Zero-shot text-to-speech (TTS) models can clone an un-
seen speaker by reference speech.
Given multiple reference
speeches, Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) shows po-
tential in enhancing synthesis quality.
The existing method
faces two key limitations: semantically-biased retrieval tends
to introduce style mismatch, and the deterministic mapping be-
tween text and speech fails to fully capture the unique style
of the speaker. To solve these, we propose a Speaker-Aware
Retrieval Augmented TTS Synthesis (SA-RAS) model.
It
consists of a two-stage TTS model, Split-TTS and a zero-
shot CLAP. The former embeds style and timbre separately
to obtain a style space for retrieval. The latter optimizes the
correlation between text and style by incorporating speaker
information into the retrieval model.
Experiments prove
that SA-RAS boosts the style expressiveness of synthesized
speech.
Speech samples are available at the following link
https://zuoguaidong.github.io/2025interspeech/.
Index Terms: Zero-shot TTS, retrieval augmentation, con-
trastive learning
1. Introduction
Zero-shot text-to-speech (TTS) is a powerful synthesis model
[1] [2]. It can extract features such as prosody and speaker fea-
tures from reference speech to generate natural speech for un-
seen speakers [3] [4]. In practical applications, it is quite com-
mon to have multiple speeches of the target speaker available as
references. The key to fully leveraging the value of these data
lies in precisely selecting the most fitting reference speech for
TTS according to the target synthesized text. This process is of
great significance for enhancing the style expressiveness of the
synthesized speech [5].
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has achieved re-
markable success in optimizing the generation performance of
large language models (LLMs) [6] [7]. In light of this suc-
cess, RAG has also started to penetrate into audio tasks and is
applied in audio deepfake detection [8], audio captioning [9],
and speech synthesis [10]. Xue et al. [10] used Context-Aware
Contrastive Language-Audio Pretraining (CA-CLAP) as the re-
trieval method to select text and audio prompts. This optimiza-
tion of speech prompts effectively enhanced the synthesis per-
formance of the prompt-based TTS system. However, there is
still a phenomenon of style retrieval bias, which is mainly at-
tributable to the following two causes.
Firstly, both speech and text contain rich semantic informa-
tion, and different audio encoders can extract various features,
such as content, prosody, and timbre [11]. With Contrastive

Language-Audio Pretraining (CLAP) [12] being trained to learn
the correspondence between speech and text for retrieval, the
embeddings of speech and text are mapped into a shared la-
tent space. This embedding-mapping process, in turn, guides
the audio encoder in CLAP to tend to extract semantic features.
However, there are cases of high semantic similarity but low
style similarity. Directly establishing a retrieval relationship in
the semantic space is highly likely to result in retrieving audio
with a mismatched style, as shown in Figure 1(A).
Secondly, there exists a one-to-many relationship between
text and styles. Different speakers may exhibit style differences
when expressing the same text [13]. If retrieval is conducted
based on the deterministic mapping between text and speech, it
becomes impossible to capture and represent the distinct style
characteristics of each individual speaker, let alone those of un-
seen speakers. Consequently, further improving the retrieval
model to adapt to zero-shot TTS is needed.
For the first issue, it is the text embedding that should be
mapped into the style space. Speaking style and speaker timbre
are highly entangled as both of them affect the prosody patterns
of speech. However, timbre, tied to a speaker’s unique phys-
iology, doesn’t relate directly to text-conveyed style and can
disrupt style retrieval. Therefore, it’s necessary to construct a
TTS framework that can split timbre and style, so as to obtain a
style encoder that can extract timbre-independent style feature
for the retrieval model. In regard to the second issue, inspired
by zero-shot [14], we introduce speaker information into the
retrieval model. It serves to direct the model to dynamically ad-
just the retrieval relationship between text and style, addressing
the one-to-many relationship between text and styles caused by
speakers. Moreover, it enables the model to maintain the abil-

To improve the retrieval model, we propose the Zero-shot
Contrastive Language-Audio Pre-training (Zero-shot CLAP)
model. With the aid of speaker embedding, Zero-shot CLAP is
trained to learn the correspondence between texts and styles of
different speakers, extracting appropriate style-related features
from the text for retrieval. By this method, it breaks through the
limitations of deterministic mapping.
Overall, our paper makes the following contributions:
• We propose Zero-shot CLAP and innovatively incorporate
speaker embedding into the retrieval model.
• To achieve a retrieval between text and style, we design a
two-stage Split-TTS, which separates style and non-style fea-
tures, to obtain a retrieval-compatible style encoder.
• We conduct a comprehensive evaluation on the LibriTTS [15]
and ESD [16] datasets, demonstrating that SA-RAS improves
the style expressiveness of zero-shot TTS with multiple ref-
erences.
2. Methodology
The SA-RAS consists of two parts: the style retrieval model
Zero-shot CLAP and the speech generation model Split-TTS.
These two models assist each other. Zero-shot CLAP uses the
style encoder well-trained in Split-TTS to train a semantic style
encoder for style retrieval. Split-TTS, on the other hand, lever-
ages the retrieval ability of Zero-shot CLAP to augment the ex-
pressiveness of the synthesized speech.
2.1. Split-TTS Model
The proposed Split-TTS, a two-stage zero-shot TTS model, is
trained to provide a style encoder and perform speech synthesis
for SA-RAS. Split-TTS is capable of splitting and separately
embedding style and timbre.
An overview of the Split-TTS
framework is presented in Figure 2(A). The first stage is a gen-
eration process from text to non-timbre features (NTF), and the
second stage is from NTF to speech synthesis.
Before training the model, feature extraction is required to
obtain the NTF and timbre features needed for the two-stage
training of Split-TTS, as shown in Figure 2(B). Seed-VC[17],
which has the main architecture of a diffusion transformer and
a length regulator, has powerful voice conversion capability. We
use Seed-VC to process the data: fixing one speech as reference
to extract NTF with a unified timbre. By doing so, the features
are completely stripped of effective timbre information. Since
the second-stage model is based on Seed-VC, we extract timbre
features in Seed-VC for timbre embedding.
The text-to-NTF model is based on VITS [18], which
adopts the architecture of conditional variational autoencoder
(CVAE), significantly enhancing the naturalness of speech gen-
eration. The architectural details are shown in Figure 2(C). We
introduce the GST [19] as the style encoder to extract global
style embedding from the NTF. The extracted global style em-
bedding is then added to the input of the duration predictor, nor-
malizing flow, posterior encoder, and decoder. In the second
stage, we design the NTF-to-speech model based on Seed-VC.
We perform adaptive fine-tuning on the diffusion transformer to
recover speech from timbre features and the NTF generated in
the first stage.

2.3. Inference stage
As shown in Figure 3(B), the application of the SA-RAS in-
cludes three steps: indexing, retrieval, and synthesis.
• Indexing: We utilize the style encoder and timbre encoder
to encode all the available reference speeches into style em-
bedding and timbre features constructing a database. And we
compute the average speaker embedding from all candidate
speeches for CA-CLAP.
• Retrieval: The input text is encoded and fused with the
speaker embedding to generate the retrieval query. The style
embeddings in the database are then projected through the
MLP layer, and similarity scores are calculated between the
query and each embedding to select the top one.
• Synthesis: With the retrieved style embedding and timbre
features from the selected reference speech, Split-TTS gen-
erates the target speech.
3. Experiment
3.1. Datasets
We use the LibriTTS [15] and ESD [16] datasets in the experi-
ment and adjust the sampling rate to 22050Hz.
LibriTTS is a multi-speaker English corpus that contains
approximately 585 hours of speech from over 2300 speakers.
We merge all the training and validation sets of LibriTTS and
carry out a random re-splitting according to speakers. The val-
idation set and the test set contain all the data from 10 and 12
speakers respectively. This data partitioning method is applica-
ble to both the Zero-shot CLAP and Split TTS models.
ESD is an Emotional Speech Database that consists of 350
parallel utterances spoken by 10 native English and 10 native
Chinese speakers and covers 5 emotion categories. We employ
this dataset to evaluate the retrieval space.

3.2. Comparison Experiments
Our work focuses on enhancing the style expressiveness of
zero-shot TTS through retrieval. Thus, we conduct compara-
tive experiments of different retrieval methods on Split–TTS,
including the speech paired with the target text, CA-CLAP [10],
the randomly selected speech, and Zero-shot CLAP.
For CA-Clap, The model and hyperparameters are kept
consistent with those in [10]. Since context is not considered
in the experiment, we remove the context-related modules in
the model for comparison.
We train the first stage of Split-TTS for 100k steps on one
NVIDIA RTX 2080 GPU with a batch size of 32. For both Zero-
shot CLAP and CA-CLAP, 32 epochs of training are performed
with a batch size of 32.
The comparison experiment is conducted on unseen speak-
ers. To ensure there are sufficient samples to support the re-
trieval, apart from the speech that matches the currently synthe-
sized text, all the other speeches are used as samples in the style
database for selection.
3.3. Objective Evaluation
Following the experiment of CA-CLAP, We utilize three met-
rics including energy, F0, and mel-cepstral distortion (MCD)
aligned by Dynamic Time Warping (DTW). We also evalu-
ate the Emotion Cosine Similarity (EECS) between synthesized
speech and ground-truth speech using emotion2vec [22], which
serves to assess the similarity of style from an emotional per-
spective.
The objective evaluation results are presented in Table 1.
Comprehensively considering multiple metrics, the results show
that 1) Using the speech that is paired with the text for recon-
struction yields the best results. 2) Zero-shot CLAP compre-
hensively demonstrates a better performance than CA-CLAP,
further enhancing the style expressiveness of TTS. 3) Random
selection leads to the worst results, which shows that retrieval
augmentation is of great significance in TTS.
Speeches conveying the same emotion possess certain sim-
ilarities in style. Therefore, from the ESD dataset, we select
speeches where the same speaker expressed identical text with
happy and sad emotions respectively. Then embeddings are ex-
tracted from the audio encoders used in Zero-shot CLAP and
CA-CLAP to study their embedding space. We use t-SNE [23]
to visualize the results. As shown in Figure 4, the result shows
that the audio encoder’s embedding distribution of CA-CLAP
is uniform across emotions, while Zero-shot CLAP can better
distinguish between different emotions, which demonstrates the
superiority of Zero-shot CLAP in style retrieval.
3.4. Subjective evaluation
We conduct three Mean Opinion Score (MOS) subjective tests.
The Naturalness MOS (NMOS) is used to evaluate the natural-

all the comparative models, which demonstrates its powerful

3.5. Ablation Study
To study the impact of incorporating speaker embedding into
the retrieval model, we conducted an ablation experiment. We
removed the speaker encoder from Zero-shot CLAP and then
carried out training with the same settings. The results shown
in Table 1 indicate that 1) style-based retrieval after ablation is
still better than semantics-based retrieval. 2) In subjective eval-
uation, incorporating speaker information can boost the style
expressiveness of the synthesized speech.
4. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose SA-RAS, a speaker-aware style
retrieval-augmented zero-shot TTS architecture tailored for
multiple reference-speech scenarios. The proposed model is
composed of Split-TTS and Zero-shot CLAP. Split-TTS obtains
a style encoder for both TTS and the retrieval model. And the
Zero-shot CLAP is a speaker-aware style retrieval model. It
enables the optimal selection of reference speeches by effec-
tively leveraging the RAG paradigm in zero-shot TTS. Evalua-
tions conducted on the LibriTTS and ESD datasets demonstrate
that the SA-RAS model enhances the style expressiveness of the
synthesized speech.
