ArticulateX: End-to-End Monolingual Speech Translation in Articulator Space
Vishal Kumar, Vinayak Abrol
1CSE Department & Infosys Centre for AI , IIIT-Delhi, India
{vishalku,abrol}@iiitd.ac.in
Abstract
We present ArticulateX, the first non-autoregressive di-
rect speech-to-speech translation (S2ST) model that oper-
ates through an articulatory latent space, offering an effi-
cient alternative to existing cascaded models.
It consists of
a direct speech-to-articulator encoder, a latent articulator-to-
MelSpectrogram mapper, and a vocoder for high-fidelity speech
synthesis.
By leveraging articulatory representations, which
are inherently language-agnostic, our model effectively cap-
tures speech dynamics, preserving speaker identity, prosody
and expressiveness across languages. Unlike prior autoregres-
sive models, ArticulateX eliminates the need for intermediate
text, discrete units and/or complex self-supervised objectives,
enabling faster inference, stable training, and improved transla-
tion quality. We demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed model
in fr-en and de-en speech-to-speech translation on the CVSS
dataset, achieving BLEU scores better or comparable to exist-
ing models.

1. Introduction
During
the
past
decades,
speech-to-speech
translation
(S2ST) [1] has emerged as a critical tool for real-time cross-
linguistic communication. Traditional S2ST systems are based
on modular pipelines, typically involving Automatic Speech
Recognition (ASR) [2], Machine Translation (MT) [3], and
Text-to-Speech (TTS) [4]. Although effective, these systems
suffer from significant drawbacks, including latency, com-
pound errors, and reliance on language-specific intermediate
representations.
Recent advances in machine learning, such
as
sequence-to-sequence
models
[5]
and
self-supervised
learning [6], have enabled end-to-end S2ST systems that
bypass textual representations, introducing new possibilities
for efficiency and scalability. Models like Translatotron [7, 8]
and UnitY [9] have demonstrated the potential of language-
agnostic acoustic units and multilingual pre-training. However,
aligning
disparate
acoustic
features
such
as
phonology,
prosody, and rhythm remains a significant challenge for direct
speech-to-speech systems.
In this paper, we present ArticulateX, a novel non-
autoregressive framework [10] that introduces a language-
independent articulatory latent space for direct speech-to-
speech translation.
Unlike conventional acoustic or spectral
representations, our approach maps speech signals to dynamic
articulatory gesture configurations of the vocal tract that under-
lie speech production across all languages. Drawing inspiration
from articulatory phonetics and the motor theory of speech per-
ception [11], ArticulateX models speech as a universal set of ar-
ticulatory movements, abstracting away language and speaker-
specific variability. This enables more effective cross-linguistic
alignment and addresses challenges related to acoustic variabil-
ity and coarticulatory effects.
The ArticulateX framework1 employs an end-to-end design
that consists of three key components: a source audio to target
articulator encoder to map input speech into articulatory repre-
sentations, a latent articulator-to-MelSpectrogram generator to
generate target speech features, and a vocoder for high-quality
speech synthesis. Using state-of-the-art deep learning archi-
tectures, including convolutional layers, conformer blocks [12],
and transformer [13], our model captures subtle articulatory dy-
namics essential for natural synthesis while remaining robust to
speaker variability and environmental noise. Furthermore, the
non-autoregressive architecture enables parallelized inference,
significantly reducing latency compared to autoregressive mod-
els. By introducing articulatory representations as a universal
intermediary, ArticulateX provides a scalable, efficient, and ro-
bust solution for multilingual speech-to-speech translation. Ex-
perimental results demonstrate its ability to achieve high trans-
lation accuracy and speech naturalness while addressing the in-
herent limitations of existing S2ST systems2.
In summary, this work makes the following contributions.
1. We propose a novel speech-to-speech translation framework
that takes advantage of a language-agnostic articulatory space
to bridge the gap between source and target languages.
2. We present a detailed methodology for mapping acoustic sig-
nals to articulatory representations using deep neural net-
works.
3. We provide extensive experimental evaluations that highlight
the robustness of our system.
2. Related Works
Conventional S2S translation systems are implemented as a cas-
cade of three pre-trained components: automatic speech recog-
nition (ASR) for the source language, machine translation (MT)
for the conversion of the source language to the target lan-
guage, and text-to-speech synthesis (TTS) for speech genera-
tion in the target language [1].
Recent works have reduced
the dependence on multiple components by utilising learned
discrete speech representations as an intermediate representa-
tion. For instance, recently, Zhang et al. [14] jointly trained
a phoneme-conditioned VAE model for a multilingual setting,
while Lee et al. [15] used a pre-trained vocoder to directly pre-

End-to-end S2S translation systems have gained considerable

Similarly, work in [18] proposes a simple yet effective frame-

self-supervised learning (SSL) based models (e.g., wav2vec or

tems while maintaining high naturalness (MOS). Additionally,
non-autoregressive models like FastSpeech-2 [4] achieve sub-
second latency through parallel spectrogram prediction, and
other works have enhanced synthesis quality by preserving
speaker traits [20].
Existing models exhibit several limitations:
(1) robust-
ness challenges, including over-/under-generation and difficul-
ties with long sequences; (2) reliance on autoregressive archi-
tectures that yield slow inference and degrade quality when re-
placed by non-autoregressive synthesis; and (3) closed-source,
data-intensive, and expensive training processes that hinder
open-source progress.
3. ArticulateX
In this section, we introduce ArticulateX, which bypasses tex-
tual intermediaries by directly mapping acoustic signals into a
language-agnostic articulatory domain. The pipeline comprises
of three modules: (1) S2A Encoder, (2) MelSpectrogram Gen-
erator and (3) Vocoder/PostProcessing.
1. Speech-2-Articulator (S2A) Encoder: is a core innovation
in ArticulateX, mapping raw speech from the source lan-
guage to a language-independent articulatory latent space of
the target language. The S2A Encoder integrates a feature
extractor, Conformer blocks, and a sparse transformer, all
carefully optimized to balance expressive power, efficiency,
and scalability.
By operating in a universal articulatory
space, the S2A Encoder eliminates language-specific biases,
making it adept at modeling cross-lingual speech dynamics.
This ensures robust and natural translations, even for low-
resource languages. Inspired by LoRA [21], all trainable lay-
ers are reparameterized into low-rank representations (rank
4), significantly reducing computational overhead and model
size (3.1M parameters) while retaining expressiveness. This
makes ArticulateX scalable and efficient for real-world appli-
cations. Figure 2(a), 2(c), and 2(b) depict the components of
the S2A encoder.
• Feature

is followed by four standard Conv1D layers with increas-
ing kernel size & strides to progressively downsample
features, culminating in an attentive average pooling layer
that emphasizes the most informative temporal segments.
• Conformer Block: The four Conformer blocks [12] seam-
lessly enable the model to capture both local acoustic nu-
ances and global speech patterns, crucial for preserving
speaker characteristics and prosody in the translation pro-
cess.
• Sparse Transformer: is a lightweight variant of the stan-
dard encoder-decoder transformer [13] with dense layers
replaced with sparse linear layers that use a trainable soft-
max threshold. To stabilize training, a Straight-Through
Estimator (STE) is applied during backpropagation, ensur-
ing gradients remain smooth and effective while training
with sparse inputs/outputs.
Articulatory representations encode both local dependencies
(e.g., transitions between the neighbouring phonemes) and
global dynamics (e.g., rhythm, prosody, or speaker-level
characteristics). A Conformer block is explicitly designed to
handle this dual nature by combining convolutions for local
modeling with self-attention for global relationships. Fur-
ther, articulatory representations are highly sparse in nature,
meaning that only a subset of features is active at a given
time. Thus, a sparse transformer is well-suited for such data
as it can selectively attend to the most salient regions in the
feature space, avoiding overfitting on inactive or less relevant
dimensions of the articulatory representation. A large Trans-
former alone excel at capturing global patterns but lacks the
inductive bias to efficiently model fine-grained local details,
which are critical for articulatory mapping.
2. Articulator to MelSpectrogram (A2Mel) Generator: in
ArticulateX transforms sparse articulatory feature sequences
in the target language into high-fidelity MelSpectrograms. It
addresses the unique challenges of mapping sparse represen-
tations to dense spectrograms while preserving fine-grained
prosodic and acoustic details.

4.2. Model Training
Our overall pipeline is implemented in PyTorch and is
architecture-agnostic for constituent building blocks. First, the
target language text is converted into a sequence of phonemes
that are then converted to binary-encoded articulatory features.
S2A encoder is trained using a composite loss that combines
masked prediction loss for accurate sequence length estimation
and MAE loss for precise articulator feature reconstruction. For
extracting articulatory features and the A2Mel generator, we
adapted the implementation and training pipeline from the IMS
Toucan toolkit [24]. Finally, the HiFiGAN vocoder is fine-tuned
using a composite loss on the generated raw waveforms. The
first loss is the standard MelLoss from [22], while the second
one is a multi-resolution STFT loss proposed in [25].
Our model components are trained on a machine with
3× NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs (24 GB each) and 512 GB of
RAM. Model optimization is achieved with the Adam opti-
mizer, batchsize of 64 and a learning rate of 10−3 with a co-
sine annealing scheduler. The STE in sparse transformer also
uses gradient clipping. Our S2A encoder is primarily trained on
4-sec audio segments and, together with A2Mel+Vocoder Artic-
ulateX can successfully translate sequences up to 12 seconds.

5.2. Qualitative Evaluation & Ablation Study
To evaluate the naturalness and equivalency of the translated
outputs, we designed a rigorous Mean Opinion Score (MOS)
experiment involving English-speaking individuals with En-
glish as their second language. Each participant was presented
with a randomized set of translation samples to mitigate poten-
tial biases and was instructed to evaluate the outputs based on
two key aspects: 1) naturalness, which measures the human-
likeness of the translation, and 2) equivalency, which assesses
the accuracy of the meaning relative to the source text. Raters
used a standardized Likert scale ranging from 1 (poor) to 5 (ex-
cellent), with comprehensive scoring guidelines provided to en-
sure consistency across evaluations. By averaging the scores
across all samples and participants, we derived an aggregate
MOS for our system.
In order to ascertain the contribution of individual compo-
nents to our system’s overall performance, we conducted an ex-
tensive ablation study. Specifically, we explain below a few
approaches that resulted in intelligible outputs.
1. Initially, we attempted to map the articulatory representa-
tions of the source language (fr/de) to those of the target
language using classical machine learning techniques. We
explored various configurations combining Gaussian mixture
models (GMM) and support vector regression (SVR). Specif-
ically, a GMM was trained on unique articulatory represen-
tations to predict posterior probabilities for each articulator,
while a multivariate SVR was simultaneously trained on the
same data. The outputs of both models were combined via a
weighted average to obtain the final articulatory representa-
tion, which was then input to a pretrained A2Mel+Vocoder

comparative analysis clearly indicates that our S2A-based ap-
proach in ArticulateX delivers significantly higher MOS scores
for both naturalness and equivalence compared to A2A & T2A

demonstrating near real-time processing capability. These re-
sults underscore the efficacy of audio-based articulatory repre-
sentations in improving the quality and fidelity of speech syn-
thesis, and they provide a robust foundation for future develop-
ments in speech translation technologies.
6. Conclusion & Future Work
We presented ArticulateX, a non-autoregressive direct S2S
translation model that innovatively leverages a language-
independent articulatory latent space for efficient and high-
fidelity translation. With its novel lightweight S2A Encoder and
A2Mel Generator, ArticulateX achieves state-of-the-art perfor-
mance by combining sparse articulatory representations with
computationally efficient modules like Conformer blocks and
Sparse Transformers.
Future work will extend the training
dataset to include longer sequences for improved temporal mod-
eling and explore multilingual capabilities through architectural
and training paradigm enhancements, aiming to make Articula-
teX a robust, scalable solution for S2S translation.
