Abstract
Defective unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), like other malfunc-
tioning machinery, often emit abnormal sounds. Early detection
of such acoustic abnormalities enables timely countermeasures
to prevent equipment loss and human casualties. This paper
proposes a deep learning scheme to passively detect abnormal
sounds emitted from UAVs, using only a single ground-based
acoustic vector sensor (AVS). This is because lone AVS mea-
sured sound intensity spectrogram can already provide infor-
mation on both the sound source’s polar-azimuthal direction
and abnormality time-frequency signature.
This paper inte-
grates a “convolutional autoencoder” with an “attention mod-
ule”, for UAV abnormality detection. Field experiments, with
a UAV in flight and a single AVS on the ground, validate the
effectiveness of the proposed method, achieving an F1-score (a
machine-learning accuracy metric) exceeding 95% for success-
ful anomaly detection.
Index Terms: Acoustic vector sensor, sound intensity, UAV,
anomaly detection, autoencoder, deep learning
1. Introduction
Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) are compact in size, highly
maneuverable, low in cost, and easy to operate. Thus, they are
often used for applications in military reconnaissance, aerial
photography, disaster detection, and delivery services [1–6].
Such UAVs are liable to electromechanical malfunctions, lead-
ing to mission failure, loss of equipment, and collateral dam-
ages [7]. Nonetheless, abnormal sounds from the UAV often
accompany such malfunctioning. Early detection of UAV ab-
normalities can allow timely mitigation, so it is practically vital
to develop acoustic technology to detect abnormal sounds emit-
ted from UAVs.
Anomaly detection by sound/noise/vibration signals has
frequently been applied in practice due to the advantages of
non-contact measurements and long-range detection.
Unlike all existing methods that rely solely on sound pres-
sure scalar signals for anomaly detection, the proposed ap-
proach utilizes the directional information naturally embedded
in the measurements at a single acoustic vector sensor (AVS)
. This helps suppress isotropic environmental noise, highlight-
ing the characteristics of anomalous UAV sounds and improving

Specifically, this paper proposes an unsupervised method
for UAV anomaly detection based on acoustic vector signals, us-
ing a single ground-based AVS passively receives and processes
UAV noise to identify anomalies. Unlike the sound pressure
signals measured by the conventional microphones, the acous-
tic vector signals measured by a single AVS inherently carry
the information of source direction without requiring complex
microphone arrays of notable spatial dimensions.
It is pre-
cisely the directional information carried by the acoustic vec-
tor signals that enables effective suppression of isotropic envi-
ronmental noise and highlights the characteristics of anomalous
UAV sounds by directly extracting the sound intensity spectro-
gram that combines sound pressure and particle velocity phase
information, without the need to perform complex signal en-
hancement algorithms based on the explicit sound source direc-
tion.The proposed method uses an unsupervised learning strat-
egy, avoiding the need for a large amount of labeled anoma-
lous data. An encoder-decoder-encoder architecture is adopted,
which introduces the reconstruction error of the encoded fea-
tures into the loss function, making it more effective at cap-
turing anomaly patterns in the spectrogram.
Additionally, a
Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) [17] was in-
tegrated into the encoder, which dynamically assigns varying
weights to salient and less significant features during the learn-
ing process, emphasizing crucial features to improve detection
accuracy.

3.2. Anomaly detection model
3.2.1. Model structure
The proposed anomaly detection model in Figure 3 is inspired
by a generative adversarial network specifically designed to
implement anomaly detection, i.e., the GANomaly in [20].
In the proposed design, the GANomaly’s generator with the
encoder-decoder-encoder network structure is adopted, while
the GANomaly’s discriminator is abandoned, aiming to lower
the computational complexity.
Additionally, a sequence of
CBAMs [17] are incorporated in both encoders E1 and E2,
as shown in Figure 3(a), aiming to capture the global feature
through the channel and spatial attentions. The first encoder E1
maps the input feature map Fi to a latent representation zi, a
d dimensional vector. The decoder D reconstructs the feature
map ˆFi from zi. Similarly to E1, the second encoder E2 maps
ˆFi to another d dimensional vector ˆzi, which is supposed to be
an estimate of zi.
The above designed network is fundamentally a convolu-
tional autoencoder with the CBAMs, or CAE-CBAM in brief.

4.2. Hyperparameters
The proposed CAE-CBAM model adopts the Adam optimizer
[23], with an initial learning rate r = 2 × 10−4, momentum
parameters β1 = 0.1, and β2 = 0.999. The training process
is conducted over 20 epochs, employing the loss function L in
equation (4), where the weights are empirically set as ω1 = 100
and ω2 = 1 for the optimum results. Each of the encoders E1,
E2, and the decoder D consists of 5 convolutional layers.
