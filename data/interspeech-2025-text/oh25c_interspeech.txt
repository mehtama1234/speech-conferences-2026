Improving Cross-Attention based on Positional Alignment during Inference
for Robust Long-form Speech Recognition
Changhan Oh1,2, Kiyoung Park1,2, Jeomja Kang2, Woo Yong Choi2, Hwa Jeon Song2
1University of Science and Technology, South Korea
2Electronics and Telecommunications Research Institute, South Korea
{ochh508, pkyoung, jjkang, wychoi4, songhj}@etri.re.kr
Abstract
End-to-end (E2E) models have significantly advanced au-
tomatic speech recognition (ASR), with hybrid architectures
that combine Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) and
attention-based encoder-decoder (AED) models demonstrat-
ing superior performance. However, AED architectures, par-
ticularly Conformer, face notable challenges with long-form
speech, with performance degradation becoming evident for au-
dio exceeding 25 second. In this study, we propose improv-
ing the Conformer’s robustness for long-form ASR by apply-
ing Gaussian masking to the cross-attention mechanism of the
Transformer decoder during inference, using the aligned posi-
tions obtained from the CTC prefix score. The proposed method
achieves an error reduction rate (ERR) of 88.41% (from 26.41%
to 3.06%) for audio longer than 20 seconds on a LibriSpeech
evaluation set constructed by concatenating three utterances.
Moreover, the method remains effective with either a Trans-
former or an E-Branchformer encoder.

1. Introduction
With the advent of end-to-end (E2E) models, automatic speech
recognition (ASR) has seen remarkable advancements. Con-
nectionist Temporal Classification (CTC) [1] and Attention-
based encoder-decoder (AED) models [2, 3] have played piv-
otal roles in the evolution of E2E ASR systems. Subsequently,
the hybrid CTC/attention architecture [4], combining CTC and
AED models, has been proposed, demonstrating superior ASR
performance compared to previous models.
Recent work has focused on improving encoder architec-
tures, which can be seamlessly applied into hybrid structures.
Transformer, which achieved significant success in machine
translation [5], has also proven effective in ASR [6], demon-
strating an exceptional ability to capture global context through
self-attention mechanisms.
Conformer surpasses the Trans-
former by incorporating both self-attention and convolutional
modules [7], enabling the learning of both local and global
contexts, making it one of the most prominent encoder mod-
els.
E-Branchformer [8, 9] achieves state-of-the-art perfor-
mance by enhancing the merge module of the Branchformer
[10], which captures local and global context through two sep-
arate branches.
However, conventional AED models exhibit significant per-
formance degradation in long-form speech [11, 12, 13]. Espe-
cially, the Conformer with hybrid architecture has shown weak-
nesses in long-form exceeding 25 seconds [14]. Conventional
ASR models have primarily focused on short-form speech [11],
leading to the development of methods that process long-form
speech by segmenting it [15, 16].
These approaches often
require additional modules, such as a voice activity detector
(VAD). More recent research has aimed to improve the robust-
ness of Transformer-based models for long-form speech with-
out the need for segmentation by enhancing attention mecha-
nisms [17]. However, these studies typically involve modify-
ing or redesigning model architectures, making it challenging
to leverage existing pre-trained models.
In long-form speech recognition, while CTC-only models
perform well, the performance of hybrid models degrades sig-
nificantly. To address this, we propose a modification to cross-
attention that improves the robustness of AED models for long-
form speech. During inference, alignment is achieved by uti-
lizing the position corresponding to the maximum CTC pre-
fix score along the encoder output dimension. This approach
demonstrates effective across Transformer, Conformer, and E-
Branchformer encoders.
2. Related works
ASR datasets like LibriSpeech [18] primarily consist of short
utterances under 30 seconds, but real-world applications often
involve longer speech segments. To handle long-form speech,
E2E ASR models typically segment audio using methods such
as VAD or language models [15, 16, 19], or modify the in-
ference process to move the window during transcription [20].
Another approach focuses on architectural improvements, with
several studies suggesting encoder modifications [14, 21].
Ensuring monotonic alignment between speech input and
text output is crucial in ASR, leading to proposals for attention
mechanism adjustments [22, 23, 24, 25]. While these changes
are rarely evaluated on long-form audio, [11] showed that en-
forcing monotonicity in attention benefits longer speech. Addi-
tionally, Fast Conformer [17] enhanced performance by intro-
ducing limited context attention and global tokens.
In AED-based or hybrid CTC/Attention-based models,
maintaining monotonicity between the encoder output and de-
coder input is equally important, leading to proposals for
modifying cross-attention [26, 27].
These methods can
achieve monotonic alignment without being overly dependent
on the specific encoder architecture (e.g., Conformer [7], E-
Branchformer [8]).
In this paper, we build on the work of [26] by introducing
Gaussian masking to the cross-attention mechanism to achieve
monotonic alignment. However, to find the alignment position,
we do not modify the cross-attention during training; instead,
we solely leverage the argmax of the CTC prefix score along the
encoder output axis during inference. Our method significantly
enhances performance on long-form speech with only minimal
code modifications and can also be applied to pre-trained hybrid

3. Method
In this section, we briefly introduce cross-attention in AED
models and then describe methods for positional alignment.
3.1. Cross-attention
The cross-attention mechanism in the Transformer decoder, as
shown in Fig. 1, models the relationship between the encoder’s
final output and hidden text vectors. Unlike self-attention, in
cross-attention, the queries come from the decoder while the

to consider the entire encoder sequence at each time-step. Given
that Y = (y0, . . . , yi, . . . , yl) denotes the decoder hidden state
value for each layer, and X = (x0, . . . , xj, . . . , xT −1) de-
notes the final output of the encoder. The corresponding cross-

3.2. Cross-attention with positional alignment
In applications like speech recognition, the input and output
are strictly aligned in a monotonous order, meaning that only
a specific range of input is most relevant to generate the current
output token. Therefore, in ASR, it is more effective to focus
on a limited range of encoder output in cross-attention, rather
than considering the entire input sentence. In a conventional
AED structure, cross-attention can focus on any part of the in-
put. However, by limiting the range of cross-attention, ASR
performance can be improved.

3.2.2. Proposed method
In [26], Eq. 3 is required to be applied to both training and in-
ference. By contrast, our work focuses on achieving monotonic
attention by searching for alignment positions within the infer-
ence process, without relying on these training-based methods
[26, 28].
This approach enables a straightforward enhance-
ment of attention, as it does not require fine-tuning a pre-trained
model or training a modified model from scratch. Fig. 1 depicts
the inference process of the conventional hybrid model and our
proposed method.

The cross-attention weights are computed over all parts of

alignment position to increase or remain unchanged, thereby en-

score for every element in the encoder output X, a higher score

this configuration achieved the best performance. This discrep-

during training.
4. Experiments
4.1. Setup
We use the LibriSpeech corpus, which consists of approx-
imately 1,000 hours of English speech, and train Trans-
former, Conformer, and E-Branchformer models—each with
a Transformer decoder—based on the ESPnet [30] toolkit’s
egs2/librispeech/asr1 recipe. Except for the absence
of a language model, the configuration is identical to that in the
recipe; therefore, all models are trained as conventional hybrid
architectures. For the training set, we use the original utter-
ances without any concatenation. This is intended to verify that
even without additional techniques, our proposed method alone
enables a hybrid model to achieve robust performance in long-
form ASR.
Furthermore, since a publicly available implementation of
[26] does not exist, we re-implemented it using the same recipe
as our approach. The configuration is largely identical to ours,
except that σ is treated as a learnable parameter, and the method
for obtaining the aligned position k differs. Unlike [26], the
encoder is trained with a Conformer instead of a Transformer.

hibits poor recognition performance on long-form speech. To
investigate this, we evaluated recognition performance on the
Librispeech evaluation set across different utterance lengths, as

5. Conclusion
In this work, we propose a method for improving long-form
speech recognition by integrating a Gaussian mask into the
Transformer decoder’s cross-attention mechanism during infer-
ence. Specifically, we utilize the position index, where the CTC
prefix score is maximized along the encoder output index, as the
aligned position for constructing the Gaussian mask. In evaluat-
ing Conformer models with a hybrid CTC/attention architecture
and those with a CTC-only structure across different utterance
lengths, we observed notable performance degradation in the
hybrid model for utterances of 25-30 seconds or longer. Our
method maintains the performance of hybrid models on short
utterances while improving performance on longer utterances,
outperforming CTC-only models. Furthermore, our method re-
mains effective even when the hybrid model’s encoder is either
a Transformer or an E-Branchformer.
