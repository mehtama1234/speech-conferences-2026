Abstract
Recent anomalous sound detection (ASD) methods have
achieved impressive performance by leveraging attribute infor-
mation. However, domain shift and imbalance issues caused by
inconsistent attributes between source and target domains re-
main a key challenge. In this paper, we propose a novel global
and local attribute mining method for ASD (GLAM-ASD) with
a Transformer based encoder consisting of teacher and stu-
dent branches to exploit semantic-rich information. The student
mines contextual information via masked patch reconstruction
while the teacher obtains weights from the student via expo-
nential moving average. Global and local attributes are then de-
rived in an unsupervised way. The student branch is further op-
timized by global self-distillation and pseudo-label prediction
via learned local attributes. Evaluations on the DCASE2023
Task2 benchmark demonstrate the superiority of GLAM-ASD,
especially in its target domain generalization performance.
Index Terms: Anomalous Sound Detection, Self-supervised
learning, Local Attributes, Global Attributes
1. Introduction
Anomalous Sound Detection (ASD) refers to the analysis of
machine audio to determine whether the machine is operating
normally. This has recently begun to be widely applied in in-
dustrial fields. DCASE ASD challenges [1, 2, 3] mirror real-
world deployment scenarios by only providing normal sounds
for training, and requiring models to learn how to distinguish
anomalous sounds using only normal data. This task is particu-
larly challenging due to the scarcity and diversity of anomalous
sounds, as well as domain shifts caused by varying background
noise, machine types and operating conditions.
Effective modeling approaches often rely on discriminative
learning methods, such as ResNet [4, 5, 6], MobileNetV2 [7],
and STgram-MFN [8]. These methods extract labels from train-
ing meta information such as machine IDs and attributes, treat-
ing different categories as anomalies and subsequently training
models using an Outlier Exposure (OE) approach [9]. However,
there is always a trade-off between accuracy and generalization
when converting attributes into labels. When the label granular-
ity is too fine, the model tends to overfit, whereas overly coarse
labels may lead to the model overlooking differences between
distinct attributes. To address this issue, Zhang et al. [10] pro-
posed a multi-task learning framework that incorporates labels
of different granularities, while Wang et al. [11] trained inde-
pendent classifiers for each attribute, leveraging attribute diver-
sity to enhance their generalization capability.
Existing methods generally tune hyperparameters for each
machine type, which is possible where development and eval-
uation datasets share the same machine types as in past ASD
tasks [1, 2]. However, it may not be possible in some practical
deployments to collect both normal and anomalous data. De-
tection then becomes a “first-shot” problem and the challenge
is to adapt to a completely new machine type for which only
normal data is available, and still be capable of accurately de-
tecting anomalies. This is the focus of DCASE2023 Task 2 [3].
Generative methods such as autoencoder (AE)-based sys-
tems [12, 13, 14] are trained to reconstruct frame-level features
of normal audio, which do not rely on labels. These methods
operate under the assumption that anomalous audio will exhibit
notably higher reconstruction errors. Generative methods are
known for their strong generalization capabilities, which enable
them to handle first-shot problems effectively. However, they
may overgeneralize by incorporating certain anomalies that are
well-reconstructed, leading to misclassification. Zavrtanik et
al. [15] took a different approach. Highlighting the importance
of local features in anomaly detection, they distinguished be-
tween normal and anomalous samples by simulating anomalies
in local frame-level features. AnoPatch [16] was proposed to
leverage a Transformer backbone pre-trained on the extensive
AudioSet dataset and fine-tune it on task-specific machine au-
dio. It was shown that modeling from patch-level features can
effectively address the sparsity of machine audio. However,
fine-tuning pre-trained complex models still requires substantial
computational resources, which can result in inefficient infer-
ence and high memory consumption. In [4, 17], self-supervised
representation learning methods were presented, which mainly
rely on data augmentations like mixup [18] and FeatEx [4].
In this paper, we propose a novel global and local attribute
mining based ASD (GLAM-ASD) method to address the first-
shot problem, by exploiting a self-supervised method in both
clip- and frame-level embedding spaces. As shown in Fig. 1,
a Transformer based encoder consisting of student and teacher
branches is designed to learn both attributes in an unsupervised
way. Local attributes refer to specific patch-level patterns in
machine audio, which may detect anomalies with fine granu-
larity. Specifically, Mel-spectrograms are extracted from the
raw audio input, then patchified and embedded into a patch se-
quence as described in [19]. Following [20, 21], the sequence
is multi-cropped and masked, which is used as input to the stu-
dent for performing masked patch reconstruction by exploiting
the contextual information among visible patches. The teacher
is then obtained from the student via exponential moving aver-
age (EMA), from which local attributes are learned by an online
clustering algorithm on the whole input sequence.
Global attributes refer to clip-level embeddings that aggre-
gate local attribute mappings and can detect anomalies with
coarse granularity. Following multi-crop training in [20], the
global attributes from the student branch reflect various combi-
nations of local attributes, while those from the teacher branch

2. ASD Based on Global and Local
Attribute Mining
As shown in Fig. 1, the GLAM encoder consists of a teacher-
student dual-branch architecture with a codebook C ∈RK×D
to represent local attributes. Adopting a “pre-training + fine-
tuning” paradigm, GLAM-ASD is first pre-trained to learn
global and local attributes on development and additional
datasets in a self-supervised way, and then fine-tuned with at-
tributes (i.e., machine types, sections [3]) extracted from manu-
ally annotated meta information.
The pre-training stage involves global and local attribute
mining. Local attribute mining (LAM) learns semantic-rich in-
formation via masked patch reconstruction and self-supervised
learning from patch-level embeddings. Inspired by [25], the
teacher obtains weights from the student via EMA to provide
patch-level supervision, while the student is optimized by In-
foNCE [26]. Global attribute mining (GAM) learns clip-level
discriminative representations. Similar to DINO [20], multi-
cropped training is applied to the student, while the teacher is
centered on the entire input for calculating the KL-divergence.
In the fine-tuning stage, we utilize the label extracted from meta
information to perform OE [9].
The proposed system has following merits: (1) GLAM-
ASD can characterize the distribution of normal audio from
multiple granularities, i.e., patch-, clip- and category-level,
which can effectively address the first-shot problem in real-
world deployments. (2) The GLAM encoder pre-training, based
on self-supervised learning, makes it possible to utilize a large-
scale dataset, or incorporate existing pre-trained models [27, 28]
to provide additional supervisions. (3) Compared to AE-based
generative methods [22], GLAM-ASD demonstrates improved
discrimination by leveraging learned global attributes, achiev-
ing comparable or even better performance than other discrimi-
native methods [16, 22, 23].

2.1.2. Global Attributes Mining
We adopt a dual-branch structure inspired by DINO [20], where
the student branch learns from the teacher branch via knowledge
distillation, and the teacher branch is updated using the EMA of
the student branch. We apply random cropping and various data
augmentation strategies to the input audio to obtain different
views. Longer audio clips are treated as the global view Xg in
DINO, while shorter audio clips are treated as the local view
Xl. All audio clips are fed into the student branch, but only the
global view is fed into the teacher branch. During pre-training,
the global audio representations are learned by matching the
distributions between the global view and the local view.

a codebook C to obtain the local attribute distribution of the

2.2. Pre-training with global and local attributes
By combining global self-distillation learning with local at-
tribute prediction, the GLAM loss can be computed as LGLAM =
LLAP + λ1LKL + λ2LVQ, where λ1 and λ2 are hyperparameters
that control the balance between global and local contributions,
as well as the update of the codebook.
LLAP and LVQ learn discriminative features in the local fea-
ture space, creating a high-quality codebook as local attributes.
LKL utilizes the local attributes and their combinations to form
robust global attributes. By establishing connections between
local and global features in a self-supervised manner, it adap-
tively adjusts the granularity of anomaly detection between fine-
grained and coarse-grained levels. Joint optimization of these
three losses enhances feature discriminability, leading to a more
precise and robust anomaly detection performance.
2.3. Fine-tuning with attributes from meta information
In the GLAM fine-tuning stage, we only use the pre-trained
teacher encoder and the codebook, adding a linear classifier to

created from the given meta information, with each unique com-

3. Experiments
3.1. Dataset and Evaluation Metrics
We conducted experiments on the DCASE2023 Task 2
dataset [31, 32], which includes 14 machine types, with do-
main shifts reflecting different operational states of the ma-
chines. The dataset meta information indicates machine type,
machine section, and machine attributes. Each machine type
has only one machine section, and each section contains 990
samples from the source domain 10 from the target domain. In
the evaluation set, each machine section contains 200 normal
and anomalous audio samples. Audio clip durations range from
6 to 18 seconds, and are sampled at 16 kHz.
Adhering to the challenge guidelines [3], the area under the
receiver operating characteristics curve (AUC) and the partial
AUC (pAUC) is used as evaluation metrics for our system. We
report the harmonic mean of source AUC (sAUC, %), target
AUC (tAUC, %), and pAUC (%) for each machine type, along
with the official score Ω(%), which is the harmonic mean of
the scores across all machine types.
3.2. Implementation Details
During both the pre-training and fine-tuning stages, only the
Development and Additional training data from DCASE2023
Task 2 are used for training, without utilizing any external data.
We extract 128-dimensional Mel-spectrograms as input fea-
tures, obtained via a 1024-point FFT with a Hamming window
of length 1024 and a hop size of 512.
Instance normaliza-
tion [33] is applied to the input features.
During pre-training, we apply data augmentation tech-
niques including white noise injection and fade-in/out effects,
with SNRs ranging from 0 to 15 dB. The long clip Xg is set to
6 seconds, while the short clip Xl is set to 2 seconds. The model
was trained using the AdamW [34] optimizer with a batch size
of 64 over 150 epochs. The learning rate linearly increases to
1e-4 over the first 10 epochs, and then follows a cosine anneal-
ing schedule, decaying to 1e-8 for the remaining epochs. The
Transformer model contains 6 encoder blocks, with 6 attention
heads per block and a hidden size of 384. A codebook size of 64
is used, based on ablation experiments presented in Table 3. The
temperature parameters for the teacher and student branches, τt
and τs, were set to 0.1 and 0.05, respectively, and λ1 and λ2
were set to 1 and 0.1 based on initial tests.
In the fine-tuning stage, we used the AdamW optimizer
with a learning rate of 1e-4, a batch size of 64, and train over
100 epochs. ArcFace loss was used with a scale of 30 and a
margin of 0.15.
3.3. Experimental Results
As shown in Table 1, the proposed GLAM method achieves
state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance without external data. De-
spite the use of large-scale pre-trained models in methods like
AnoPatch, our approach maintains a significant lead, demon-
strating its effectiveness. Additionally, we evaluated GLAM in
an unsupervised setting, where only a pre-trained model was
used for feature extraction, followed by anomaly detection via
KNN. Results confirm that our method performs well even in
this scenario.

4. Conclusion
This paper has proposed a novel GLAM-ASD method based
on global and local attributes mining. A Transformer based en-
coder containing teacher and student branches is designed to ex-
ploit semantic-rich information from patch- and clip-level fea-
tures in an unsupervised way. The student exploits the con-
text information via masked patch reconstruction, while the
teacher is updated from the student via EMA. Local attributes
are then learned to describe specific patch-level patterns in ma-
chine audio by online clustering, which can also provide super-
vision for optimizing student through pseudo-labeling. Follow-
ing DINO, global attributes relate to clip-level patterns, which
can be mined by aggregating local attribute mappings. With
the help of multi-crop learning, we can exploit the teacher-
student architecture again to perform self-distillation without
the need for labels. A superior ASD performance of 67.06%
and 76.79% in terms of official score Ωhas been achieved by
multi-granularity attribute learning related to the patch-, clip-
and categorical-level representations respectively, demonstrat-
ing the effectiveness of GLAM-ASD to address the first-shot
problem of the DCASE2023 Task2 challenge.
