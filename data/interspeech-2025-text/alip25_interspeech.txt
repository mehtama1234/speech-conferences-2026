Abstract
With the rapid advancement of multi-channel speech enhance-
ment (MCSE) research, nonlinear spatial filtering methods in-
tegrating spatial and spectral processing are prevalent. How-
ever, many existing approaches overlook the explicit extraction
of speech spectral structure information, leading to insufficient
learning of joint spatial-spectral information, limiting perfor-
mance under low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) conditions. To
address this, we propose a three-stage multi-channel speech en-
hancement framework. The first stage has an acoustic struc-
ture extraction module capturing speech spectral patterns from
noisy inputs, enabling spatial-spectral cue exploration and in-
teraction. In the next two stages, the process combines full-
band noise reduction with speech structure refinement by de-
coupling enhancement into coarse enhancement and spectral re-
finement. Experiments on LibriSpeech-based datasets demon-
strate that the proposed method significantly outperforms the
reference method.
Index Terms: Multi-Channel Speech Enhancement, Joint In-
formation, Spatial Filtering
1. Introduction
Multi-channel speech enhancement aims to denoise speech sig-
nals more efficiently by effectively using spatial cues across
channels, enabling better separation of speech from noise [1].
This spatial information, which is not present in single-channel
methods, significantly improves the clarity and effectiveness
of speech enhancement across various acoustic environments
[2, 3].
Neural beamforming, which leverages deep learning to im-
prove traditional beamforming weight prediction, has become
popular for its efficiency and generalizability [4, 5]. Typically,
these methods follow a two-stage process: predicting time-
frequency masks to estimate statistical parameters, followed by
deriving beamforming weights from these statistics [6]. While
these approaches leverage the spatial selectivity of beamform-
ing, separating spatial processing from temporal-spectral in-
formation overlooks their inherent interdependencies. End-to-
end frame-wise neural beamforming, which processes spatial
and temporal-spectral information simultaneously, has demon-
strated superior performance [7–10]. To understand the reason
for its effectiveness, in [11], frame-by-frame beamforming is
performed using learned embeddings that encapsulate both spa-
tial and spectral discriminative information. This study sug-
gests that a comprehensive spatial-spectral representation is cru-
cial for the network to effectively distinguish between speech
and noise patterns. In [12], the authors control the informa-

tion sources available to the network, enabling it to learn com-
bined spatial and temporal-spectral information, showing that
the key to improved performance is exploiting the interdepen-
dencies between these domains, rather than the non-linear op-
erations of the filtering process. Building on this, [13] further
improves performance by introducing additional LSTM layers
to extract full-band and sub-band spectral information, enhanc-
ing the modeling of spectral structure patterns, though this in-
creases model complexity. However, unlike the above methods
for modeling spectral patterns, recent single-channel speech en-
hancement methods use a priori knowledge to model the speech
acoustic structure [14–16].
Given the critical role of spatial-spectral interaction in
MCSE performance, researchers have focused on effectively
extracting spatial information across channels [17–19]. How-
ever, in low-SNR environments, both the inherent acoustic
structure and frequency information of speech can be heav-
ily distorted by noise, limiting the network’s ability to explore
spectral information and hindering its interaction with spatial
information. Inspired by the success of modeling speech acous-
tic structures in single-channel speech enhancement [15, 16],
we propose a three-stage beamforming method with harmonic
guidance. Specifically, in the first Feature Extraction stage, we
design a Structural-Spectral Embedding (SSE) module to ex-
tract blurred harmonic structures from noisy spectrograms as
an auxiliary feature. In the subsequent Coarse Enhancement
stage, we use harmonic structures as cues to achieve prelimi-
nary noise suppression while preserving the acoustic structure.
Finally, by utilizing a residual iterative strategy, the joint infor-
mation is learned in a step-by-step and in-depth manner during
each iteration, leading to the continuous optimization of spec-
tral details. Experimental results demonstrate that our model
significantly outperforms the baseline.

3. Proposed Method
3.1. Overview
The overall diagram of the proposed framework is shown in
Fig. 1. The framework consists of Feature Extraction, Coarse
Enhancement, and Dual-Path Residual Spec-Refiner (DPRSR).
The Joint Spatial-Spectral Embedding (JSSE) in the Feature Ex-
traction module is based on the Embedding network from [11]
and, together with the SSE, produces two feature representa-
tions that capture joint spatial-spectral information and specific
acoustic structures. The following two-stage enhancement net-
work first uses these embeddings, along with the original noisy
speech, to perform coarse enhancement through a specially
designed multi-input and multi-output Masking and Refining
module. Finally, the Dual-Path Residual Spec-Refiner module,
composed of multiple cascaded refiners, takes the multi-channel
coarse enhancement results along with residuals from the previ-
ous refiners. Each refiner progressively refines spectral details
that were missed in earlier enhancement steps.
3.2. Structural-Spectral Embedding
Most MCSE models rely on the mixed speech log power spec-
trum or arrange data differently in Recurrent Neural Networks
(RNNs) to capture spectral patterns [13,20]. However, learning
these patterns directly from noisy speech via non-linear map-
ping may be insufficient, as the acoustic structures of speech
(e.g., formants and harmonic cues) are heavily masked by noise
in low-SNR environments. This degradation severely limits
the network’s capacity to discern and recover critical speech-
specific patterns, even with non-linear transformations. There-
fore, inadequately learned spectral information has difficulty
in interacting effectively with spatial information. Spatial fea-
tures such as inter-channel phase difference (IPD) and direc-
tional feature [21], with structural traits in the time-frequency
(T-F) domain, help separate speech from noise. Similarly, we
introduce the SSE module to explicitly model spectral patterns
by extracting intrinsic acoustic features, particularly the comb -
like harmonic structure.

gration [15], and DPRNN [22]. The reference channel’s noisy

Batch Normalization and PReLU. The Harmonic Integration
(HI) module computes the harmonic distribution via a comb-
pitch conversion matrix Q∈ RL×F [15] with L pitch candi-
dates:

3.3. Masking and Refining
Speech signals exhibit inherent sparsity in the frequency do-
main [23], often manifesting as periodic harmonic structures.
As mentioned in 3.2, these harmonics can serve as additional
cues to interact with spatial information. However at the same
time, they can also act as specific enhancement targets that align
with human auditory perception. Therefore, we designed a
Masking and Refining module, which performs full-band noise
reduction through masking operations while simultaneously re-
fining the acoustic structure. This allows harmonic information
and spatial information to jointly reconstruct the spectral pat-
tern of speech, ensuring the result aligns with human auditory
perception through information interaction between these two
types of information.
This module takes the embedding A and the output embed-
ding E of the JSSE, concatenates them with the noisy speech
along the channel dimension, and then reduces the channel di-
mension using a convolutional block, batch normalization, and

PReLU. The output is fed into a Self-Interaction module, which
contains two stacked multi-head Self-attention: one along the
channel dimension and the other along the frequency dimen-
sion. Self-attention is employed to model dependencies across
different dimensions. When working with 3D embeddings that
contain spatial-spectral information, Self-attention enables in-
teractions between elements in different dimensions (e.g., fre-
quency and channel), enhancing the model’s ability to capture
interdependency between spatial and spectral information. This
process is expressed as:
I = SI (BN( Conv(Concat(A, E, X)))), (5)
where SI(·), BN (·), Concat () represent self-Interaction, batch
normalization, and concatenation, respectively. The resulting
joint information I is then passed through a GRU-based mask
estimator to predict the complex ratio mask (CRM). The es-
timator is composed of a GRU module that receives narrow-
band data arrangements, with GRU shared across frequency
subbands, and fully connected layers (FC). This allows the GRU
to capture fine-grained spatial and temporal information while
focusing on global spectral statistics. The process is described
by:
M = FC(GRU(I)), (6)
Additionally, I passes through a Harmonic Integration module
that leverages the joint spatial-spectral information in I to dis-
tinguish and enhance the original acoustic structure. This mod-
ule captures fine-grained spectral information. The overall en-
hancement process is given by:
ˆSc = M∗ X + HI (I) , (7)
where HI(·) represents Harmonic Integration, ∗ denotes com-
plex multiplication, ˆSc is the result of coarse enhancement. Af-
ter the Masking and Refining process, we believe the model not
only restores specific acoustic structures but also effectively re-
moves noise from non-harmonic frequencies, even in the steer-
ing direction.
3.4. Dual-Path Residual Spec-Refiner
Decomposing the speech enhancement task into coarse en-
hancement and subsequent iterative restoration has proven to
be effective in both single-channel and multi-channel tasks
[24, 25]. However, multi-channel tasks often reduce the coarse
enhancement result to a single channel, which sacrifices the in-
tegrity of both spatial and spectral processing. To this end, we
propose a Dual-Path Residual Spec-Refiner that refines spectral
details using multi-channel results and joint spatial-spectral in-
formation.

4. Experiment Result
4.1. Dataset
We generate multi-channel noisy-clean speech pairs using the
LibriSpeech corpus [26] (train-clean-100, dev-clean, and test-
clean subsets). Noise data is sourced from the DNS-Challenge
[27], with approximately 20,000 samples selected for train-
ing. Multi-channel room impulse responses (RIRs) are sim-
ulated with a 7-microphone uniform linear array, spaced 5cm
apart. Room dimensions range from 5m-5m-3m to 10m-10m-
4m (length, width, height), with reverberation times (T60) be-
tween 0.1s and 1.0s. To generalize the model, the distance
from the target and noise sources to the microphones ranges
from 0.5m to 5.0m in 0.5m increments. The direction of ar-
rival (DOA) difference between target and noise is at least 5◦.
Training data has a SNR randomly chosen from [-6dB, 6dB].
We generate 40,000 training pairs and 4,000 validation pairs,
and the duration of each segment is 4 seconds. The test dataset
includes about 50 types of environmental noises from MU-
SAN [28], with four challenging SNR conditions (-10dB, -5dB,
-2dB, 0dB), each containing 200 test pairs, specifically chosen
to emphasize our focus on enhancement tasks in low-SNR en-
vironments.
4.2. Training Setup
All utterances are sampled at 16 kHz. A 20ms Hanning win-
dow is used with a 50% overlap between frames, and 320-point
FFT is applied. The parameters in the JSSE module are con-
sistent with those in [11], except that the number of channels
in the encoder and decoder is set to 32. The channel numbers
for the SSE and each refiner are 6, 12 and 32, 14, respectively.
The kernel sizes for the convolution in the Conv block and HI
are (5,2) and (1,3), respectively, with all convolutions having a
stride of (1,1). The HI and Self-Interaction modules employ 4,
8, and 1 heads, respectively. The GRU module is a 2-layer uni-
directional GRU with 64 hidden units. JSSE is retrained from
scratch rather than fine - tuned. Anechoic speech is used as the
reference signal to measure the SI-SDR [29], PESQ [30], and
STOI [31] of the enhanced speech.
We introduce a combination of the time-domain scale-
invariant signal-to-noise ratio (SI-SNR) and PMSQE [32]based
on psychoacoustic perception, as the overall loss function,
where the two losses are weighted equally (in a 1:1 ratio).

4.4. Ablation Study
Table 2 shows that increasing the number of refiners (N ) im-
proves all metrics. With N = 3 , SI-SDR increases by 1.75 dB
compared to no refiners, indicating effective repair of missed
spectral details. However, with N = 4, performance decreases,
suggesting that additional refiners become redundant. There-
fore, N = 3 is used for baseline comparisons. To verify
whether simply increasing the number of parameters improves
performance in low-SNR acoustic environments, we increased
the number of channels in EaBNet’s convolution and TCN mod-
ules, resulting in a parameter count similar to our model with
three refiners (EaBNet[Large]). This, however, degraded per-
formance, highlighting that more parameters do not always lead
to better results. In contrast, the advantage of our model in
low-SNR acoustic conditions stems from explicitly modeling
the acoustic information of the speech itself and integrating
multi-channel spatial information. This promotes information
interaction and fusion during end-to-end training and leverages
harmonics to guide the model to fully learn the joint spatial -
spectral information. Integrating the SSE module into EaBNet
(+SSE), which uses the extracted spectral information as addi-
tional guidance, improves PESQ and SI-SDR by 0.09 and 0.72
dB, respectively. This shows that combining prior knowledge
with implicit learning through the nonlinear mapping capabil-
ities of neural networks enhances performance with fewer pa-
rameters. Removing the SSE or JSSE modules (−SSE,−JSSE)
reduces the metrics, emphasizing the importance of spatial and
spectral information. Without the SSE, PESQ shows a signif-
icant drop, confirming the critical role of harmonic structures
in speech intelligibility. This also validates our hypothesis that
the SSE module can effectively enrich spectral information in
the joint spatial-spectral representation learned by the enhance-
ment system, especially under low-SNR conditions. PESQ, an
objective speech quality metric, evaluates spectral and temporal
features based on human auditory perception. When a neural
network learns sufficient spectral information, it can accurately
reconstruct speech signals, making enhanced speech closer to
the clean signal in frequency and harmonics. This improves nat-
uralness and intelligibility, leading to higher PESQ scores. Re-
placing JSSE with cosIPD (JSSE → cosIPD) slightly reduces
performance but outperforms EaBNet. This demonstrates the
network’s ability to integrate spatial and spectral information,
though relying solely on phase information causes some loss.
5. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose a three-stage multi-channel speech
enhancement framework addressing the challenges of spatial-
spectral information learning in low-SNR environments. The
core innovation lies in the acoustic structure extraction mod-
ule, which captures spectral patterns from noisy signals, effec-
tively integrating spatial-spectral features. Experimental results
show that modeling the acoustic structure and interacting it with
spatial information can address the information learning bottle-
neck in multi-channel speech enhancement. This improvement
is particularly significant in low-SNR scenarios, where existing
methods often struggle to maintain performance.
