Improving Generalization of End-to-End ASR through Diversity and
Independence Regularization
Ye-Eun Ko1, Mun-Hak Lee1, Dong-Hyun Kim1, Joon-Hyuk Chang1,†
1Department of Electronic Engineering, Hanyang University, Seoul, Republic of Korea
{yee772, lullaby0804, dlstjd1477, jchang}@hanyang.ac.kr
Abstract
Automatic speech recognition (ASR) has been driven by rep-
resentative end-to-end model architectures, including connec-
tionist temporal classification (CTC), attention-based encoder-
decoder (AED), and recurrent neural network transducer (RNN-
T). However, these models are prone to overfitting during train-
ing, which degrades their generalization performance. In this
paper, we propose a novel regularization technique applicable
to various ASR models: diversity loss and independence loss.
Diversity loss reduces the similarity between feature represen-
tations, encouraging the model to learn diverse patterns. Inde-
pendence loss minimizes the covariance between feature vec-
tors, ensuring that they contain independent information and re-
ducing redundancy. We apply these techniques to CTC, AED,
and RNN-T models and demonstrate that the proposed regular-
ization method effectively improves the model generalization
performance and robustness through extensive experiments.
Index Terms: speech recognition, diversity loss, independence
loss, regularization
1. Introduction
Speech recognition technology has transitioned from traditional
GMM-HMM models [1] to modern end-to-end (E2E) neu-
ral networks, including connectionist temporal classification
(CTC) [2], attention-based encoder–decoder (AED) [3], and re-
current neural network transducer (RNN-T) [4] models. These
approaches achieve outstanding performance without intricate
preprocessing, yet larger models are susceptible to overfitting
[5]. CTC assumes conditional independence and uses a blank
token for length mismatches [2], but its lack of explicit align-
ment restricts complex pattern modeling and context under-
standing [5, 6]. AED focuses attention effectively but requires
vast training data due to its large parameter space [3]. RNN-T
supports flexible alignment for online use but can over-optimize
noisy or long utterances [4]. Overfitting worsens when internal
feature representations fixate too narrowly on specific speech
patterns or acoustic conditions. Although speech signals nat-
urally contain repeated phonemes and similar acoustic events,
excessive similarity among feature vectors biases the model and
undermines generalization. Controlling this redundancy while
promoting diverse and independent features remains essential.
To address the overfitting challenge, this paper pro-
poses a diversity and independence regularization strategy
for CTC, AED, and RNN-T models. Specifically, two loss
terms—diversity loss and independence loss—moderate the co-
sine similarity and covariance among encoder feature vectors.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
• We propose an architecture agnostic regularization technique
based on diversity and independence loss, which integrates
seamlessly into various E2E ASR models to mitigate overfit-
ting.
• Our regularization method incurs negligible computational
overhead, as it solely introduces additional loss terms dur-
ing training without altering the model architecture, ensuring
efficient and scalable integration.
• We validate the effectiveness of our approach on datasets
with high background noise and complex conversational
speech, demonstrating improved model robustness and gen-
eralization.
2. Related works
Various methods have been proposed to prevent overfitting and
stabilize training. Representative techniques include dropout [7]
and batch normalization [8], which mitigate excessive reliance
on specific paths or channels by regulating parameter variance
at the neuron or channel level. Recently, approaches that reduce
redundancy among feature vectors to learn richer representa-
tions have drawn attention [9,10], alongside work on regulariza-
tion strategies aimed at increasing model diversity [11,12]. For
example, VICReg [13] integrates control of feature variance,
invariance, and covariance in self-supervised learning to guide
models toward diverse representations. Studies have also exam-
ined feature diversity and decorrelation in audio representation
learning to prevent model collapse [14]. Input-level augmen-
tations such as SpecAugment [15] and Frame-Level SpecAug-
ment [16] similarly target feature redundancy.
In CTC-based ASR architectures, several methods target
overfitting and improved training regularization [17–20]. For in-
stance, Mask CTC [21] mitigates overfitting by masking parts of
the input sequence, encouraging the model to learn diverse pat-
terns while partially addressing the conditional independence
assumption in CTC. Improved Mask-CTC [22] enhances this
approach with a conformer-based encoder and dynamic length

3. Proposed method
To improve the representational capability of speech recogni-
tion models, our regularization approach introduces two loss
terms during training—one to increase feature diversity and the
other to reduce redundancy across dimensions. This regulariza-
tion promotes the learning of more expressive representations in
feature space, enabling the model to capture a broader range of
speech patterns more effectively. The regularization term con-
sists of the diversity loss and the independence loss, which are
combined with the ASR loss to define the final objective func-
tion. We implement and evaluate our regularization scheme on
CTC, AED, and RNN-T models, as illustrated in Figure 1.
3.1. Diversity loss
Speech signals exhibit high correlations between adjacent sam-
ples, which limits the representational power of neural net-
works [26]. Diversity loss reduces similarity among feature vec-
tors within an utterance, mitigating correlation between adja-
cent samples. This encourages learned representations to cap-
ture a broader range of information and perspectives, ultimately
enhancing the model’s capacity for nuanced feature extraction.
Diversity loss is calculated based on the cosine similarity be-
tween pairs of normalized feature vectors ci and cj extracted
from the encoded feature C = {c1, . . . , cN}, where N denotes
the sequence length. Since cosine similarity can be negative, the
loss function applies its absolute value. The diversity loss Ldiv
is then defined as:

3.3. Final Loss Function
For model optimization, we employ a combined objective func-
tion that integrates diversity loss Ldiv and independence loss
Lind with the ASR loss LASR, which varies depending on the
E2E model architecture, such as the CTC loss, AED loss, or
RNN-T loss. The total loss function Ltotal is defined as follows:

4. Experiments
4.1. Datasets
We conducted experiments on the LibriSpeech [27], TEDLIUM
Release 2 (TEDLIUM2) [28], CHiME-3 [29], and Kspon-
Speech [30] datasets. The primary experiments for CTC, AED,
and RNN-T models were performed on the LibriSpeech train-
ing dataset, which contains 960 hours of English speech data.
Model evaluation was conducted using the test-clean, and test-
other subsets. For the CTC model, additional experiments
were conducted on TEDLIUM2, CHiME-3, and KsponSpeech
datasets. TEDLIUM2 consists of 207 hours of transcribed
TED talk recordings, and we utilized the dev and test sets
for evaluation. KsponSpeech provides 965 hours of Korean
conversational speech, with evaluation subsets (eval-clean and
eval-other) categorized based on perplexity. KsponSpeech is a
large-scale Korean conversational dataset that includes repeated
words and phonemes, and frequent context shifts. As a result,
it inherently exhibits high redundancy among feature vectors
within an utterance. CHiME-3 contains noisy speech recorded
in various indoor and outdoor environments, with 9,137 training
utterances and dt and et subsets provided for evaluation.
4.2. Experimental Setup
Toolikts. We trained the CTC, AED, and RNN-T models us-
ing frameworks optimized for each architecture: Fairseq [31]
for CTC, ESPnet [32] for AED, and Icefall for RNN-T.
Training details. CTC models were fine-tuned end-to-end us-
ing a pretrained wav2vec 2.0 model [33] using the CTC loss and
the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 1e-5. For AED, we
trained models with a conformer-transformer architecture, com-
prising a 12-block conformer encoder (512 output dimensions,
four attention heads, 2048 feed-forward dimensions), along
with a 6-block transformer decoder. AED model optimization
was performed using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate
of 0.0025. RNN-T models were trained using a conformer en-
coder, an LSTM decoder, and a Noam-scheduler-based opti-
mizer with a learning rate factor of 2.5 for 35 epochs. The best
checkpoints for all architectures were selected based on the low-
est validation WER.
Evaluation. Model performance was evaluated using WER,
and we additionally employed the RERR for comparisons with
existing methods. RERR is calculated by comparing the base-
line WER with the WER of the proposed method, and then aver-
aging over both development and test sets. All evaluations were
performed using viterbi, beam search, and greedy search decod-
ing, with WER measured without language models.

5. Results
5.1. Effectiveness in various E2E ASR models
Table 1 presents the WER and RERR for the test-clean and test-
other sets of the CTC, AED, and RNN-T models trained on
960 hours of LibriSpeech training dataset. Experiments were
conducted with three sets of loss weights: λdiv = 1.0, λind =
1.0; λdiv = 0.1, λind = 1.0; and λdiv = 1.0, λind = 0.1.
Across all of the E2E model architectures, our proposed di-
versity–independence regularization method led to significant
WER reductions, with the highest RERR of 4.96% observed
in the RNN-T model. CTC and AED models also showed sub-
stantial improvements, achieving RERR values between 2.13%
and 3.80%, demonstrating the model-agnostic nature of our
approach. Furthermore, performance imporvements were ob-
served even with a low weight of 0.1 for either λdiv or λinv,
indicating that these regularization terms enhance model perfor-
mance without requiring large weight values. The effectiveness
of our approach stems from maximizing entropy of individual
data samples, which broadens the representation space and dis-
perses representation vectors, as illustrated in Figure 2.

5.3. Comparison with other regularization methods
We evaluated our method against existing regularization tech-
niques, including Mask-CTC [21] and Improved Mask-CTC
[22], using the CTC model on TEDLIUM2. As shown in Ta-
ble 4, our approach achieved the highest RERR of 2.76%, sur-
passing Mask-CTC (0.80%) and Improved Mask-CTC (1.26%),
confirming its superior effectiveness. To ensure result reliability,
we conducted three independent runs and reported 95% confi-
dence intervals in Table 4, providing a statistically robust com-
parison. These intervals account for variability across runs, con-
firming that the improvements are not due to chance but repre-
sent a meaningful advantage. Moreover, this study is the first to
introduce and compare regularization techniques for stabilizing
E2E ASR models, laying the groundwork for future research on
improving generalization and robustness.
5.4. Ablation studies
Ablation studies on the TEDLIUM2 dataset assessed the in-
dividual and combined effects of diversity and independence
regularization. Table 5 compares the baseline wav2vec 2.0
CTC model with models applying each regularization method
separately and together. Applying independence regularization
alone, with λdiv=0.1 and λind=1.0, resulted in an RERR of
1.08%, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving model gen-
eralization. Similarly, diversity regularization alone led to an
RERR of 1.66%, highlighting its role in enhancing feature rep-
resentations. When both regularization techniques were applied
together, the model achieved the best performance, with an
RERR of 2.76%. These results indicate that both techniques im-
prove performance independently while offering complemen-
tary and orthogonal benefits when combined.
6. Conclusion
In this paper, we proposed a novel regularization approach
to improve E2E ASR models by introducing feature diversity
and independence losses. Our method enables the model to
learn richer and more expressive features, ultimately enhancing
recognition accuracy. The proposed regularization effectively
mitigates overfitting and significantly improves generalization,
contributing to robustness across diverse speech conditions.
