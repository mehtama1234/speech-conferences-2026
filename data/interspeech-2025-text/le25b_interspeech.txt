Multistage Universal Speech Enhancement System for URGENT Challenge
Xiaohuai Le1, Zhuangqi Chen1, Siyu Sun1, Xianjun Xia1, Chuanzeng Huang1
1ByteDance, China,
(lexiaohuai.123, chenzhuangqi.audio, sunsiyu.319, xiaxianjun,
huangchuanzeng)@bytedance.com
Abstract
During audio transmission and processing, various distor-
tions may occur.
To effectively address this challenge, we
developed a multistage universal speech enhancement system,
consisting of four submodules, namely audio declipping, packet
loss compensation, audio separation, and spectral inpainting.
These modules operate across the time, sub-band, and time-
frequency domains.
We employed a pretrain-finetune train-
ing paradigm and introduce a self-distillation method to fur-
ther improve performance. Experiments on large-scale datasets
demonstrate that our system outperforms in multiple evalua-
tion metrics, particularly in improving subjective speech qual-
ity. The proposed system ranked 1st in the URGENT 2024 chal-
lenge with a MOS of 3.52 and placed 4th in the second track of
the URGENT 2025 challenge.

Universal Speech Enhancement, Multistage,

1. Introduction
In audio encoding, transmission, and processing, various dis-
tortions such as noise, signal amplitude clipping, codec arti-
facts, and spectral loss are commonly encountered. These dis-
tortions significantly impair speech perceptual quality and lead
to performance degradation in downstream tasks, such as au-
tomatic speech recognition. Recent speech enhancement sys-
tems designed for a single task have achieved remarkable suc-
cess, producing significant results in specific scenarios, includ-
ing speech enhancement [1, 2], spectral band extension [3, 4],
and packet loss compensation (PLC) [5]. Some models employ
multistage [6, 7] or end-to-end [8] approaches to simultaneously
address multiple of the aforementioned distortions. Universal
speech enhancement [9], which aims to handle all potential dis-
turbances, has gained increasing attention in recent research.
To extend speech enhancement capabilities from single to
universal scenarios, it is essential to address significant varia-
tions in distortions across multiple scales and domains. Early
works attempted to integrate speech enhancement with derever-
beration [10], as these tasks can be equivalently formulated as
additive noise removal through spectral mask estimation. Un-
like additive noise, spectral band loss is more challenging, as
it requires spectrogram estimation, which is difficult to learn.
Unified approaches such as TF-GridNet [2] or Apollo [3] have
been proposed to simultaneously handle noise and spectral dis-
tortions. Discontinuities in the time domain are another criti-
cal factor that contributes to perceptual degradation, commonly
manifested as audio clipping, interruptions, and packet loss.
Conventional time-frequency models struggle to accurately lo-
calize distortions at signal sampling points due to their limited
spectral resolution, making time domain [11] and subband do-
main [5] models more widely used. Multistage models [7] pro-
vide a robust solution by combining the advantages of differ-
ent models or domains to effectively handle various distortions.
These models have been extensively studied in the context of
speech enhancement and restoration [6, 12], demonstrating their
compatibility with both noise reduction and spectrogram exten-
sion. Generative models, such as diffusion models [13, 14] and
generative adversarial networks (GANs) [15], have also been
introduced to the field of universal speech enhancement. These
approaches typically treat the correction of various distortions
as a unified generative task, conditioned on the distorted speech
feature. In recent studies, how semantic information embedded
in speech can facilitate audio restoration has been investigated
[16, 17]. Using pre-trained self-supervised embeddings, audio
restoration models can produce more intelligible speech, even
under severe distortion or packet loss conditions [18].
Although previous competitions such as the Deep Noise
Suppression (DNS) Challenge [19] have substantially increased
attention and research on the speech enhancement task, there is
a dearth of relevant datasets and appropriate evaluation metrics.
Researchers often focus on closed-domain datasets from spe-
cific subfields that lack comprehensive cross-domain validation.
Furthermore, there has been no well-recognized benchmark to
assess the performance of these models across the wide variety
of speech quality distortions encountered in real-world scenar-
ios. The URGENT Challenge1 provides such a platform, en-
couraging researchers to develop universally robust speech en-
hancement systems. More importantly, the organizers offer 14
evaluation metrics in four main categories, evaluating objective
signal quality, subjective human perception, and downstream
task performance.
This paper presents our universal speech enhancement sys-
tem submitted to the URGENT Challenge.
The system ad-
dresses various types of distortion through a combination of
four submodules, which respectively handle clipping, packet
loss, additive noises, and spectrogram distortion. To optimize
each submodule for optimal performance, we adopted a training
paradigm in which the submodules are individually pretrained,
followed by fine-tuning of the entire system. During testing, we
also introduced a routing strategy by detecting the existence of
distortions, where an additional detector controls the activation
of the Declipping and PLC modules. Our system achieved the
1st place in the URGENT Challenge 2024, with a mean opin-
ion score (MOS) of 3.52, significantly higher than other teams.
Additionally, we ranked 4th in Track-2 of URGENT Challenge
2025. These results demonstrate the outstanding performance
and effectiveness of our system in addressing complex audio
distortions.

2. System
2.1. Problem formulations and Methodology
Let s ∈RT represent the clean speech signal and the degraded
speech x can be written as
x = d1 ◦d2 ◦...dN(s) ∈RT
(1)
where ◦stands for the composition of the function and N is the
number of distortions. The distortions in real scenarios can be
mainly classified into the following categories.
Clipping: dclip(s) = max(min(s, δ1), δ2)), where δ1 and δ2
are the lower and upper bounds of the signal that limit the am-
plitude. In this paper, we only consider hard clipping and do not
consider the problem of sample discontinuity.
Packet loss: dP L(s) = s ⊙m, where m ∈RT denotes the
mask of packet loss in the time domain. The value of m(k) is 0
or 1, which indicates whether packet loss occurred in packet k.
Additive noise and reverberation: dnoise(s) = s ∗h + n,
where h and n respectively denote the room impulse response
(RIR) and the noise signal. In this paper, clean speech with
early reverberation is used as the noise suppression target.
Low bandwidth: dlowb(s) = Resample(s ∗hlp, sro, srt),
where hlp is a low-pass filter and Resample(·, sro, srt) refers
to the resampling operation that converts the original sample
rate sro to the target srt.
Spectral artifacts: dspec(s) = F(s), where F(·) denotes the
process of a specific audio codec or front-end algorithm.
During the training process, we do not assume a fixed dis-
tortion order for data construction. Instead, during enhance-
ment, we prioritize handling time-domain distortions, such as
clipping, followed by frequency-domain distortions. This ap-
proach facilitates the decoupling of the effects from different
domains, thereby reducing the optimization difficulties. Spec-
trum inpainting is placed at the final stage to mitigate the ad-
ditional artifacts introduced by the preceding stages. Based on
this methodology, we construct a multistage audio enhancement
system consisting of submodules, denoted Ei. The enhanced
speech e ∈RT is represented as
e = E1 ◦E2 ◦...EM(x)
(2)
2.2. System overview
As shown in Figure 1, our system addresses distortions by uti-
lizing a combination of four submodules: a time-domain de-
clipping module, a time-frequency domain speech separation
module, a subband-domain PLC model, and a time-frequency
domain inpainting module. These modules are cascaded and
can be independently optimized.
All submodules follow an
encoder-decoder architecture. The structure of the declipping
module is depicted in Figure 2-(a). We adopted the Demucs
architecture [20], which consists of 12 layers of symmetric
convolutional encoders and decoders, along with a bottleneck
layer comprising 3 BLSTMs. The PLC module is based on
BS-PLCNET [5], the champion system of the PLC Challenge
2024 [21]. This model operates in the pseudo quadrature mir-
ror filter bank (PQMF) domain and employs multi-resolution
adversarial training for learning. Given that additive noise is
the most common type of distortion, we employ the state-of-
the-art audio separation framework BSRoformer [22] for de-
noising and dereveraberation. As shown in Figure 2-(b), this
model separates speech by dividing the spectrum into 62 non-
overlapping frequency bands, which are processed through 16

Rope-Transformer layers [23] to capture the dependencies be-
tween the bands and the frames.
The speech spectral in-
painting module, similar to the declipping model, employs
a 2-dimensional convolutional recurrent network in the time-
frequency domain, in which the bottleneck BLSTM is replaced
by DPBLSTM [24]. To process audio with arbitrary sample
rates, both the declipping and the separation module are trained
on a diverse range of sample rates, spanning from 8 kHz to
48 kHz. Specifically, the sample rate is provided as an addi-
tional input to the BSRoformer following the band-split mod-
ule, enabling it to determine the appropriate number of fre-
quency bands for subsequent processing. For the PLC and in-
painting modules, all the input clips are resampled to 48 kHz
and the outputs are then resampled back to the original sample
rate.
To further reduce the inference complexity, we trained a
time-domain detector which can assess the occurrence of clip-
ping and packet loss, and then decide whether to activate the de-
clipping and PLC module accordingly. By using Temporal Con-
volutional Networks (TCN), the detector effectively identifies
clipping and packet loss at the sample level. The model is com-
posed of 7 convolutional layers followed by a linear layer for
probability prediction. The predictions generated by the model
not only determine whether the module should be activated but
also act as supplementary input features to boost model perfor-
mance.

high-quality enhanced samples serve as additional supervision

our experiments, the selected samples are mixed with ideal la-

3. Results
3.1. Parameter setups and Metrics
In our experiments, we utilized the full dataset provided by
the URGENT Challenge, generating nearly 2 TB of FLAC-
format audio for training. The four modules have parameter
sizes of 6 M, 125 M, 3.8 M and 4.6 M, with a total computa-
tional cost of 350 GMACs for 48 kHz input. The model was
trained on 8 A100-80GB GPUs, totaling 44 GPU days. The
learning rate was set to 5e-4 for pre-training and 1e-5 for fine-
tuning, with a decay rate of 0.95, both optimized with Adam.
Self-distillation was applied for 20 epochs.
For evaluation,
we selected 8 key metrics from the original challenge-provided
evaluation metrcs, covering non-intrusive speech quality met-
rics (DNSMOS [26] and NISQA [27]), intrusive metrics (PESQ
[28], ESTOI [29], SDR [30] and LSD [31]), downstream task
performance (SpeechBERT Score [32] and WAcc) and subjec-
tive evaluation (MOS).
3.2. Experimental Results
We developed multiple systems and evaluated them on the UR-
GENT Challenge 2024 non-blind test set. As shown in Table 1,
audio after declipping and separation module already achieved
good DNSMOS and SDR scores, but the objective metrics were
suboptimal due to spectral loss. Notably, the inpainting mod-
ule significantly improved these metrics, demonstrating the ad-
vantage of multi-stage processing in integrating the strengths
of different modules. Furthermore, with the addition of self-
distillation, most metrics showed improvement, particularly the
non-intrusive ones, while a few, such as SDR and WAcc, exhib-
ited slight degradation. This suggests that the approach effec-
tively balances trade-offs among different metrics. A compari-
son with other top-tier systems of URGENT Challenge 2024, as
presented in Table 1 and 2, highlights the superiority of our sys-
tem in non-intrusive metrics and perceptual quality. Ultimately,
our system ranked first across five metrics, notably achieving
a MOS of 3.52, significantly outperforming other teams. This
contributed to our top ranking on the blind test set. However,
since our approach did not primarily focus on downstream tasks
during training and fine-tuning, our performance on those met-
rics was relatively lower.
The results of our system with an additional PLC mod-
ule on the URGENT 2025 blind test set are presented in Ta-
ble 3. Although the system achieved robust performance on
non-intrusive metrics, severe packet loss in some samples posed
challenges beyond the current capabilities of our modules, re-
sulting in a decline in objective scores. This is likely attributable
to the absence of a PLC module specifically designed for the
competition task. We also evaluated the activation strategy of
the detector. In our experiments, the declipping and PLC mod-
ules are selectively disabled when the clipped sample ratio falls

Moreover, we visualized the Pearson correlation matrix for
all metrics across our 38 submissions in Figure 4. A positive
correlation is observed among the four main metric categories.
Notably, non-intrusive metrics exhibit relatively lower correla-

For
downstream tasks, SpeechBERTScore and ESTOI show strong
correlations, while WAcc demonstrates limited correlation with
other metrics and can be considered as an independent refer-
ence. These insights can serve as valuable guidelines for speech
quality evaluation in future work.
4. Conclusion
In summary, we propose a multistage cross-domain model
for the universal speech enhancement task, trained within a
pretrain-finetune framework. By optimizing both objective and
subjective evaluation metrics, our system achieves top-tier per-
formance and ranks 1st in the URGENT Challenge 2024 and
4th in the URGENT Challenge 2025. In the future, we aim
to improve generalization and computational efficiency of the
model and leveraging more generative modules to further boost
performance, especially under conditions of severe distortions.
