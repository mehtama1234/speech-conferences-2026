ReSepNet: A Uniﬁed-Light Model for Recursive Speech Separation with
Unknown Speaker Count
Hadi Alizadeh, Rahil Mahdian Toroghi, Hassan Zareian
1Faculty of Technology and Media Engineering, Iran Broadcasting University (IRIBU), Tehran, Iran
alizadeh.hadi08@gmail.com, mahdian.t.r@gmail.com, zareian@iribu.ac.ir
Abstract
Single-channel speech separation remains a signiﬁcant chal-
lenge, particularly when the number of concurrent speakers
is unknown. Existing methods often rely on prior assump-
tions about speaker count, limiting their real-world applicabil-
ity. This paper introduces ReSepNet (Recursive Separation Net-
work), a novel, uniﬁed model for speaker-independent speech
separation that dynamically adapts to an unknown number of
speakers. ReSepNet employs a recursive separation architec-
ture, enabling it to iteratively isolate individual voices without
prior knowledge of the speaker count. To enhance efﬁciency
and reduce model complexity, we introduce a novel objective
function and workﬂow. We demonstrate the effectiveness of Re-
SepNet on the WSJ0 datasets, achieving state-of-the-art separa-
tion performance and accurate speaker count estimation. Fur-
thermore, ReSepNet generalizes well to mixtures containing
four and ﬁve speakers, showcasing its robustness and adaptabil-
ity to challenging scenarios.
Index Terms: Speech separation, recursive, unknown speaker
count, improved transformer, single channel separation
1. Introduction
Speech separation, a core challenge in audio processing, aims
to isolate individual speech sources from their mixtures. Appli-
cations span speech recognition, voice assistants, and speaker
veriﬁcation. Traditional methods like Independent Component
Analysis (ICA)[1] and Independent Vector Analysis (IV A)[2]
excel in overdetermined scenarios, while underdetermined mix-
tures often rely on clustering techniques such as those in [3] and
Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM) [4]. Deep Neural Networks
(DNNs) have revolutionized underdetermined speech separa-
tion, with models like Conv-TasNet-gLN [5] achieving state-
of-the-art results. However, most existing methods necessitate
prior knowledge of the number of speakers in the mixture, a
crucial piece of information for model input.
Existing approaches for underdetermined speech separation
with unknown speaker counts often exhibit limitations. For in-
stance, [6] employs a Count-Head block to estimate the speaker
count and then utilizes a dedicated decoder for the estimated
number, restricting the maximum separable speaker count. Al-
ternatively, [7] trains separate models for each possible speaker
count and selects the model with the highest output channel ac-
tivity. This approach also limits the maximum separable count
and necessitates a complex multi-model architecture.
Novel approaches in [8, 9] overcome the limitation of train-
ing separate models for each speaker count by iteratively sep-
arating one source at a time. However, these methods are con-
strained to scenarios with equal or fewer speakers than those
encountered during training and operate in the time-frequency

domain. In contrast, recursive approaches in [10, 11] exhibit
greater ﬂexibility, enabling separation of mixtures with more
speakers than those seen during training. [10] employs itera-
tive separation, while [11] reﬁnes the initial separation by re-
separating the extracted sources from the original mixture. De-
spite their potential, these methods suffer from signiﬁcant draw-
backs. The approaches in [8, 9] encounter a heavy model due to
the utilization of AlexNet [12] for iteration termination, leading
to increased computational complexity. Furthermore, the em-
ployment of CNNs in [10] can result in limited receptive ﬁelds,
while the use of RNNs in [11] necessitates information prop-
agation through numerous intermediate states [13], potentially
hindering performance.
ReSepNet, our proposed framework, employs a recursive
architecture. At each iteration, it generates two outputs: the
separated speech of one speaker and the residual signal con-
taining the remaining speakers. This residual signal then serves
as input for the subsequent iteration. To effectively terminate
the separation process, we introduce an Iteration Authorization
Block (Fig. 1). To enhance separation quality and minimize
model complexity, we incorporate several key innovations:
1. Transformer-based Architecture: We leverage the strengths
of improved Transformer architectures [13, 14], enabling di-
rect context-aware modeling of speech sequences and over-
coming the limitations of RNNs and CNNs in capturing long-
range dependencies.
2. Dual-Path structure: To effectively handle long input se-
quences, we adopt a Dual-Path structure [15], enabling more
efﬁcient analysis of extended audio segments.
3. Counting method: We introduce a novel source counting
mechanism that effectively overcomes the limitations of ex-
isting methods, such as delays and excessive parameter re-
quirements, by leveraging the model’s output characteristics.

2. SYSTEM OVERVIEW
ReSepNet operates in the time domain and comprises four key
stages: an Encoder, a Mask Estimation module, a Decoder, and
an Iteration Authorization Block (Fig. 1). The separation pro-
cess proceeds iteratively. In each iteration, the Encoder gen-
erates an embedding of the input mixture. Subsequently, the
Mask Estimation module produces separation masks that iso-
late the target speaker’s contribution. The Decoder then recon-
structs the time-domain signal corresponding to the separated
speaker. Finally, the Iteration Authorization Block evaluates
the residual signal to determine if additional speakers remain.
The separation process continues iteratively until the residual
signal primarily consists of noise, indicating the completion of
the separation process.

2.2. Mask Estimation
As illustrated in Fig. 2, the Mask Estimation module generates

3. Iteration Authorization Block
The Iteration Authorization Block determines whether the
residual signal necessitates further processing to extract addi-
tional speakers. This block enables the model to be iteratively
reused by treating the residual signal as a new input mixture,
provided it exhibits characteristics indicative of residual speech.
The block’s design is informed by an analysis of the model’s
output behavior. For a mixture of C speakers, the model should
ideally execute C iterations. As illustrated in Fig. 2 for a two-
speaker mixture, the residual signal in the ﬁnal iteration exhibits
a high degree of noise, while the extracted speech signal closely
resembles the original input. These observations guide the de-
sign of the Iteration Authorization Block, which incorporates
two key components:
• Cross correlation between the mixed input and the separated
source audio.
• Cross correlation between the mixed input and the residual
signal.
The Iteration Authorization Block produces a binary output. If
the absolute value of any of the calculated cross-correlations
exceeds or equals 0.93, the block signals the end of the iteration
process by outputting 1. Otherwise, it outputs 0, indicating that
further iterations are required.

sources, ˆrk(t), is consistently trained to appear in the second
output channel. This constraint ensures that no trace of the ex-
tracted speaker’s speech is present in the residual signal, effec-
tively enforcing a separation constraint. By explicitly attending
to the separated speaker in one output channel and the resid-
ual signal in the other, the model’s separation performance is
signiﬁcantly enhanced, as demonstrated in [10].
5. Model Training
Model training was conducted using the WSJ0 dataset [20], fol-
lowing the methodologies outlined in [21, 10]. Audio record-
ings from distinct speakers were randomly mixed with Signal-
to-Noise Ratios (SNRs) ranging from -2.5 dB to 2.5 dB, result-
ing in the creation of the WSJ0-2mix and WSJ0-3mix datasets
for two- and three-speaker scenarios, respectively. ReSepNet
was exclusively trained on a combined dataset of WSJ0-2mix
and WSJ0-3mix, setting the maximum number of speakers dur-
ing training CM to 3. To reduce computational demands, audio
samples were downsampled to 8 kHz. For training, the audio
data was segmented into 4-second windows. Segments shorter
than 2 seconds were discarded, while shorter segments were
zero-padded to achieve a consistent 4-second duration. To eval-
uate the model’s generalization capabilities, additional WSJ0-
4mix and WSJ0-5mix datasets were created for four- and ﬁve-
speaker test scenarios.

6. Experimental Results
The model was trained on a server with a high-end NVIDIA
GeForce RTX 3090 GPU with 24 GBytes RAM, and ev-
ery epoch of training required approximately 6.2 hours. Re-
markably, despite being trained exclusively on WSJ0-2mix
and WSJ0-3mix datasets, ReSepNet successfully generalizes
to separate challenging WSJ0-4mix and WSJ0-5mix mixtures,
demonstrating its robustness and adaptability. This achievement
is particularly noteworthy considering the signiﬁcantly reduced
model complexity compared to existing state-of-the-art meth-
ods. Separation samples are available on our GitHub repository
for independent evaluation and analysis1. Furthermore, ReSep-
Net surpasses the performance of models designed for a known
number of speakers, such as DPTNet [13] and SepFormer [17],
which utilize the Dual-path method without the dynamic mixing
capability of our approach.
To rigorously assess the accuracy of the Iteration Autho-
rization Block, we conducted extensive evaluations on a dedi-
cated test set comprising 3000 samples for each of the 1, 2, and
3 speaker conditions, drawn from the WSJ0 test sets, WSJ0-
2mix, and WSJ0-3mix, respectively. A successful classiﬁcation
is deﬁned as the block accurately outputting zeros for all but the
ﬁnal iteration, where a 1 is expected. Our proposed approach
achieves an impressive accuracy of 96.6%, surpassing the per-
formance of existing recursive models [10, 11] which exhibit an
accuracy of 95.7%. Notably, unlike these models that rely on
computationally expensive AlexNet for iteration control and re-
quire 10-second input segments of mel spectrograms, our novel
Iteration Authorization Block solely leverages cross-correlation
analysis, eliminating the need for feature extraction and impos-
ing no constraints on input segment length.

7. Conclusion
This paper presented ReSepNet, a novel recursive speech
separation framework that dynamically adapts to mixtures
with an unknown number of speakers. By integrating a
transformer-based architecture with a recursive separation
workﬂow and a lightweight Iteration Authorization Block,
ReSepNet eliminates the need for prior assumptions about
speaker counts while achieving state-of-the-art performance.
The key innovations including a dual-path transformer structure
for efﬁcient long-context modeling, a permutation-invariant
training objective that reduces computational complexity, and
a cross-correlation-based iteration termination mechanism
achieves 96.6% accuracy in speaker count estimation. Ex-
perimental results on WSJ0-2mix and WSJ0-3mix datasets
demonstrate ReSepNet’s superiority over existing methods,
with SI-SNRi improvements. This work opens new directions
for real-world speech separation systems, particularly in
scenarios with unpredictable speaker counts. Future research
will explore extending ReSepNet to real-time applications and
integrating speaker diarization for fully end-to-end solutions.
