Abstract
Existing anomalous sound detection (ASD) methods generally
rely on the learned patterns of normal sounds to identify de-
viations as anomalies in generative and discriminative ways.
However, partly due to the diverse and sparse distribution of
anomalies, they may suffer from poor generalization capability
to distinguish unseen normal sounds and anomalies. In this pa-
per, we propose to finetune the large pre-trained model based
on frequency-wise multi-query attention pooling (FMQAP) for
ASD. Specifically, FMQAP first dynamically fuses multiple
output embeddings from the pre-trained model, and then ex-
ploits a novel frequency-wise attention pooling scheme to im-
prove the generalization capability for various machine types.
Evaluations on DCASE 2023 Task2 benchmark demonstrate the
superiority of FMQAP, achieving the state-of-the-art 77.62% in
terms of official score.
Index Terms: anomalous sound detection, pre-trained models,
frequency-wise, attention pooling
1. Introduction
Anomalous sound detection (ASD) aims to automatically iden-
tify anomalous states through audio signal analysis during ma-
chine operation [1,2]. Compared with conventional monitoring
approaches, ASD enables non-contact, cost-effective, and effi-
cient intelligent monitoring, with broad application potential in
industrial manufacturing domains.
Due to the scarcity and diversity of anomalies, existing
ASD methods typically train the model to characterize the dis-
tribution of normal audio, and samples deviate from it are iden-
tified as anomalies [3,4]. For example, autoencoders (AE) [5,6]
are used to model the distribution of normal samples in an
unsupervised way. However, due to the strong generalization
ability of generative based methods, the performance is gener-
ally far from satisfactory. Discriminative based methods [7–10]
utilize labels from meta annotation to train the convolutional
neural networks (CNN). From the perspective of outlier expo-
sure (OE) [11], they treat the the normal data from other cate-
gories as outliers to learn a discriminative and compact distri-
bution.
Extensions have been proposed by exploiting the time
and frequency domain information. Guan et.al [12] proposed
the Time Weighted Frequency Domain Representation with
GMM (TWFR-GMM) ASD method, which can adapt to differ-
ent machine types. Chen et.al. [13] proposed to use statics ex-
change (StEx) on time or frequency axis of input spectrogram to
construct the pseudo anomalies. In [14], high-pass filtering was
applied to the audio of certain machine. Li et al. [15] designed
filterbanks with varying distribution densities across different
frequencies for each machine type in the MIMII dataset [16].
These studies indicate that different machines exhibit distinct
frequency patterns, emphasizing the significance of specific fre-
quencies and selecting appropriate frequency features for ASD.
Despite the promising performance of the discriminative
based ASD methods, they heavily rely on the labels extracted
from meta information, which may not be able to address the
first-shot problem in DCASE 2023 Challenge Task 2 [2]. The
challenge is to train model for a complete new machine, where
machine types in the development and evaluation datasets are
completely different. This may incur several issues that signif-
icantly degrade the ASD performance. 1) The model learned
from the normal data in training set can hardly discriminate
whether the deviations are from normal data of new machine
types or form anomalies. 2) The domain shifts between source
and target domains of development dataset caused by various
factors, including different machine’s operation conditions and
environment, differ from those of evaluation. This issue can-
not be resolved solely by using additional training dataset or
data augmentation techniques. It is a dilemma to balance the
precision and recall metrics that are related to the miss- and
false-alarm in detection systems.
Several works have been proposed for first-shot prob-
lem [15, 17, 18].
For example, Han et al. [17] suggested
to leverage self-supervised pre-trained models on large large-
scale speech data.
By fusing the output of different layers
and data augmentation to simulate different machine operat-
ing sets. Jiang et al. [18] finetuned the pre-trained models on
AudioSet [19] to adapt to various conditions of machine au-
dio. Leveraging large pre-trained models enables the acquisi-
tion of common knowledge about speech and audio from ex-
tensive training data, effectively mitigating the issue of data
scarcity [18].
However, existing methods commonly focus on reduce the
inconsistency of the pre-trained model and inductive bias of
machine audio. BEATs and ATST-frame use average-pooling
to aggregate the patch-level features into a segment-level repre-
sentation [20,21]. For ASD, Anopatch [18] and Conformer [11]
employ attentive statistics pooling (ASP) [22]. The simple aver-
aging or weighted summing aggregation scheme disregards the
time-frequency characteristics in audio signal, which has been
demonstrated to be effective for ASD tasks [6,12,15,23].
In this paper we propose a novel frequency-wise multi-
query attention pooling (FMQAP) method to address the first-
shot problem by exploiting the pre-trained model, as shown in
Fig.1. The key idea is to exploit information extracted from
multiple transformer layers, frequency sub-bands to improve
the robustness and effectiveness of first-shot problem in ASD.
Specifically, the pre-trained model on AudioSet is used as fea-
ture encoder to extract the patch-level features. Following [17],
a layer-wise weighted average pooling (L-WAP) is designed to

quency patterns. As shown in [12–15], selecting appropriate
frequency sub-band may be beneficial for ASD, especially in

3. Experiments and Results
3.1. Datasets
We evaluate our method on the DCASE 2020 Task 2 and
DCASE 2023 Task 2 datasets. The DCASE 2020 dataset com-
prises parts of ToyADMOS [33] and the MIMII [16] dataset,
which contains 6 machine types with multiple machine IDs per
type. Each ID includes approximately 1,000 normal audio clips
in the training set and 400 mixed normal/anomalous clips in
the evaluation set. The DCASE 2023 dataset comprises parts
of ToyADMOS2 [34] and the MIMII DG [35] dataset, which
extends the challenge with domain shift scenarios across 14
machine types. Each machine type contains a single section
composed of 990 source-domain samples and 10 target-domain
samples. The evaluation set provides 200 test samples per ma-
chine section, reflecting diverse operational states through com-
binations of machine type, section, and attributes.
3.2. Evaluation Metrics
Following DCASE challenge protocols [1, 2], we adopt the
area under the receiver operating characteristics curve (AUC)
and partial AUC (pAUC) with a low false positive rate restric-
tion (FPR) range [0, 0.1] as metrics.

3.3. Implementation Details
Audio inputs are resampled to 16 kHz and processed into 10-
second clips.
Mel spectrograms with 128 frequency bands
are extracted using 25 ms windows and a hop size of 10 ms.
These spectrograms are then split into patches with patch-size
of 16 × 16. The BEATsiter3 backbone [20] with 12 transformer
layers (768-dim hidden states) is used as the feature extractor.
Optimization is performed using the AdamW optimizer [36],
with a batch size of 64, an peak learning rate of 1 × 10−4, and
a total of 100 training epochs are trained. The learning rate
is warmed up for the first 10 epochs, linearly increasing to its
target value, and then decayed following a cosine schedule to
5 × 10−8 over the remaining epochs. The ArcFace loss param-
eters are set with margin 0.1 and scale 30. In FMQAP, N=24
learnable queries are used , followed by K = 1 KNN-based
anomaly scoring.
In the baseline ASD system, Cross-Entropy (CE) loss is
used together with average pooling, without L-WAP or any data
augmentation. All the other settings are kept unchanged.
3.4. Results
Tables 1 and 2 present our experimental results on the DCASE
2020 and DCASE 2023 datasets, respectively. We compare our
method with previous SOTA methods, including those with pre-
trained large model.
On DCASE 2020 development and evaluation datasets, our
method achieves an average score of 91.23% and 95.34%, re-
spectively, surpassing both our baseline system and previous
SOTA approaches. On the DCASE 2023 development and eval-
uation datasets, our method obtains official scores of 68.19%
and 77.62%, significantly outperforming our baseline system
and prior SOTA methods. These results demonstrate the effec-
tiveness of our proposed approach.

4. Conclusion
This paper has proposed a novel FMQAP method to finetune
the pre-trained large audio model, i.e, BEATs for addressing
the first-shot problem in ASD tasks. FMQAP explicitly utilized
the cross-band contextual information by multi-query attention
pooling to improve the generalization capability for various ma-
chine types. The information extracted from different layers of
pre-trained model is further exploited with L-WAP. Extensive
evaluations have been conducted on DCASE 2020 and DCASE
2023 Task2 benchmarks. The results of 95.34% and 77.62% of
mean score and official score have achieved, outperforming the
previous STOA with a significant gap, demonstrating the effec-
tiveness of the proposed FMQAP.
