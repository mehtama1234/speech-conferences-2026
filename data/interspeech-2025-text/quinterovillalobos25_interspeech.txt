Rollback Speech: Smart Feedback Prompts for Lost Utterances in Unstable
Online Calls
Yuni Amaloa Quintero Villalobos1, Wafaa Wardah1,2, Sebastian M¨oller1,2, Robert P. Spang1
1Quality and Usability Lab, Technische Universit¨at Berlin, Germany
2German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence, Berlin, Germany
quintero.villalobos@campus.tu-berlin.de, wafaa.wardah@tu-berlin.de,
sebastian.moeller@tu-berlin.de, spang@tu-berlin.de
Abstract
We present a prototype system designed to enhance the user ex-
perience of online conferencing in the presence of poor network
conditions. When connection issues cause parts of a speaker’s
audio to be lost, the system uses dual automatic speech recog-
nition (ASR) outputs—one local and one remote—to detect
which utterances were not received. Upon reconnection, the
speaker is provided with extracted keywords representing the
information that did not reach the listener, enabling efficient
and targeted repetition without full redundancy.
This helps
maintain conversational flow and reduces frustration during vir-
tual communication. The system comprises real-time transcrip-
tion, network degradation simulation, discrepancy detection,
and lightweight keyword extraction in a modular architecture.
Our “Show and Tell” demonstration allows participants to ex-
perience how the system augments communication continuity,
even under conditions of packet loss or delayed transmission.
Index Terms: speech recognition, human-computer interac-
tion, computational paralinguistics
1. Introduction & General Description
Despite the convenience of online call software, these tools still
suffer from persistent usability issues, particularly in the face of
unstable network conditions. One particularly frustrating sce-
nario arises when audio is cut off due to packet loss, fluctuating
bandwidth, or spotty Wi-Fi and mobile reception [1, 2]. These
disruptions can interrupt the natural flow of conversation and di-
minish comprehension—especially when speakers must repeat
themselves without knowing exactly what the listener missed.
This lack of feedback can lead to confusion, wasted effort, and
a general ”sense of disconnect” among participants [3].
To address this problem, we developed an early-stage pro-
totype that intelligently enhances online call experiences by de-
tecting when speech has been lost due to connection failures.
Our system leverages Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) on
both sides of a call to monitor what was said and what was re-
ceived in real time. When audio fails to transmit during a con-
nection drop, the system identifies the discrepancy and gener-
ates a set of keywords to summarize the missing portion. These
are shown to the speaker upon reconnection, enabling them to
efficiently reiterate only the relevant points, rather than restating
everything.
This approach fills a unique gap in the current landscape
of conferencing tools.
Solutions such as MeetDot [4] or
translation-enhanced captioning systems [5] prioritize accessi-
bility and comprehension but generally assume reliable, unin-
terrupted links.
Similarly, captioning systems for education
[6], and speech processing techniques for noisy environments
or overlapping speech [7], are not designed to handle ad-hoc
transmission losses. Even comprehension enhancement strate-
gies using artificial delays during collaboration [8] fall short of
tackling the intermittent, real-time packet loss typical of home
connections.
Our prototype targets cases where network disruptions
cause speech loss. By comparing ASR transcripts from both
speaker and listener clients, the system identifies missed utter-
ances and prompts the speaker to rephrase only the lost content.
This preserves conversational flow and reduces redundancy dur-
ing critical exchanges like meetings or presentations. In our
demo, a simulated two-client call includes a brief disconnec-
tion. Upon reconnection, the speaker sees keyword prompts
summarizing what was not received—demonstrating a practi-
cal, user-focused solution for restoring continuity in unstable
network conditions.
2. Theoretical Background
Conventional online conferencing systems often fail to handle
transient network outages gracefully, leading to unintelligible
speech or lost message segments. This creates significant com-
munication bottlenecks, particularly when speakers must repeat
entire portions of their message due to a short-lived connection
loss. Our goal is to improve the subjective user experience in
such situations by identifying and recovering only the most rel-
evant speech fragments missed during disconnection.
The key objective of our system is to detect periods of net-
work instability, compare what the speaker said versus what
was received on the other end, and extract semantically mean-
ingful keywords from the untransmitted segment. This allows
the speaker to selectively reiterate only the missing informa-
tion, maintaining conversational context and reducing frustra-
tion. We implemented this using a modular pipeline:
• Speech Capture and Transcription: Both client and server
components use automatic speech recognition (ASR) to tran-
scribe speech in real time.
• Network Simulation: To evaluate robustness, an artificial
network degradation module simulates bandwidth drops and
introduces packet loss.
• Mismatch Detection: Upon network recovery, the transcrip-
tions from sender and receiver are compared to identify dis-
crepancies.
• Keyword Extraction:
From the unreceived segment, a
lightweight keyword extraction algorithm identifies and dis-
plays essential terms back to the speaker to prompt summari-
sation.
Compared to existing approaches, which primarily focus on
either live captioning or improving bandwidth efficiency, our
solution is unique in that it handles the aftermath of speech loss

3. Detailed Workflow
To demonstrate the feasibility of our approach, we implemented
a web-based prototype that mimics real-world online commu-
nication scenarios under unstable network conditions. The sys-
tem enables one-way or two-way speech between participants,
leveraging local transcription and network-aware logic to detect
and handle disruptions.
3.1. System Architecture
The system consists of two primary client nodes—representing
two users in a call—and a central server component that man-
ages network simulation and message comparison. Below is a
conceptual overview of the core workflow (see also Figure 1):
1. Speech Capture: The local client records and transcribes
the speaker’s utterances using a real-time automatic speech
recognition (ASR) engine.
2. Audio Transmission: Audio data is transmitted to the receiv-
ing client via a WebSocket connection.
3. Network Simulation: Controlled packet loss and latency are
introduced to simulate poor connectivity using an artificial
degradation module.
4. Discrepancy Detection:
When a disconnection occurs,
transmitted and received transcripts are logged.
Once the
connection is restored, the system compares the complete lo-
cal transcription with the (incomplete) received transcription.
5. Keyword Extraction: From the untransmitted portion, cru-
cial keywords are extracted using a term frequency-based
method and displayed to the speaker as feedback.
6. Speaker Prompt: The speaker sees the extracted keywords
and is prompted to selectively rephrase the relevant part with-
out repeating everything. This facilitates smoother commu-
nication recovery.
3.2. Implementation Details
Hardware and Environment: The current prototype operates
on consumer-grade laptops as an interface, and uses a GPU
server for the ASR via a network connection. The interface it-
self requires a standard web browser and provides a connection
to its peer via WebRTC and WebSocket protocols. Automatic
Speech Recognition: The demonstrated system uses the Whis-
per ASR system on our own servers for privacy and latency rea-
sons [10]. Keyword Algorithm: A simple Python NLTK based
keyword extraction approach was implemented to surface the
most relevant words in the missing sequence. This way, the
speaker can be provided with meaningful keywords while not
having to read an entire sentence. Software Stack: The fron-
tend system is implemented in Vue.JS and Node.JS. The back-
end is written in python. Apart from the ASR component, the
software used was created entirely for research purposes.
During the Show and Tell demonstration, two people will
speak via the system across a table. A brief disconnection is
initiated to simulate degraded connection. Upon reconnection,
the visitor will see a feedback panel showing extracted key-
words—showcasing how important speech elements can be pre-
served despite connectivity issues. This workflow offers a new
user-centric approach to sustaining context in digital conversa-
tions.
