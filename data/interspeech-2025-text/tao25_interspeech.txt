Abstract
The identification of direct-path recordings for direction-of-
arrival (DOA) estimation has been recognized as a crucial factor
in enhancing the localization robustness under reverberant en-
vironments, especially for the challenging cases involving mul-
tiple acoustic sources.
In particular, the recently introduced
spatial feature denoted relative harmonic coefficients (RHC)
has been extensively investigated for DOA estimation due to
a strong association with spatial cues. Building upon this, this
paper presents a multi-source localization method that utilizes
higher-order RHC vector to detect direct-path dominant (DPD)
bins in the time-frequency (TF) domain. Specifically, DPD bins
are effectively identified by applying an orthogonal projection
matrix (OPM) to the RHC vector. Moreover, we integrate the
OPM with a closed-form single source DOA estimator, thus
circumventing the exhaustive search over a dense spatial grid.
Extensive experimental evaluations using both simulated and
real-world recordings confirm the proposed algorithm’s effec-
tiveness, and superiority over baseline methods.
Index Terms: direction-of-arrival estimation, multi-source, re-
verberation, spherical microphone arrays
1. Introduction
Acoustic source direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation contin-
ues to be a prominent topic in the audio signal processing com-
munity, with the goal of recovering the directional informa-
tion of source(s). Accurate knowledge of source positions con-
tributes to a range of practical audio-only applications, such
as robot navigation, cochlear implants and intelligent surveil-
lance systems [1]. Furthermore, numerous acoustic signal pro-
cessing techniques, including but not limited to dereverberation
[2], beamforming [3], and spatial acoustic rendering [4], mostly
require the prior knowledge of DOA to enhance their perfor-
mance or achieve the intended objectives.
Recently, spherical microphone arrays (SMA) have gained
increasing popularity for DOA estimation due to several unique
advantages: (i) providing comprehensive spatial cues over a
two-dimensional (2-D) directional space; and (ii) decompos-
ing into the spherical harmonics (SH) domain, enabling the de-
coupling of frequency- and angular-dependent components [5].
This facilitates significant advancements in source localization
algorithms with such arrays, including beamforming [6], sub-
space [7, 8], pseudo-intensity vector (PIV) [9, 10], and relative
harmonic coefficient (RHC)-based approaches [11, 12]. How-
ever, in practical acoustic environments, acoustic reflections due
to reverberation pose a major challenge as they severely distort
the propagation of direct-path recordings, thus significantly de-
grades localization performance in multi-source scenarios. One
of the most efficient solution is to detect the direct-path dom-
inant (DPD) components prior to source localization, which
leverages the sparsity of speech signals in the time-frequency
(TF) domain. Consequently, considerable efforts have been de-
voted to developing reliable DPD bin detectors, to enhancing
the robustness of acoustic localization approaches against re-
verberations [13, 14, 15].
One early method for detecting DPD components checks
whether the rank of the correlation matrix is unity with a coher-
ence test [16]. Later, Nadiri et al. proposed identifying DPD
bins by comparing the ratio of the largest- and second eigenval-
ues of spatial covariance matrix, where frequency smoothing
techniques are applied to de-correlate the mixed signals [13]. In
another study [17], sound field directivity was utilized to guide
the extraction of DPD bins, achieving significant performance
improvements. Instead of focusing on bin-level detection, the
authors in [18] identified DPD TF zones by calculating the cor-
relation coefficient of magnitude between any two sensors. Re-
cently, Hu et al. introduced a promising SH-domain feature,
known as the RHC, for DOA estimation, which serves as the
SH-domain counterpart to the relative transfer function (RTF).
Two key properties of RHC are summarized as follows: (i) it
is independent of the source signal, and (ii) it depends solely
on the source’s DOA. Both advantages have facilitated the de-
velopment of 2-D grid search [19], decoupled [20], and closed-
form multi-source DOA estimation algorithms [21]. However,
grid-search based approaches suffer from high computational
complexity, while the closed-form method not fully utilizes the
higher-order SH coefficients. As a result, existing RHC-based
methods remain suboptimal in practice.
Motivated by the advantages of RHC, this paper proposes
a novel direct-path components detection method by introduc-
ing an orthogonal projection matrix (OPM) to the higher-order
RHC vector. It is shown that the l2 norm of the vector, obtained
by applying an properly designed OPM to direct-path RHC, be-
come zero. This unique property can effectively guide the iden-
tification of DPD bin in the TF domain. To improve computa-
tional efficiency, the establishment of OPM is skillfully associ-
ated with a closed-form single-source DOA estimator, eliminat-
ing the need for tedious grid search procedures.

4. Experiments
This section evaluates the proposed algorithm using both the
simulated and real-world recordings. Moreover, we present the
comparison results with four baseline approaches, i.e., WDO
(using all STFT bins within the given frequency band), Closed-
form [21], Decoupled [20], and DPD-MUSIC methods [13].
Localization accuracy is measured by the commonly used met-
ric of mean absolute estimated error (MAEE).
4.1. Simulated Environments
We simulate a rectangular room with dimensions of 6m×4m×
3m using the image source method [29]. An open spherical ar-
ray equipped with 32 capsules is positioned at the center of the
room to capture the interior sound field. All source signals are
assumed to be static and placed 1m away from the array origin.
Dry speech signals are randomly selected from the TIMIT cor-
pus at a sampling rate of 16 kHz [30] and are convolved with
the room impulse response (RIR). Gaussian white noise with an
SNR level of 15 dB is added to each channel to generate noisy
measurements, which are then segmented into 2-second signal
blocks for DOA estimation. The noisy signals are transformed
into the frequency domain using the STFT with a Hamming
window of 512 samples, a 50% overlap between frames, and
a 2048-sample fast Fourier transform (FFT). For DOA estima-
tion, a limited frequency range of [2600, 3600] Hz is utilized,
as SH coefficients can be estimated up to the 3rd order avoiding
the ”Bessel-zero” problem [31].
Before evaluating the localization accuracy, we first exam-
ine the capability of the proposed DPD bin detection method
by simulating three plane waves propagating from (48◦, 112◦),
(89◦, 76◦) and (217◦, 142◦), and they are polluted by 15 dB

the proportion of valid bins decreases for both methods as the
reverberation level increases. Compared to the WDO method,
however, the proposed approach significantly increases the pro-

of our DPD component detection.
Table 1 presents the MAEE results of the five methods un-
der varying reverberation levels in both two- and three-source
scenarios, whereby each result is averaged over fifty repeated
experiments. As expected, the performance of all approaches
degrade by the increase of T60, primarily due to the diminish-
ing of direct-path components. Specifically, the WDO method
performs the worst due to not using DPD bin detection, thus the
smearing effect caused by reverberation severely affects its lo-
calization accuracy. By contrast, the other four methods localize
the sources more robustly. The Closed-form method, limited to
the first-order SH coefficients, exhibits a lower DPD bin detec-
tion capability. The Decoupled method only uses the magnitude
information in higher-order RHC vector, which improves its
performance but still renders it suboptimal. The DPD-MUSIC
method is hard to distinguish the DPD and non-DPD bins under
highly reverberation conditions. It is noted that our approach
outperforms the reference methods in most scenarios due to re-
liable and accurate identification of DPD bins. This highlights
the strength of the proposed method in enhancing localization
accuracy under adverse acoustic environments.
4.2. Real-world Environments
We finally evaluate the proposed algorithm using real-world
measurements recorded with a commercial Eigenmike [32] in
a reverberant chamber. The room dimensions are [7, 6, 4]m,
with T60 ≈350 ms and background noise originating from
outdoor activity. Three loudspeakers are positioned 1m away
from the array at approximate directions of Φ1 = (229◦, 98◦),
Φ2 = (314◦, 104◦) and Φ3 = (59◦, 92◦), respectively. All
other experimental settings are consistent with those used in
the simulations above, and five repetitive trials are conducted
for credible results. The MAEE results of five methods (Pro-
posed, DPD-MUSIC, Decoupled, Closed-form and WDO) are
{3.17◦, 3.45◦, 3.35◦, 3.66◦, 6.4◦}. We note that the localiza-
tion accuracy is lower than the simulated results due to unavoid-
able errors in real acoustic scenarios, our method still outper-
forms the others, which again validate the effectiveness of the
proposed algorithm in practical scenarios.
5. Conclusions
In this paper, we address the challenging problem of multi-
source DOA estimation in reverberant environments using a
spherical array.
We propose to apply an OPM to the RHC
vector for identifying direct-path components in the STFT do-
main. Additionally, the determination of OPM is associated
with a closed-form DOA estimator, thereby avoiding the tedious
search on a spatial grid. Both the simulation and experimental
results validate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm.
