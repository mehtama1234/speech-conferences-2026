Abstract
We present the LiRI Corpus Platform (LCP), a web-based in-
frastructure for storing, exploring, and analyzing diverse lin-
guistic corpora with a focus on multimodal and audiovisual
data.
The platform supports synchronized querying of text,
speech, and video through unified interfaces and a custom
query language (Descriptive Query Definition - DQD). Dedi-
cated frontends enable time-aligned exploration of gesture, au-
dio, and spoken transcripts. LCP is designed to support re-
searchers working with multimodal and annotated datasets, en-
abling cross-modal queries and layered annotation.1
Index Terms: corpus platform, multimodal corpora, audiovi-
sual corpora, information retrieval, query language
1. Introduction
Multimodal corpora combining text, speech, and video are in-
creasingly central to research in linguistics, interaction analy-
sis, and digital humanities. However, tools that allow integrated
access to these data types remain limited. Most existing plat-
forms are either text-centric or restricted to audiovisual annota-
tion without query support [1, 2, 3, 4].
Researchers often rely on a patchwork of tools to manage
multimodal data, making workflows fragmented and inefficient.
Some solutions, such as ANNIS [5] or CQPweb [6], provide
powerful textual querying but lack integrated audiovisual sup-
port. Others like VIAN [7] focus on rich video annotation but
do not offer full corpus query capabilities.
The LiRI Corpus Platform (LCP) addresses this gap by of-
fering an integrated solution for corpus storage, exploration,
and analysis across modalities, developed at the University of
Zurich as part of the LiRI (Linguistic Research Infrastructure)
[8]. LCP is the only platform including web-based querying,
data upload, and multi-modal analysis. It supports corpus-based
studies in linguistics, sociolinguistics, interaction research, me-
dia and communication studies, and the digital humanities, but
it is also useful for broader fields such as human-computer in-
teraction, behavioral science, etc.
LCP builds on previous experience in managing multilin-
gual, richly annotated corpora and aims to provide a user-
friendly, extensible solution for both academic and applied re-
search, emphasizing flexibility, transparency, and interoperabil-
ity [9, 10].
2. System Architecture
LCP features a modular, service-oriented architecture centered
around a PostgreSQL backend. Data is structured using a flexi-

multimodal alignments, and nested annotations without struc-

language, Descriptive Query Definition (DQD), which supports
entity pattern matching, logical filtering, and result aggregation
(e.g., frequency lists, collocation tables).

3. Query Language: Descriptive Query
Definition (DQD
The DQD language provides a declarative formalism to query
heterogeneous corpus data in LCP (Fig.1, left). Designed for
flexibility and expressiveness, DQD queries operate over the
user-defined corpus template (Fig.1, right) and support multi-
level, cross-modal search operations. Users can define entities,
sequences, filters, and constraints using a readable syntax in-
spired by natural language descriptions.
Crucially, DQD supports querying across modalities and
annotation layers. For example, users can search for spoken
utterances by specific speakers that co-occur with gesture an-
notations in a given time window, or retrieve all noun phrases

Queries can return three main result types:
• Plain: concordance-style results with optional context.
• Analysis: frequency or distributional statistics.
• Collocation: statistical co-occurrence results within a span.
The language supports logical operators, quantifiers, nega-
tion, and optional constraints on temporal alignment, speaker
identity, or metadata fields [8]. The result interface highlights
matching spans and supports download and export for further
analysis.
4. Specialized Interfaces
Three frontends offer tailored access to different modalities:
• Catchphrase — text corpora: supports linguistic querying,
collocation analysis, and metadata filtering.
• SoundScript — speech corpora: displays speaker segments,
aligned transcripts, and audio waveforms with time-based
search.
• VideoScope — audiovisual corpora: enables querying and
browsing of layered video annotations such as gestures, head
nods, and facial expressions (Fig. 2).
Figure 2: VideoScope interface with the timeline showing the
multimodal CANDOR corpus[12]
All frontends offer visual navigation, interactive result ex-
ploration, and export options. DQD queries are validated au-
tomatically based on the corpus template, and executed with
incremental sampling to ensure fast response times on large
datasets.
5. Demonstration and Use Cases
We will demonstrate several corpora live in the LCP interface:
• CANDOR corpus, a richly annotated multimodal corpus of
interactions [12], showing synchrony between visual infor-
mation and speech in dyadic conversations in VideoScope.
• OFROM corpus[13], a spoken corpus of Swiss French
speakers, illustrating how SoundScript supports spoken lan-
guage analysis.
We will show how users import their own data, define tem-
plates, run complex multimodal queries, and export results.
6. Conclusion
The LiRI Corpus Platform provides an end-to-end solution
for working with multimodal corpora, combining flexible data
modeling, expressive querying, and modality-aware user inter-
faces. It supports advanced research in speech, interaction, and
audiovisual communication by bridging the gap between anno-
tation tools and scalable analysis. The platform is open to col-
laboration and is used in several interdisciplinary projects at the
University of Zurich and beyond.
